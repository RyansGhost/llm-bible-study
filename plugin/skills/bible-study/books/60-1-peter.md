# 1 Peter

## Summary

1 Peter is a letter of hope to Christians suffering for their faith. Peter calls them "elect exiles" (1:1), and Barcley shows those two words sum up the letter. As God's elect, they are chosen, born again to a living hope, and made a royal priesthood and holy nation. As exiles, they live in a world that isn't their home, so they will face slander and suffering. Peter calls them to be holy, to live honourably among unbelievers, to submit to authorities for the Lord's sake, and to follow Christ's example of suffering without retaliating. Christ suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, and glory will follow suffering for them as it did for him.

## Setting

- **When:** between AD 60 and 68: Peter seems to know Ephesians and Colossians, and tradition says he was crucified, upside down, in Rome under Nero by AD 68 (Reformation Study Bible). Probably about AD 62–64 (the Lutheran Study Bible says before AD 67).
- **Where:** from "Babylon" (5:13), most likely a code name for Rome, where Mark had also been with Paul (Col 4:10), to churches in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia, in what is now Turkey (1:1).
- **Who wrote it:** the apostle Peter (1:1), with Silvanus (Silas) helping (5:12), which may explain its polished Greek. Barcley answers the modern arguments against Peter's authorship from the style and the kind of persecution described. Irenaeus, Tertullian, Clement of Alexandria and Origen all name Peter; Galilee was bilingual, so a fisherman in trade could know Greek; and the letter echoes Jesus' words to Peter (5:2–3; John 21:15–17) (RSB).
- **Where it sits:** one of the General Epistles. Barcley argues the readers were mostly Gentiles (1:14, 18; 2:10; 4:3), though Peter applies Israel's titles to them; Calvin thought them Jewish Christians. The persecution was mainly social: slander, insults and mistreatment for being Christians, not yet empire-wide.

## Structure

Barcley notes that 1 Peter is hard to outline because suffering keeps recurring and Peter mixes commands with teaching throughout. He organises it around "elect exiles", and the Reformation Study Bible's outline runs similarly:

- 1:1–2: Greeting to the elect exiles
- 1:3–12: The Christian's sure salvation: born again to a living hope, foretold by the prophets
- 1:13–3:12: What salvation means for life: be holy; love one another; God's chosen people; honourable conduct among unbelievers; submission in society, work and marriage
- 3:13–5:11: Christian suffering and service: suffering for doing good, Christ's example, the end of all things, the fiery trial, elders and flock, standing firm
- 5:12–14: Final greetings

## Chapter by chapter

- **1 Peter 1:** Blessed be God, who has caused us to be born again to a living hope through Jesus' resurrection, to an inheritance kept in heaven. Trials test the genuineness of faith like fire tests gold. The prophets searched to know about this salvation. "Be holy, for I am holy" (1:16). You were ransomed not with silver or gold but with the precious blood of Christ, a lamb without blemish. You have been born again through the living and abiding word of God.
- **1 Peter 2:** Long for the pure spiritual milk. Come to Christ, the living stone rejected by men but chosen by God, and be built up as a spiritual house. "You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession" (2:9). As sojourners and exiles, keep your conduct honourable. Be subject to every human institution, honour the emperor. Servants should submit even to unjust masters, following Christ, who suffered without threatening. "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree … by his wounds you have been healed" (2:24).
- **1 Peter 3:** Wives may win unbelieving husbands by their conduct, with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit. Husbands must live with their wives in an understanding way, honouring them as co-heirs of grace. Don't repay evil for evil. Always be prepared to give a defence for the hope that is in you, with gentleness and respect (3:15). Christ suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God. He proclaimed to the spirits in prison. Baptism, which corresponds to the flood, now saves you through the resurrection of Jesus Christ.
- **1 Peter 4:** Arm yourselves with Christ's way of thinking: you no longer live for human passions. The end of all things is at hand, so be sober, pray, love one another earnestly, show hospitality, and use your gifts to serve. Don't be surprised at the fiery trial. Rejoice that you share Christ's sufferings. If you suffer as a Christian, don't be ashamed. Judgment begins at the household of God.
- **1 Peter 5:** Peter, a fellow elder and witness of Christ's sufferings, urges the elders to shepherd God's flock willingly, as examples, and the chief Shepherd will give them the crown of glory. Clothe yourselves with humility. Cast all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you (5:7). Your adversary the devil prowls like a roaring lion: resist him, firm in your faith. After you have suffered a little while, the God of all grace will restore you. Greetings from "she who is at Babylon" and from Mark.

## Key people

- **Peter:** the apostle, a fellow elder, and witness of Christ's sufferings (5:1).
- **The elect exiles:** believers scattered across five regions of Asia Minor (1:1).
- **Silvanus (Silas):** a faithful brother who helped write the letter (5:12).
- **Mark:** whom Peter calls "my son" (5:13).
- **Sarah:** a model of holy womanhood who obeyed Abraham (3:6).
- **Noah:** saved through water with his family, eight people in all (3:20).
- **The elders:** shepherds of God's flock under the chief Shepherd (5:1–4).

## Key events

- The resurrection of Jesus, the ground of our living hope (1:3; 3:21)
- Christ's suffering and death for sins (2:21–24; 3:18)
- The flood in Noah's day (3:20)
- The readers' trials and slander for the name of Christ (1:6; 4:12–16)

## Themes

- **Elect exiles:** chosen by God and at home in his people, but strangers in the world (1:1; 2:9–11). Barcley highlights how Peter applies Israel's exile language to the church.
- **Living hope:** an imperishable inheritance through the resurrection (1:3–9). Christ was revealed "in these last times" (1:20), but the full salvation is still to be revealed at his coming (1:5, 13).
- **Suffering, then glory:** following the pattern of Christ (1:11; 4:13; 5:10). Barcley shows how Peter explicitly links the believers' suffering with Christ's.
- **Holiness:** be holy as God is holy (1:15–16). Barcley notes "holy" is used eight times.
- **The people of God:** a spiritual house, royal priesthood and holy nation (2:4–10).
- **Witness:** good conduct and a ready answer for the hope within (2:12; 3:15–16).
- **Submission:** to authorities, masters and husbands, for the Lord's sake (2:13–3:7).
- **Humility and trust:** cast your anxieties on God, and resist the devil (5:5–9).
- **Grace in Peter's own story:** the apostle who once rebuked Jesus for speaking of suffering, and denied him when it became dangerous, now teaches the necessity of Christ's suffering and ours (Matt 16:22; 26:69–75).

## How it points to Christ

Peter, an eyewitness of Christ's sufferings (5:1), shows Jesus as the fulfilment of the prophets, who "predicted the sufferings of Christ and the subsequent glories" (1:11). He is the spotless lamb whose blood ransomed us (1:19). He is the living stone and cornerstone of Psalm 118 and Isaiah 28 (2:4–8). Peter's description of Christ's suffering in 2:22–25 is drawn from Isaiah 53: he committed no sin, bore our sins in his body on the tree, and by his wounds we are healed. He suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God (3:18). He is now at God's right hand, with angels and powers subject to him (3:22), and he is the chief Shepherd who will appear in glory (5:4).

## Common mix-ups

- "Cast all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you" is 1 Peter 5:7.
- "Always be prepared to give a defence for the hope that is in you" is 1 Peter 3:15, the classic verse for apologetics.
- "Babylon" (5:13) is almost certainly Rome, not the city in Mesopotamia.
- "Spirits in prison" (3:19) is one of the hardest passages in the New Testament. Barcley surveys the views. Many Reformed interpreters, following Augustine, read it as Christ preaching through Noah to people now in prison. Others take it as the risen Christ, on his way to God's right hand (3:22), announcing his victory over the fallen spirits. Lutherans read it as Christ's descent into hell after his death to proclaim his victory (Formula of Concord IX). It doesn't teach a second chance after death.
- "Baptism now saves you" (3:21). Lutherans read this as baptism working salvation (Lutheran Study Bible). The Reformed confessions read it as baptism being the sign and seal of what saves, "not as a removal of dirt from the body but as an appeal to God for a good conscience, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ" (WCF 28).
- "A royal priesthood" (2:9) doesn't abolish church office: Peter still addresses elders (5:1–4).

## Going deeper

- **Elect exiles (1:1; 2:9–12).** How should a Christian be both settled and a stranger in the world? Compare Jeremiah 29 and Barcley's discussion.
- **Suffering for doing good (2:18–25; 3:13–18).** What is the difference between suffering for Christ and suffering for wrongdoing? How does Christ's example shape our response to unjust treatment?
- **Israel's titles for the church (2:9–10).** What does it mean that Peter applies Exodus 19:6 and Hosea 2 to a mostly Gentile church?
- **Spirits in prison and baptism (3:18–22).** Weigh the main views of 3:19. How do Reformed and Lutheran readings of 3:21 differ, and what do WCF 28 and the Small Catechism say?
- **Witness (3:15).** What does it look like to give a reasoned answer for our hope with gentleness and respect?
- **Shepherds (5:1–4).** What kind of elders does Peter want? How does Christ as chief Shepherd shape their work?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** How does Peter address his readers in 1:1?

   **A:** As elect exiles of the dispersion.

2. **Q:** To what has God caused us to be born again?

   **A:** A living hope, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ (1:3).

3. **Q:** With what were believers ransomed?

   **A:** The precious blood of Christ, like a lamb without blemish (1:18–19).

4. **Q:** What command from Leviticus does Peter quote?

   **A:** "You shall be holy, for I am holy" (1:16; Lev 11:44).

5. **Q:** What four titles does 2:9 give the church?

   **A:** A chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession.

6. **Q:** What did Christ do with our sins, according to 2:24?

   **A:** He bore them in his body on the tree.

7. **Q:** What should Christians always be prepared to do?

   **A:** Give a defence for the hope that is in them, with gentleness and respect (3:15).

8. **Q:** How does Peter describe Christ's death in 3:18?

   **A:** He suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God.

9. **Q:** What should we not be surprised at?

   **A:** The fiery trial (4:12).

10. **Q:** To what does Peter compare the devil?

    **A:** A roaring lion, seeking someone to devour (5:8).
