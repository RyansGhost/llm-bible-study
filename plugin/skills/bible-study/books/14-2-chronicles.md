# 2 Chronicles

## Summary

2 Chronicles tells the story of the kings of Judah from Solomon to the exile, with the temple at the centre. Solomon builds it and God's glory fills it. Afterwards, each king is weighed by whether he seeks the Lord and keeps his house of worship. Kings who humble themselves and seek God are blessed; those who forsake him suffer. The book ends with Jerusalem destroyed, and then, in its final verses, with Cyrus' decree letting God's people return to rebuild the temple.

## Setting

- **When:** from Solomon's accession (about 970 BC) to the fall of Jerusalem (586 BC) and Cyrus' decree (539/538 BC). Written after the return from exile.
- **Where:** Jerusalem and Judah. The northern kingdom appears mainly when it touches Judah.
- **Who wrote it:** the same Chronicler as 1 Chronicles, probably between about 515 and 400 BC; Jewish tradition says Ezra (Pratt; Reformation Study Bible). The Reformation Heritage KJV Study Bible (RH) thinks Ezra a likely author and dates the work to about 450–425 BC. The split into two books was only a later convenience for handling the scrolls.
- **Where it sits:** the end of Chronicles, and the end of the Hebrew Bible. It assumes readers know Kings, and focuses on Judah's spiritual health rather than its wars and wealth. Its last verses (36:22–23) are nearly the same as the opening of Ezra (Ezra 1:1–3).

## Structure

The Reformation Study Bible's three parts (Pratt divides Chronicles the same way). Solomon's reign is arranged symmetrically, with the temple's dedication and God's answer at its centre (5:2–7:22), and his power struggle and later fall are left out. Afterwards each king is measured by the ideal of David and Solomon: faithful to the law, supporting the temple, listening to prophets and priests. The RSB subdivides by reign:

- 1–9: The united kingdom under Solomon: wisdom, the temple built and dedicated, God's answer, Solomon's glory
- 10–28: The divided kingdom, told from Judah's side: Rehoboam, Abijah, Asa, Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, Ahaziah, Athaliah's usurpation, Joash, Amaziah, Uzziah, Jotham and Ahaz
- 29–36: The reunited kingdom: Hezekiah gathers the faithful of north and south around the temple again
    - 29–32: Hezekiah: the temple cleansed, a great Passover, deliverance from Assyria
    - 33: Manasseh's sin and repentance, and Amon
    - 34–35: Josiah's reforms and Passover
    - 36: Josiah's successors, the exile, and Cyrus' decree

## Chapter by chapter

- **2 Chronicles 1:** Solomon worships at Gibeon and asks for wisdom. God also gives him riches and honour.
- **2 Chronicles 2:** Solomon arranges with Hiram of Tyre for materials and craftsmen for the temple.
- **2 Chronicles 3:** The temple is built on Mount Moriah, at the threshing floor David had bought (3:1).
- **2 Chronicles 4:** The temple furnishings: the altar, the bronze sea, lampstands and tables.
- **2 Chronicles 5:** The ark is brought in. As the Levites sing "he is good, for his steadfast love endures forever", the cloud of glory fills the house.
- **2 Chronicles 6:** Solomon's prayer of dedication.
- **2 Chronicles 7:** Fire falls from heaven. God answers: "if my people … humble themselves, and pray and seek my face and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear" (7:14).
- **2 Chronicles 8:** Solomon's building projects and the ordering of temple worship.
- **2 Chronicles 9:** The Queen of Sheba's visit, Solomon's wealth, and his death.
- **2 Chronicles 10:** Rehoboam's harshness divides the kingdom.
- **2 Chronicles 11:** Rehoboam fortifies Judah, and faithful priests and Levites from the north move to Judah.
- **2 Chronicles 12:** Rehoboam abandons God's law, and Shishak of Egypt invades. The leaders humble themselves, and God limits the disaster.
- **2 Chronicles 13:** Abijah rebukes Jeroboam's apostasy and defeats Israel while relying on the Lord.
- **2 Chronicles 14:** Asa removes idols and defeats a huge Cushite army, crying to the Lord.
- **2 Chronicles 15:** The prophet Azariah urges Asa to seek God, and Judah makes a covenant to seek him.
- **2 Chronicles 16:** Late in his reign, Asa relies on Syria instead of God, imprisons the prophet who rebukes him, and seeks doctors but not the Lord.
- **2 Chronicles 17:** Jehoshaphat strengthens Judah and sends teachers through the land with the Book of the Law.
- **2 Chronicles 18:** Jehoshaphat allies with Ahab. Micaiah prophesies truly, and Ahab dies.
- **2 Chronicles 19:** Jehoshaphat is rebuked for helping the wicked, and appoints judges to judge in the fear of the Lord.
- **2 Chronicles 20:** A vast army comes against Judah. Jehoshaphat prays, "our eyes are on you" (20:12). Singers lead the army, and the enemies destroy one another.
- **2 Chronicles 21:** Jehoram follows Ahab's ways and dies of a terrible disease, unmourned.
- **2 Chronicles 22:** Ahaziah reigns briefly, and Athaliah seizes the throne.
- **2 Chronicles 23:** Jehoiada the priest crowns the boy Joash, and Athaliah is executed.
- **2 Chronicles 24:** Joash repairs the temple while Jehoiada lives. Afterwards he turns to idols and has Jehoiada's son Zechariah stoned in the temple court.
- **2 Chronicles 25:** Amaziah defeats Edom, then worships Edom's gods and is defeated by Israel.
- **2 Chronicles 26:** Uzziah prospers while he seeks God. Proud, he burns incense in the temple and is struck with leprosy.
- **2 Chronicles 27:** Jotham does right and grows strong.
- **2 Chronicles 28:** Ahaz is deeply idolatrous. Israel captures many of Judah, but frees them at the word of the prophet Oded.
- **2 Chronicles 29:** Hezekiah reopens and cleanses the temple and restores worship.
- **2 Chronicles 30:** Hezekiah invites all Israel, even the north, to a great Passover, and prays for those not properly cleansed.
- **2 Chronicles 31:** Idols are destroyed and the people give tithes generously.
- **2 Chronicles 32:** Sennacherib invades. Hezekiah trusts the Lord and Assyria is turned back. Hezekiah's pride and repentance.
- **2 Chronicles 33:** Manasseh does great evil, is taken to Babylon in chains, humbles himself, and is restored. Amon follows his worst ways.
- **2 Chronicles 34:** Josiah seeks God from his youth, purges idols, and the Book of the Law is found.
- **2 Chronicles 35:** Josiah keeps a great Passover, and dies fighting Pharaoh Neco.
- **2 Chronicles 36:** The last kings ignore God's messengers. Jerusalem and the temple are burned, and the land rests for seventy years, as Jeremiah said. Cyrus decrees that God's people may go up and rebuild (36:22–23).

## Key people

- **Solomon:** builder of the temple.
- **Rehoboam:** under whom the kingdom divides.
- **Abijah:** Rehoboam's son, who defeats Jeroboam.
- **Asa:** a reforming king who later stops relying on God.
- **Jehoshaphat:** who teaches the law and trusts God in battle.
- **Athaliah:** a usurping queen.
- **Joash and Jehoiada:** the boy-king and the faithful priest.
- **Zechariah son of Jehoiada:** a priest-prophet martyred in the temple court.
- **Uzziah (Azariah):** a strong king struck with leprosy for his pride.
- **Ahaz:** a deeply idolatrous king.
- **Hezekiah:** cleanses the temple and holds a great Passover.
- **Manasseh:** Judah's most wicked king, who repents in captivity.
- **Josiah:** reformer and restorer of the law.
- **Zedekiah:** the last king of Judah.
- **Cyrus:** king of Persia, who allows the return.

## Themes

- **Seek the Lord:** the book's refrain. Kings who seek God prosper; those who forsake him fall (15:2; 26:5).
- **Humble yourselves and pray:** 7:14 is the key. Rehoboam, Hezekiah and even Manasseh humble themselves and find mercy (12:6–7; 32:26; 33:12–13).
- **Blessing and judgment:** the Chronicler often connects a king's faithfulness or unfaithfulness directly with the outcome, as a pointed lesson to his readers (Pratt). But he isn't simplistic: Asa is attacked after his reforms (16), and Sennacherib invades right after Hezekiah's faithfulness (32:1). His aim is to call people to repentance, since the way back to blessing is always open (7:13–14) (RSB).
- **The heart, not just ritual:** the Chronicler keeps saying that God wants wholehearted devotion, not mere performance (15:12, 15; 16:9; 31:21).
- **Kings as hopeful pictures:** he presents some kings, such as Abijah, Jotham and Hezekiah, at their best, to stir hope for the ideal king still to come (RSB).
- **Rest:** seeking God brings "rest", both peace from enemies and the joy of his presence, as under Solomon, Asa and Jehoshaphat (14:6–7; 15:15; 20:30). RH links it to the rest Hebrews says remains for God's people (Heb 4:9).
- **The temple at the centre:** the kings are judged largely by how they treat God's house and its worship.
- **All Israel:** Hezekiah invites the north to Passover (30), and the Chronicler welcomes northerners who return to the Lord.
- **Hope after exile:** the book ends not with destruction but with Cyrus' decree to go up and rebuild (36:22–23).

## How it points to Christ

The temple, filled with God's glory and built where judgment was turned away by sacrifice, points to Christ, God dwelling with us (John 1:14; 2:19–21). Manasseh's restoration shows that no sinner is beyond the reach of grace for those who humble themselves and turn to God (33:12–13; compare 1 Tim 1:15).

Jesus refers to the martyrdom of Zechariah son of Jehoiada (24:20–22) when he speaks of the blood of the prophets from Abel to Zechariah (Matt 23:35; Luke 11:51). In the Hebrew order that spans the whole Old Testament, from the first book (Genesis) to the last (Chronicles). The book's final words, an invitation to "go up" and rebuild God's house, leave the Old Testament looking forward to the true King and true temple.

## Common mix-ups

- Chronicles focuses on Judah; northern kings appear only when their story touches Judah.
- Chronicles leaves out Solomon's slide into idolatry (1 Kgs 11), not because it never happened, but because of the Chronicler's purpose.
- Manasseh's repentance (33:12–19) is recorded only in Chronicles.
- The last verses of 2 Chronicles and the first verses of Ezra are almost the same: Cyrus' decree.
- The Zechariah stoned in 2 Chronicles 24 is a priest, Jehoiada's son, not the prophet Zechariah of the post-exile book.
- Uzziah and Azariah are the same king (compare 2 Kgs 15).

## Going deeper

- **"If my people … humble themselves" (7:14).** This verse is often applied to modern nations. What did it mean in its original setting, the temple and Israel's covenant? How should Christians apply it today?
- **Immediate reward and judgment.** Pratt notes that the Chronicler often links blessing and judgment closely to obedience, to warn and encourage his readers. Compare Asa's two halves (14–16) or Uzziah's rise and fall (26). What lessons did the returned exiles need?
- **Hezekiah's inclusive Passover (30).** He invites the north and prays for those who aren't ceremonially clean but seek God. What does that say about the heart and the rules of worship?
- **Manasseh's restoration.** The worst king of Judah repents and is restored. Why does Chronicles include this, and Kings not?
- **The ending (36:22–23).** Why end the Hebrew Bible with "let him go up"? How does it leave the reader waiting for God's next act?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** Where was Solomon's temple built?

   **A:** On Mount Moriah, at the threshing floor David bought (3:1).

2. **Q:** What happened when the Levites praised God at the temple dedication?

   **A:** The cloud of the Lord's glory filled the house (5:13–14).

3. **Q:** What does 2 Chronicles 7:14 promise?

   **A:** If God's people humble themselves, pray, seek his face and turn from wickedness, he will hear, forgive and heal their land.

4. **Q:** How did Jehoshaphat's army go out against the invaders?

   **A:** With singers leading, praising God (20:21–22).

5. **Q:** Why was Uzziah struck with leprosy?

   **A:** In pride he burned incense in the temple, which only priests could do (26:16–21).

6. **Q:** Who invited the northern tribes to Passover?

   **A:** Hezekiah, sending letters from Beersheba to Dan (30:1–10).

7. **Q:** What happened to Manasseh in captivity?

   **A:** He humbled himself, prayed, and God restored him to Jerusalem (33:11–13).

8. **Q:** Which king found the Book of the Law?

   **A:** Josiah (34:14–15).

9. **Q:** How long did the land rest during the exile?

   **A:** Seventy years, as Jeremiah prophesied (36:21).

10. **Q:** How does 2 Chronicles end?

    **A:** With Cyrus' decree allowing God's people to go up and rebuild the temple (36:22–23).
