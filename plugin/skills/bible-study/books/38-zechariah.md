# Zechariah

## Summary

Zechariah encourages the returned exiles to finish the temple and return to the Lord. In eight night visions God assures them that he is jealous for Jerusalem: he will judge the nations, cleanse his people, and complete his house "not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit" (4:6). The second half looks further ahead to the coming King riding on a donkey, a rejected shepherd sold for thirty pieces of silver, one "whom they have pierced", a fountain to cleanse from sin, and the day when the Lord will be king over all the earth.

## Setting

- **When:** Zechariah began in 520 BC, two months after Haggai (1:1), with dated messages to 518 BC (7:1). Chapters 9–14 may come from later in his life.
- **Where:** Jerusalem, among the people who had returned from Babylon.
- **Who wrote it:** Zechariah son of Berechiah, son of Iddo, from a priestly family (1:1; Neh 12:16). Many scholars assign chapters 9–14 to a later author because of the change in style. Traditional interpreters see one prophet with two kinds of message.
- **Where it sits:** among the Twelve, a contemporary of Haggai (Ezra 5:1; 6:14). It is the longest of the Minor Prophets.

## Structure

- 1:1–6: A call to return to the Lord
- 1:7–6:15: Eight night visions, and the crowning of Joshua
- 7–8: Questions about fasting; God's promises for Zion
- 9–11: First oracle: the coming King, and the rejected Shepherd
- 12–14: Second oracle: Jerusalem's deliverance, the pierced one, cleansing, and the Lord as King over all the earth

## Chapter by chapter

- **Zechariah 1:** "Return to me … and I will return to you" (1:3). Vision 1: a rider among myrtle trees reports the world at ease, and God declares he is jealous for Jerusalem. Vision 2: four horns that scattered Judah, and four craftsmen to throw them down.
- **Zechariah 2:** Vision 3: a man with a measuring line. Jerusalem will spill beyond its walls, and the Lord himself will be a wall of fire around it. Many nations will join themselves to the Lord.
- **Zechariah 3:** Vision 4: Joshua the high priest stands in filthy garments while Satan accuses him. The Lord rebukes Satan and clothes Joshua in clean garments. God will bring "my servant the Branch" and remove the land's iniquity in a single day.
- **Zechariah 4:** Vision 5: a golden lampstand fed by two olive trees. "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit" (4:6). Zerubbabel will finish the temple. Don't despise the day of small things.
- **Zechariah 5:** Vision 6: a huge flying scroll of curse against thieves and liars. Vision 7: a woman called Wickedness sealed in a basket and carried off to Babylon.
- **Zechariah 6:** Vision 8: four chariots go out to patrol the earth. Joshua is crowned as a sign of "the man whose name is the Branch", who will build the Lord's temple and be a priest on his throne (6:12–13).
- **Zechariah 7:** People ask whether to keep fasting for the temple's destruction. God says their fasts were for themselves; he wants true justice, kindness and mercy, as earlier generations refused to give.
- **Zechariah 8:** God will return to Zion. Old men and women will sit in the streets and children will play. Fasts will become feasts. Ten men from every nation will take hold of a Jew's robe, saying "Let us go with you, for we have heard that God is with you" (8:23).
- **Zechariah 9:** Judgment on Israel's neighbours. "Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion! … your king is coming to you; righteous and having salvation is he, humble and mounted on a donkey" (9:9). He will speak peace to the nations, and the blood of the covenant will free the prisoners.
- **Zechariah 10:** The people wander like sheep without a shepherd. God will strengthen Judah and gather Ephraim home.
- **Zechariah 11:** The prophet shepherds a flock doomed to slaughter. He breaks his staffs Favour and Union. He is paid thirty pieces of silver, which he throws to the potter in the Lord's house (11:12–13). A worthless shepherd will follow.
- **Zechariah 12:** God will make Jerusalem a cup of staggering to the nations. He will pour out a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy, and "they will look on me, on him whom they have pierced", and mourn as for an only child (12:10).
- **Zechariah 13:** A fountain will be opened to cleanse from sin and uncleanness (13:1). Idols and false prophets will be removed. "Strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered" (13:7), and a refined remnant will call on God's name.
- **Zechariah 14:** On the day of the Lord, nations gather against Jerusalem. The Lord stands on the Mount of Olives, living waters flow from Jerusalem, and the Lord will be king over all the earth. Even the bells of horses will say "Holy to the Lord".

## Key people

- **Zechariah:** a prophet and priest after the exile.
- **Joshua the high priest:** cleansed and crowned in the visions (3; 6).
- **Zerubbabel:** the governor who will complete the temple (4).
- **The Branch:** the coming priest-king (3:8; 6:12).
- **Satan:** the accuser of Joshua, rebuked by the Lord (3:1–2).
- **The angel of the Lord:** who intercedes for Jerusalem (1:12) and defends Joshua (3).
- **The shepherd:** rejected and valued at thirty pieces of silver (11), struck so the sheep scatter (13:7).

## Key events

- The call to return to the Lord (1:1–6)
- The eight night visions (1–6)
- Joshua cleansed (3), and crowned (6)
- God's promises for Zion (8)
- The prophecy of the King on a donkey (9:9)
- Thirty pieces of silver thrown to the potter (11:12–13)
- "They will look on him whom they have pierced" (12:10)
- The fountain for sin (13:1)
- The Lord king over all the earth (14)

## Themes

- **Return to God:** "Return to me … and I will return to you" (1:3). Timmer counts Zechariah among the prophets pressing repentance after the exile.
- **God's jealous love for Jerusalem:** he will restore and protect his city (1:14–17; 8:2–3).
- **Not by human strength:** God's work is done by his Spirit (4:6).
- **Cleansing from sin:** Joshua's filthy garments replaced (3), wickedness removed (5), and a fountain opened (13:1).
- **The coming King and Shepherd:** humble, righteous and bringing salvation (9:9), yet rejected, sold, pierced and struck (11–13).
- **The nations drawn to God:** many nations will join the Lord, and people from every language seek him in Jerusalem (2:11; 8:20–23; 14:16). Timmer highlights the Twelve's vision of some among the nations sharing in deliverance.
- **The final day and God's kingdom:** the Lord will reign over all the earth and everything will be holy (14:9, 20–21).

## How it points to Christ

The New Testament draws heavily on Zechariah 9–14, especially for Jesus' last week:

- **The King on a donkey:** Jesus enters Jerusalem as the humble king of 9:9 (Matt 21:4–5; John 12:14–15).
- **The struck shepherd:** on the night he was arrested, Jesus quoted "strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered" (13:7; Matt 26:31).
- **Thirty pieces of silver:** the price paid for Jesus and later thrown into the temple and used to buy the potter's field (11:12–13; Matt 26:15; 27:3–10).
- **The pierced one:** John sees "they will look on him whom they have pierced" fulfilled at the cross (12:10; John 19:37), and in Christ's return (Rev 1:7).
- **The Branch:** priest and king on one throne (6:12–13), fulfilled in Jesus our King and High Priest (Heb 7–8).
- **The fountain for cleansing** (13:1) flows from Christ's sacrifice.
- **The living waters** (14:8) echo in Jesus' promise of rivers of living water (John 7:37–39).

Joshua's filthy garments replaced with clean ones (3:3–5) pictures justification: Christ's righteousness given to sinners.

## Common mix-ups

- Zechariah the prophet is different from Zechariah son of Jehoiada (2 Chr 24) and from John the Baptist's father.
- Matthew attributes the thirty pieces of silver prophecy to "Jeremiah" (Matt 27:9). It combines Zechariah 11:12–13 with Jeremiah's potter and field themes (Jer 18–19; 32).
- Zechariah's eight visions all come in one night (1:8).
- Joshua the high priest in Zechariah 3 and 6 is not Joshua son of Nun.
- "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit" (4:6) was spoken to Zerubbabel about rebuilding the temple.

## Going deeper

- **The eight night visions.** Map them as a sequence: God's care for Jerusalem, judgment on the nations, cleansing of the priest and people, and his Spirit empowering the work. How do they encourage a small, struggling community?
- **Joshua's clean garments (3).** How does this vision picture the gospel: accusation silenced, filthy garments removed, righteousness given? Compare Romans 8:33–34.
- **The shepherd chapters (11; 13).** Trace the rejected, sold and struck shepherd into the Gospels. Why does God's true Shepherd have to suffer?
- **"Him whom they have pierced" (12:10).** Note that God speaks of "me" being pierced. How does John use this at the cross and at Christ's return?
- **The nations and Jerusalem (8:20–23; 14:16).** How does Zechariah's vision of the nations seeking God connect with the church's mission?

## Q&A

1. What did God say in Zechariah 1:3? "Return to me … and I will return to you."
2. How many night visions did Zechariah see? Eight (1:7–6:8).
3. What happened to Joshua the high priest in the vision? Satan accused him, but the Lord rebuked Satan and gave Joshua clean garments (3).
4. How would the temple be completed, according to 4:6? "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit."
5. What is the coming one called in 3:8 and 6:12? The Branch.
6. How does Zechariah 9:9 describe the coming king? Righteous, having salvation, humble, and riding on a donkey.
7. How much was the shepherd valued at? Thirty pieces of silver (11:12).
8. What will people do when they look on the one they pierced? Mourn for him as for an only child (12:10).
9. What will be opened to cleanse from sin? A fountain (13:1).
10. How does the book end? With the Lord king over all the earth, and everything "Holy to the Lord" (14:9, 20–21).
