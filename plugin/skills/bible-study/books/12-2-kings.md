# 2 Kings

## Summary

2 Kings traces both kingdoms to their fall. Elijah is taken to heaven and Elisha carries on with many miracles. But the northern kingdom never turns from Jeroboam's idols and falls to Assyria in 722 BC. Judah has bright moments under Hezekiah and Josiah, but Manasseh's wickedness seals its fate: Babylon destroys Jerusalem and the temple in 586 BC. The book ends in exile, with one small sign of hope: David's descendant Jehoiachin released from prison in Babylon.

## Setting

- **When:** from about 853 BC (King Ahaziah of Israel) to 561 BC (Jehoiachin's release in Babylon). Landmarks (Reformation Study Bible): Samaria falls to Assyria in 722 BC; Hezekiah (715–686 BC) leads a great reform; Josiah (640–609 BC) tries to reform and reunite the land, but dies at Megiddo in 609 BC; Babylon destroys Jerusalem in 586 BC.
- **Where:** Israel (capital Samaria) and Judah (capital Jerusalem), with the great empires pressing in: Syria (Aram), Assyria, Egypt, and finally Babylon.
- **Who wrote it:** unknown, probably a Judean writer during the exile, about 550 BC. 1 and 2 Kings were one book (Fullilove; RSB).
- **Where it sits:** the second half of Kings and the end of the Former Prophets. Prophets like Isaiah, Hosea, Amos, Micah, Jeremiah and Jonah ministered during these reigns. Read their books alongside it.

## Structure

The Reformation Study Bible's outline continues from 1 Kings:

- 1:1–8:15: Prophets and kings: Elijah taken up, and Elisha's ministry (continuing from 1 Kgs 17)
- 8:16–17:41: The late divided kingdom: from Jehu's purge to the fall of Samaria (722 BC), with a reflection on why Israel went into exile (17:7–23)
- 18–25: Judah alone: Hezekiah, Manasseh, Josiah, the fall of Jerusalem (586 BC), Gedaliah's murder, and Jehoiachin in Babylon

## Chapter by chapter

- **2 Kings 1:** King Ahaziah of Israel consults Baal-zebub after a fall. Elijah calls down fire on the soldiers sent to arrest him, and Ahaziah dies.
- **2 Kings 2:** Elijah is taken to heaven in a whirlwind, with chariots of fire. Elisha receives a double portion of his spirit, parts the Jordan, and heals Jericho's water.
- **2 Kings 3:** Israel, Judah and Edom march against rebellious Moab. Elisha promises water, and Moab is beaten.
- **2 Kings 4:** Elisha multiplies a widow's oil, raises the Shunammite woman's son, makes poisoned stew safe, and feeds a hundred men with twenty loaves.
- **2 Kings 5:** Naaman, a Syrian general, is healed of leprosy by washing in the Jordan seven times. Elisha's servant Gehazi takes money and is struck with leprosy.
- **2 Kings 6:** An axe head floats. Elisha's servant sees the hills full of fiery chariots, and the Syrian raiders are blinded. Samaria is besieged and starving.
- **2 Kings 7:** Four lepers find the Syrian camp deserted, the siege is lifted, and Elisha's word about cheap food comes true.
- **2 Kings 8:** The Shunammite's land is restored. Hazael murders Ben-hadad and takes the Syrian throne. Jehoram and Ahaziah reign in Judah.
- **2 Kings 9:** Jehu is anointed king of Israel. He kills King Joram of Israel and King Ahaziah of Judah, and Jezebel is thrown from a window and eaten by dogs.
- **2 Kings 10:** Jehu wipes out Ahab's family and the worshippers of Baal, but keeps Jeroboam's golden calves.
- **2 Kings 11:** Athaliah seizes Judah's throne and kills the royal family, but the baby Joash is hidden. Jehoiada the priest crowns him seven years later.
- **2 Kings 12:** Joash repairs the temple, buys off Hazael with temple treasures, and is murdered.
- **2 Kings 13:** Jehoahaz and Jehoash reign in Israel. Elisha dies, and a dead man thrown into his grave comes back to life.
- **2 Kings 14:** Amaziah of Judah is defeated by Israel. Jeroboam II restores Israel's borders, as Jonah prophesied (14:25).
- **2 Kings 15:** Azariah (Uzziah) and Jotham reign in Judah. Israel churns through Zechariah, Shallum, Menahem, Pekahiah and Pekah as Assyria rises.
- **2 Kings 16:** Ahaz of Judah turns to Assyria for help and copies a pagan altar for the temple.
- **2 Kings 17:** Assyria captures Samaria and exiles Israel (722 BC). The writer explains why: they rejected God's covenant and worshipped idols. Foreigners are resettled in the land.
- **2 Kings 18:** Hezekiah trusts the Lord and removes idols, even the bronze serpent. Sennacherib of Assyria invades, and his officer mocks Judah's God.
- **2 Kings 19:** Hezekiah spreads the threatening letter before the Lord and prays. Isaiah brings God's answer, and the angel of the Lord strikes the Assyrian army.
- **2 Kings 20:** Hezekiah is healed and given fifteen more years, with the shadow going back as a sign. He shows his treasures to Babylonian envoys, and Isaiah foretells exile to Babylon.
- **2 Kings 21:** Manasseh reigns 55 years and is the most wicked king of Judah. God declares Jerusalem's doom. His son Amon is assassinated.
- **2 Kings 22:** Young Josiah repairs the temple and the Book of the Law is found. The prophetess Huldah confirms judgment will come, though not in Josiah's day.
- **2 Kings 23:** Josiah renews the covenant, destroys idols (including Bethel's altar, as foretold in 1 Kgs 13:2), and keeps a great Passover. He is killed by Pharaoh Neco at Megiddo.
- **2 Kings 24:** Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim and Jehoiachin reign. Nebuchadnezzar takes Jehoiachin and the leading people to Babylon (597 BC).
- **2 Kings 25:** Zedekiah rebels, Jerusalem is besieged, and the city and temple are burned (586 BC). Governor Gedaliah is murdered. In Babylon, Jehoiachin is released and given a place at the king's table.

## Key people

- **Elijah:** taken to heaven without dying.
- **Elisha:** Elijah's successor, with a ministry full of miracles of healing, provision and life.
- **Naaman:** the Syrian commander healed of leprosy, who confesses Israel's God.
- **Gehazi:** Elisha's greedy servant.
- **Jehu:** a fierce king who destroys Ahab's house and Baal worship.
- **Jezebel:** the queen who dies as Elijah foretold.
- **Athaliah:** the queen who seizes Judah's throne.
- **Joash and Jehoiada:** the boy-king who was saved, and the priest who crowned him.
- **Jeroboam II:** a powerful king of Israel.
- **Hezekiah:** a faithful king of Judah who trusts God against Assyria.
- **Sennacherib:** the Assyrian king who besieges Jerusalem.
- **Isaiah:** the prophet who counsels Hezekiah.
- **Manasseh:** Judah's most wicked king.
- **Josiah:** a reforming king who finds the Book of the Law.
- **Huldah:** the prophetess consulted by Josiah.
- **Nebuchadnezzar:** king of Babylon, who destroys Jerusalem.
- **Zedekiah:** Judah's last king.
- **Jehoiachin:** the exiled king released at the end.

## Themes

- **Covenant curses fulfilled:** the exiles of Israel and Judah are exactly what Deuteronomy warned (Deut 28:36, 64; 2 Kgs 17:7–23). Fullilove shows Kings explaining why the exile happened.
- **The prophetic word never fails:** prophecies are fulfilled again and again, sometimes centuries later (1 Kgs 13:2 and 2 Kgs 23:15–16). Fullilove highlights "delayed retribution": God's patience before judgment falls.
- **God's patience and justice:** God sends prophet after prophet (17:13), but finally judges.
- **Reform is real but not enough:** Hezekiah and Josiah are among Judah's best kings, yet the people's hearts aren't changed and judgment comes (23:25–27).
- **God's care for outsiders:** a Syrian general (5) and a Shunammite woman (4; 8) receive God's mercy through Elisha.
- **The Davidic line preserved:** despite judgment, God keeps a "lamp" for David (8:19). The book ends with David's heir alive and honoured above the other captive kings in Babylon (25:27–30), a glimmer of hope fulfilled in Christ, the Son of David (RSB).

## How it points to Christ

Elisha's miracles foreshadow Jesus' works. He raises a son for his mother (4:32–37; Luke 7:11–15), feeds a crowd with a few loaves with food left over (4:42–44; John 6:9–13), and cleanses a Gentile leper, Naaman, whom Jesus himself mentions (Luke 4:27). Elijah's ascent to heaven anticipates Christ's ascension and the giving of the Spirit to his followers, as Elisha received a double portion.

Above all, Kings ends with a question about God's promise to David. Jehoiachin's release (25:27–30) keeps it alive, and Matthew traces Jesus' genealogy through Jechoniah (Jehoiachin) "at the time of the deportation to Babylon" to the Christ (Matt 1:11–12, 17). The exile ends in the true King, who builds a temple that can't be destroyed (John 2:19–21).

## Common mix-ups

- Samaria (the northern capital) fell to Assyria in 722 BC. Jerusalem fell to Babylon in 586 BC, more than a century later.
- Elijah is taken up in 2 Kings 2. Elisha's ministry is mainly 2 Kings 2–13.
- The kings' names repeat across the two kingdoms. There is a Joram/Jehoram and an Ahaziah in both Israel and Judah around the same time.
- Jehu is a king of Israel, not a prophet. There is also a prophet named Jehu in 1 Kings 16.
- Hezekiah destroys the bronze serpent Moses made, because people had been burning incense to it (18:4).
- The "Samaritans" of the Gospels come partly from the peoples Assyria resettled in the north (17:24–41).
- Josiah is the boy-king who reforms Judah; Joash is the boy-king hidden from Athaliah.

## Going deeper

- **Why did Israel fall?** 2 Kings 17:7–23 is the writer's sermon on the north's collapse. Trace each charge back to the covenant in Deuteronomy. Read: Fullilove, The Mosaic Covenant as Expressed in Deuteronomy.
- **Delayed retribution.** Ahab repents and his judgment is postponed (1 Kgs 21:29). Josiah's goodness delays Judah's end (22:18–20). Manasseh's sin makes it certain (23:26). What do these reveal about God's patience and justice?
- **Naaman's conversion.** A foreign enemy healed by humbling himself in the Jordan (5). What does this say about grace, pride and the nations? Compare Luke 4:27.
- **Hezekiah's prayer.** He spreads Sennacherib's letter before the Lord (19:14–19). What does his prayer teach about God's glory and the nations? Compare Isaiah 36–39.
- **Josiah's discovery.** The law had been lost in the temple itself (22). What happens when God's people neglect his word, and when they rediscover it?
- **Hope at the end?** Why does Kings end with Jehoiachin eating at the Babylonian king's table (25:27–30)? Connect it to the Davidic covenant (2 Sam 7).

## Q&A

1. **Q:** How did Elijah leave the earth?

   **A:** Taken up to heaven by a whirlwind, with a chariot and horses of fire (2:11).

2. **Q:** What did Elisha ask from Elijah?

   **A:** A double portion of his spirit (2:9).

3. **Q:** How was Naaman healed?

   **A:** By washing seven times in the Jordan, as Elisha told him (5:14).

4. **Q:** Who killed Jezebel?

   **A:** Jehu ordered her thrown from a window (9:30–37).

5. **Q:** When and to whom did the northern kingdom fall?

   **A:** To Assyria, in 722 BC (17:6).

6. **Q:** Which king of Judah prayed when Assyria besieged Jerusalem?

   **A:** Hezekiah (19:14–19).

7. **Q:** What happened to Sennacherib's army?

   **A:** The angel of the Lord struck down 185,000 men (19:35).

8. **Q:** What did Josiah's workers find in the temple?

   **A:** The Book of the Law (22:8).

9. **Q:** When was Jerusalem destroyed, and by whom?

   **A:** In 586 BC, by Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon (25:8–10).

10. **Q:** How does 2 Kings end?

    **A:** With King Jehoiachin released from prison in Babylon and eating at the king's table (25:27–30).
