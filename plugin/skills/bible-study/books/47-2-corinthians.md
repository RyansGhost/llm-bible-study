# 2 Corinthians

## Summary

2 Corinthians is Paul's most personal letter. After a painful visit and a tearful letter, Paul writes with relief that many Corinthians have repented, but also with deep concern about "super-apostles" undermining him. He defends his ministry, not by boasting in strength, but by pointing to the cross: God's power is shown in weakness, like treasure in jars of clay. He also urges them to complete their generous gift for the poor believers in Jerusalem. Charles Hodge noted that no letter of Paul's carries such strong feeling (quoted in Waters).

## Setting

- **When:** about AD 55–56, from Macedonia, after Titus brought Paul news from Corinth (2:13; 7:5–7).
- **Where:** to the church at Corinth "with all the saints in the whole of Achaia" (1:1).
- **Who wrote it:** Paul, with Timothy (1:1).
- **Where it sits:** the second surviving letter to Corinth. Between 1 and 2 Corinthians came a "painful visit" (2:1) and a "tearful letter" (2:3–4). Some scholars think 2 Corinthians combines several letters, especially because of the change in tone at chapter 10. Many read it as one letter.

## Structure

Waters' outline:

- 1–7: Paul's defence of his ministry: his changed travel plans, the new covenant ministry, and his joy at the church's repentance
- 8–9: The offering from the Gentile churches for Jerusalem
- 10–13: Paul's defence against the "super-apostles": boasting in weakness

## Chapter by chapter

- **2 Corinthians 1:** The God of all comfort comforts us in affliction so we can comfort others. Paul nearly died in Asia. He explains his changed travel plans: all God's promises are "Yes" in Christ.
- **2 Corinthians 2:** Paul wrote a tearful letter instead of making another painful visit. Forgive and restore the repentant offender. Believers are the aroma of Christ.
- **2 Corinthians 3:** The Corinthians are Paul's letter of recommendation. He ministers a new covenant of the Spirit, more glorious than Moses' fading glory. "We all … beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed" (3:18).
- **2 Corinthians 4:** The god of this world blinds unbelievers, but God has shone in our hearts. "We have this treasure in jars of clay" (4:7): afflicted but not crushed. The outer self wastes away while the inner self is renewed.
- **2 Corinthians 5:** We long for our heavenly dwelling and walk by faith, not sight. All must appear before Christ's judgment seat. In Christ there is a new creation (5:17), and Paul is an ambassador of reconciliation: God "made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (5:21).
- **2 Corinthians 6:** Now is the day of salvation. Paul's hardships commend his ministry. Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers; you are the temple of the living God.
- **2 Corinthians 7:** Paul's joy at Titus' news. Godly grief produces repentance that leads to salvation.
- **2 Corinthians 8:** The poor Macedonian churches gave generously. "Though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor" (8:9). Finish what you began. Titus and others will collect the gift.
- **2 Corinthians 9:** Whoever sows bountifully will reap bountifully. "God loves a cheerful giver" (9:7). Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift.
- **2 Corinthians 10:** Paul defends his authority. His weapons aren't worldly: "take every thought captive to obey Christ" (10:5). "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord".
- **2 Corinthians 11:** Paul fears the Corinthians are being led astray by "super-apostles", false apostles disguised as servants of righteousness, as Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. Paul "boasts" of his sufferings: beatings, shipwrecks, dangers and anxiety for the churches.
- **2 Corinthians 12:** Paul was caught up to the third heaven, then given a thorn in the flesh. "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" (12:9). He is coming a third time.
- **2 Corinthians 13:** Examine yourselves before Paul arrives. Aim for restoration. A Trinitarian blessing: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all" (13:14).

## Key people

- **Paul:** defending his ministry with deep emotion.
- **Timothy:** co-sender.
- **Titus:** Paul's co-worker, who carried news and organised the collection (2:13; 7:6–15; 8:6, 16–23).
- **The offender:** a church member disciplined and now to be forgiven (2:5–11).
- **The "super-apostles":** false teachers undermining Paul (11:5, 13).
- **The Macedonian churches:** examples of generous giving (8:1–5).

## Key events

- Paul's affliction in Asia (1:8–11)
- The "painful visit" and the "tearful letter" (2:1–4)
- Titus' report of the Corinthians' repentance (7:5–16)
- The collection for Jerusalem (8–9)
- Paul's vision of the third heaven, and his thorn (12:1–10)

## Themes

- **Comfort in affliction:** God comforts his people in suffering so they can comfort others (1:3–7).
- **Power in weakness:** Paul's ministry looks weak, but God's power works through it (4:7; 12:9–10). Waters connects this to the cross: Paul's ministry follows the pattern of Christ's death and resurrection life (4:10–11; 13:4).
- **The new covenant:** a ministry of the Spirit, of righteousness and of surpassing glory (3).
- **Reconciliation:** God reconciled the world to himself in Christ and gives his servants the message of reconciliation (5:17–21).
- **Generous giving:** grace-motivated, cheerful giving modelled on Christ's self-giving (8–9).
- **True and false ministry:** credentials, eloquence and boasting against faithfulness, suffering and Christ-likeness (10–13).
- **Repentance and restoration:** godly grief that leads to repentance; forgiving the repentant (2:5–11; 7:8–11).

## How it points to Christ

At the heart of the letter is the great exchange: God made Christ, who knew no sin, to be sin for us, so that in him we become the righteousness of God (5:21). Christ, rich in glory, became poor so that we might become rich (8:9). All God's promises find their "Yes" in him (1:20). In his face shines "the light of the knowledge of the glory of God" (4:6). Believers carry around in their bodies the death of Jesus, so that his life may be shown in them (4:10). He was crucified in weakness but lives by God's power (13:4). Those in Christ are a new creation (5:17).

## Common mix-ups

- "My grace is sufficient for you … power is made perfect in weakness" is 2 Corinthians 12:9.
- "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation" is 2 Corinthians 5:17.
- "God loves a cheerful giver" is 2 Corinthians 9:7, not Proverbs.
- The "thorn in the flesh" isn't identified. Suggestions include illness, poor eyesight, or opponents, but the text doesn't say.
- The "third heaven" (12:2) means the presence of God (paradise), not a ranking of heavens to work through.
- "Do not be unequally yoked" (6:14) is from this letter.
- The "super-apostles" are false teachers, not the original Twelve.

## Going deeper

- **Treasure in jars of clay (4:7–18).** Why does God put the treasure of the gospel in weak human containers? How does this shape how we view suffering and ministry?
- **The great exchange (5:21).** How does this verse support the Reformation teaching of Christ bearing our sin and his righteousness being credited to us?
- **One letter or several?** Some scholars think chapters 10–13 were a separate letter because of the sharp change of tone. How might a single-letter reading explain the shift?
- **Grace and giving (8–9).** Paul never commands an amount, but appeals to Christ's example and God's provision. What principles guide Christian giving today?
- **Boasting in weakness (11–12).** Why does Paul "boast" in his sufferings rather than his visions and achievements? What does this teach about leadership in the church?

## Q&A

1. How does Paul describe God in 1:3? The Father of mercies and the God of all comfort.
2. What does Paul say about God's promises in Christ? All of them find their "Yes" in him (1:20).
3. Which covenant does Paul minister? The new covenant, of the Spirit, not the letter (3:6).
4. Where do we have the treasure of the gospel? In jars of clay (4:7).
5. What is anyone in Christ? A new creation (5:17).
6. What did God do with Christ, according to 5:21? Made him to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.
7. What kind of giver does God love? A cheerful giver (9:7).
8. What does Paul call the false teachers? "Super-apostles" and false apostles (11:5, 13).
9. What did God say about Paul's thorn in the flesh? "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" (12:9).
10. How does 2 Corinthians end? With a Trinitarian blessing: the grace of Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit (13:14).
