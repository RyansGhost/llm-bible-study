# 2 Samuel

## Summary

2 Samuel is the story of David's reign. He becomes king, first over Judah and then over all Israel, makes Jerusalem his capital, and brings up the ark. God makes a covenant promising David a dynasty and a throne that will last forever (7). But David's adultery with Bathsheba and the murder of Uriah bring God's discipline: violence in his own family, Absalom's rebellion, and lasting turmoil. The book shows both the glory and the failure of the best human king.

## Setting

- **When:** David's reign, about 1010–970 BC (the Lutheran Study Bible gives 1009–970 BC).
- **Where:** Hebron, where David first reigns over Judah for seven and a half years; then Jerusalem, captured from the Jebusites and made the "city of David"; and the surrounding nations he defeats.
- **Who wrote it:** unknown, as with 1 Samuel. 1 Chronicles 29:29 mentions records by Samuel, Nathan and Gad.
- **Where it sits:** the second half of the one book of Samuel, continuing straight from Saul's death. 1 Kings picks up with David's old age.

## Structure

- 1–4: David king over Judah at Hebron; civil war with Saul's house
- 5–10: David king over all Israel: Jerusalem, the ark, God's covenant with David, victories, kindness to Mephibosheth
- 11–12: David's sin with Bathsheba, and Nathan's rebuke
- 13–20: The consequences in David's house: Amnon, Absalom's rebellion, Sheba's revolt
- 21–24: Appendix: famine and the Gibeonites, David's song, his last words, his mighty men, the census and the altar

## Chapter by chapter

- **2 Samuel 1:** An Amalekite reports Saul's death and claims credit, and David has him executed. David laments: "How the mighty have fallen!"
- **2 Samuel 2:** David is anointed king over Judah at Hebron. Abner makes Saul's son Ish-bosheth king over Israel, and Abner kills Asahel, Joab's brother.
- **2 Samuel 3:** David's house grows stronger. Abner defects to David but is murdered by Joab, and David mourns him publicly.
- **2 Samuel 4:** Two men murder Ish-bosheth and bring David his head. David executes them. Jonathan's son Mephibosheth is lame in both feet.
- **2 Samuel 5:** All Israel anoints David king. He captures Jerusalem from the Jebusites and defeats the Philistines.
- **2 Samuel 6:** David brings the ark to Jerusalem. Uzzah is struck down for touching it. David dances before the Lord, and Michal despises him.
- **2 Samuel 7:** David wants to build God a house, but God promises to build David a house: an offspring whose throne he will establish forever (7:12–16). David responds in humble prayer.
- **2 Samuel 8:** David defeats the Philistines, Moab, Zobah, Syria and Edom, and rules with justice.
- **2 Samuel 9:** David shows kindness to Mephibosheth for Jonathan's sake, giving him a place at his table.
- **2 Samuel 10:** The Ammonites humiliate David's envoys, and David defeats Ammon and its Syrian allies.
- **2 Samuel 11:** Staying home from war, David takes Bathsheba, and she conceives. He arranges for her husband Uriah to die in battle.
- **2 Samuel 12:** Nathan's parable of the poor man's lamb: "You are the man!" David repents (Ps 51), the child dies, and Solomon is born.
- **2 Samuel 13:** Amnon rapes his half-sister Tamar. Absalom kills Amnon two years later and flees.
- **2 Samuel 14:** Joab uses a wise woman of Tekoa to bring Absalom home, and David finally receives him.
- **2 Samuel 15:** Absalom steals the people's hearts and launches a revolt from Hebron. David flees Jerusalem across the Kidron, weeping.
- **2 Samuel 16:** Ziba slanders Mephibosheth, and Shimei curses David, who accepts it. Absalom publicly takes David's concubines.
- **2 Samuel 17:** Hushai's counsel defeats Ahithophel's, and Ahithophel hangs himself.
- **2 Samuel 18:** David's army defeats Absalom's in the forest of Ephraim. Absalom is caught by his hair in a tree and killed by Joab. David grieves: "O my son Absalom!"
- **2 Samuel 19:** Joab rebukes David's grief. David returns to Jerusalem, pardons Shimei, and the tribes quarrel over the king.
- **2 Samuel 20:** Sheba of Benjamin rebels. Joab murders Amasa, and a wise woman of Abel ends the revolt.
- **2 Samuel 21:** A famine because of Saul's crime against the Gibeonites. Rizpah guards her sons' bodies. David's men kill Philistine giants.
- **2 Samuel 22:** David's song of deliverance, nearly identical to Psalm 18.
- **2 Samuel 23:** David's last words about a righteous ruler, and the list of his mighty men, ending with Uriah the Hittite.
- **2 Samuel 24:** David's census brings a plague. He buys Araunah's threshing floor and builds an altar there, where the temple will later stand.

## Key people

- **David:** king of Judah, then all Israel. A man after God's heart who falls badly and repents deeply.
- **Joab:** David's ruthless army commander and nephew, who kills Abner, Absalom and Amasa.
- **Abner:** Saul's commander, who defects to David and is murdered by Joab.
- **Ish-bosheth:** Saul's son, a weak rival king over Israel.
- **Mephibosheth:** Jonathan's lame son, shown kindness by David.
- **Michal:** Saul's daughter and David's wife, who despises his dancing.
- **Nathan:** the prophet who delivers God's covenant promise (7) and confronts David's sin (12).
- **Bathsheba:** Uriah's wife, taken by David; later mother of Solomon.
- **Uriah the Hittite:** a loyal soldier murdered on David's orders.
- **Amnon, Tamar and Absalom:** David's children, whose rape, revenge and rebellion tear his house apart.
- **Ahithophel and Hushai:** David's counsellors, one who betrays him and one who stays loyal.
- **Shimei:** a man of Saul's family who curses David as he flees.

## Key events

- David's lament for Saul and Jonathan (1)
- David king over Judah, then over all Israel (2; 5)
- Jerusalem captured (5)
- The ark brought to Jerusalem (6)
- God's covenant with David (7)
- Kindness to Mephibosheth (9)
- David and Bathsheba; Uriah's murder (11)
- Nathan: "You are the man" (12)
- Absalom's rebellion and death (15–18)
- David's song of deliverance (22)
- The census, the plague, and Araunah's threshing floor (24)

## Themes

- **The Davidic covenant:** God promises David an everlasting house, kingdom and throne through his offspring (7:12–16; Ps 89; Ps 132). McKelvey shows how it carries forward the promises to Abraham, including that kings would come from him (Gen 17:6).
- **Sin and its consequences:** David is forgiven (12:13), yet "the sword shall never depart from your house" (12:10). The rest of the book unfolds that judgment.
- **Repentance:** David's honest confession (12:13; Ps 51) marks him off from Saul's excuses.
- **God's kindness (*hesed*):** David's loyal love to Mephibosheth (9) reflects God's own covenant loyalty.
- **Worship and God's holiness:** David's joy before the ark and Uzzah's death both show that God's presence is a delight and must be approached his way (6).
- **The limits of human kingship:** even the best king fails. Israel needs a greater Son of David.

## How it points to Christ

The covenant with David (7:12–16) is fulfilled in Jesus, the Son of David. The angel tells Mary that God will give him "the throne of his father David" and that his kingdom will have no end (Luke 1:32–33). Hebrews applies "I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son" (7:14) to Christ (Heb 1:5), and Peter says David foresaw the Messiah's resurrection (Acts 2:29–36).

David fleeing Jerusalem across the Kidron, rejected by his own people (15:23), anticipates Jesus crossing the Kidron on the night of his betrayal (John 18:1). Ahithophel, the trusted friend who betrayed the king and then hanged himself (17:23), anticipates Judas. And the threshing floor David bought becomes the temple site, the place of sacrifice that Christ fulfils.

## Common mix-ups

- Ish-bosheth (Saul's son, a rival king) and Mephibosheth (Jonathan's lame son, shown kindness) are different people.
- Joab, not David, kills Absalom, and against David's orders.
- Absalom rebels; Amnon rapes Tamar and is killed by Absalom. Don't mix up the two brothers.
- David's sin with Bathsheba is in 2 Samuel 11. Psalm 51 is his prayer of repentance afterwards.
- 2 Samuel 22 is almost the same as Psalm 18.
- 2 Samuel 24 says the Lord incited David's census; 1 Chronicles 21:1 says Satan did. Both are true in different senses: God's judgment working through Satan's malice.
- 2 Samuel 21:19 credits Elhanan with killing "Goliath"; 1 Chronicles 20:5 says he killed Goliath's brother. This is likely a copying issue, and David kills Goliath in 1 Samuel 17.

## Going deeper

- **What exactly does God promise David (7)?** A great name, a place for Israel, rest, a house (dynasty), an offspring who will build God's house, a father–son relationship, and a throne forever. Which promises are fulfilled in Solomon, and which only in Christ? Read: McKelvey, The Davidic Covenant.
- **Forgiven, yet disciplined.** Nathan says the Lord has put away David's sin (12:13), yet the child dies and the sword stays in his house. How do forgiveness and consequences fit together (Heb 12:5–11)?
- **David and the Psalms.** McKelvey's excursus links psalm titles to events in David's life: Psalm 3 (fleeing Absalom), Psalm 18 (deliverance), Psalm 51 (after Nathan). Read them alongside the stories.
- **Why did Uzzah die?** He touched the ark to steady it (6:6–7). Compare how the ark was meant to be carried (Num 4:15). What does it teach about God's holiness and his instructions for worship?
- **David as father.** David's failure to discipline Amnon and his indulgence of Absalom feed the disaster (13:21; 14:33). What does Scripture say about fathers and justice at home?

## Q&A

1. Where was David first made king, and over whom? At Hebron, over Judah (2:4).
2. Which city did David capture and make his capital? Jerusalem, from the Jebusites (5:6–9).
3. What did God promise David in chapter 7? To build him a "house", a dynasty whose throne will be established forever (7:11–16).
4. Why was Uzzah struck down? For touching the ark (6:6–7).
5. To whom did David show kindness for Jonathan's sake? Mephibosheth (9).
6. What sin did David commit with Bathsheba, and how did he cover it up? Adultery; he arranged for her husband Uriah to die in battle (11).
7. What did Nathan say to David after his parable? "You are the man!" (12:7).
8. Which of David's sons rebelled against him? Absalom (15).
9. How did Absalom die? Caught by his hair in an oak tree, then killed by Joab (18:9–15).
10. What did David buy from Araunah? His threshing floor, to build an altar where the plague stopped (24:18–25).
