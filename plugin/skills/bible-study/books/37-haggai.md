# Haggai

## Summary

Sixteen years after returning from exile, the people of Jerusalem have stopped rebuilding the temple and are busy with their own houses, and their harvests keep failing. In four dated messages over four months in 520 BC, Haggai calls them to "consider your ways" and rebuild God's house. The people obey, and God promises his presence, a future glory for the temple greater than Solomon's, and a special place for Zerubbabel, David's descendant.

## Setting

- **When:** the second year of King Darius of Persia, 520 BC. All four messages fall between late August and mid-December (1:1; 2:1, 10, 20). Timmer notes Haggai and Zechariah give some of the clearest dates in the Twelve.
- **Where:** Jerusalem and Judah, a small Persian province.
- **Who wrote it:** Haggai the prophet. Ezra mentions him with Zechariah encouraging the rebuilding (Ezra 5:1; 6:14).
- **Where it sits:** among the Twelve, the first of the three prophets after the exile (Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi). Its events fit into Ezra 4:24–6:15.

## Structure

Four messages, each precisely dated:

- 1:1–15: Rebuild the house! (late August). The people obey and work begins (September).
- 2:1–9: Take courage: the latter glory will be greater (October)
- 2:10–19: Unclean and holy: from today I will bless you (December)
- 2:20–23: Zerubbabel, God's signet ring (December)

## Chapter by chapter

- **Haggai 1:** The people say it isn't time to rebuild the Lord's house. Haggai asks whether it is time for them to live in panelled houses while God's house lies in ruins (1:4). "Consider your ways": they sow much and harvest little, because they neglect God's house. Zerubbabel, Joshua the high priest and the remnant obey and fear the Lord. "I am with you, declares the Lord" (1:13). Work begins three weeks later.
- **Haggai 2:** To those who remember Solomon's temple and think this one is nothing: be strong, "for I am with you", and my Spirit remains among you. God will shake the heavens, the earth and the nations, and fill this house with glory: "the latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former" (2:6–9). Through questions about holy and unclean things, God shows that their neglect had defiled their work, but from this day he will bless them. Zerubbabel will be like God's signet ring, chosen (2:23).

## Key people

- **Haggai:** a prophet after the exile.
- **Darius:** king of Persia (Darius I).
- **Zerubbabel:** governor of Judah and descendant of David.
- **Joshua (Jeshua):** the high priest.
- **The remnant of the people:** those who returned from exile.

## Key events

- The call to rebuild the temple (1:1–11)
- The people's obedience, and work begins (1:12–15)
- The promise of greater glory (2:1–9)
- The promise of blessing from that day (2:10–19)
- Zerubbabel named God's signet ring (2:20–23)

## Themes

- **Priorities:** God's house had been neglected while people built their own. Haggai calls them to put God first (1:2–9; compare Matt 6:33).
- **"Consider your ways":** examine your life in light of God's word (1:5, 7; 2:15, 18).
- **Obedience and repentance:** the people respond quickly (1:12). Timmer lists Haggai among the prophets who press repentance on God's people.
- **God's presence:** "I am with you" (1:13; 2:4), and his Spirit remains among them (2:5).
- **Future glory:** God will shake all things and fill his house with glory (2:6–9).
- **The Davidic hope:** Zerubbabel, as God's chosen signet ring, keeps David's line alive (2:23; compare Jer 22:24).

## How it points to Christ

Hebrews quotes Haggai's promise to shake the heavens and the earth (2:6). It contrasts the shaking of everything created with the unshakable kingdom believers receive in Christ (Heb 12:26–28). The "latter glory" of the second temple was fulfilled when the Lord himself came to it: the infant Jesus presented there (Luke 2:22–32), and Jesus teaching in its courts. He is the true temple (John 2:19–21).

Zerubbabel, God's chosen signet, appears in Jesus' genealogy (Matt 1:12–13; Luke 3:27). The promise to David's line continues toward the greater Son of David.

## Common mix-ups

- Haggai prophesied after the exile (520 BC), not before. He encouraged the rebuilding of the second temple, which was finished in 516/515 BC (Ezra 6:15).
- Haggai and Zechariah prophesied at the same time and are named together in Ezra 5:1.
- The temple, not the walls, is Haggai's concern. The walls are Nehemiah's, about 75 years later.
- Joshua the high priest here is the same as Jeshua in Ezra.

## Going deeper

- **"Is it a time for you to dwell in your panelled houses?" (1:4).** What are modern ways of putting our comfort ahead of God's work? How does Matthew 6:33 apply?
- **Discouragement in small beginnings (2:3).** Older people thought the new temple was nothing compared with Solomon's. How does God answer? Compare Zechariah 4:10: "who has despised the day of small things?"
- **Holy and unclean (2:10–14).** Uncleanness spreads by contact; holiness doesn't. What does that teach about sin and our need for cleansing?
- **Shaking all things (2:6–7).** How does Hebrews 12:26–28 apply this? What remains when everything is shaken?
- **Zerubbabel the signet ring.** Compare Jeremiah 22:24, where God rejects Jehoiachin as a signet ring. Why does Haggai's reversal matter for the Messiah's line?

## Q&A

1. When did Haggai prophesy? In 520 BC, the second year of King Darius of Persia (1:1).
2. What had the people stopped building? The temple, the house of the Lord.
3. What were the people busy with instead? Their own panelled houses (1:4).
4. What phrase does Haggai repeat to make the people think? "Consider your ways" (1:5, 7).
5. Why were their harvests poor? Because they had neglected God's house (1:9–11).
6. Who led the people in obeying? Zerubbabel the governor and Joshua the high priest (1:12–14).
7. What did God promise about the new temple's glory? The latter glory would be greater than the former (2:9).
8. What did God say he would shake? The heavens, the earth, the sea, the dry land and all nations (2:6–7).
9. What did God call Zerubbabel? His signet ring, chosen (2:23).
10. Which prophet ministered alongside Haggai? Zechariah (Ezra 5:1).
