# 3 John

## Summary

3 John is a personal letter from "the elder" to his dear friend Gaius. John praises Gaius for walking in the truth and for welcoming travelling missionaries, "fellow workers for the truth", and asks him to send them on their way well supplied. By contrast, a church leader named Diotrephes, "who likes to put himself first", refuses to acknowledge John's authority, slanders him, won't welcome the missionaries, and throws out of the church those who do. John will deal with him when he comes. He commends Demetrius, who probably carried the letter. Hill shows how this short letter puts into practice 1 John's teaching on love for the brothers, and its warning against hating them.

## Setting

- **When:** late first century, about AD 85–100, probably soon after 2 John (Hill). The Reformation Study Bible notes there's no sign of the dispute about Christ's humanity, so it could be earlier, in the 80s, or as late as the 90s.
- **Where:** probably from Ephesus, to Gaius, a member of a church in the province of Asia.
- **Who wrote it:** "the elder" (v. 1), the same author as 2 John. Hill argues that the authority the elder assumes over more than one church fits the apostle John.
- **Where it sits:** the shortest book in the New Testament by word count in Greek, in the form of a short ancient letter of recommendation, commending Demetrius, probably its carrier, to Gaius. Hill suggests the letter John wrote "to the church" (v. 9) may be 2 John, which Diotrephes rejected.

## Structure

Hill's outline:

- vv. 1–2: Greeting
- vv. 3–4: The faithfulness of Gaius
- vv. 5–8: Faithful treatment of the missionaries
- vv. 9–11: The faithlessness of Diotrephes
- vv. 12–15: Commendation of Demetrius and closing

## Chapter by chapter

3 John has only one chapter, so here it is by section:

- **vv. 1–4:** The elder writes to the beloved Gaius, whom he loves in truth, and prays that he may be in good health, as it goes well with his soul. Brothers have testified to his faithfulness. "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth" (v. 4).
- **vv. 5–8:** Gaius has been faithful in welcoming the travelling brothers, though they were strangers. Send them on their way in a manner worthy of God. They went out for the sake of the name, accepting nothing from the Gentiles, so we ought to support people like them, to be fellow workers for the truth.
- **vv. 9–11:** John wrote something to the church, but Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first, doesn't acknowledge his authority. When John comes, he will bring up what Diotrephes is doing: talking wicked nonsense against John, refusing to welcome the brothers, stopping those who want to, and putting them out of the church. Don't imitate evil but good. Whoever does good is from God.
- **vv. 12–15:** Demetrius has a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth itself. John has much to write, but hopes to see Gaius soon and talk face to face. Peace be to you. The friends greet you.

## Key people

- **The elder:** the apostle John, who delights in his spiritual children walking in the truth.
- **Gaius:** a beloved and faithful believer, generous to travelling missionaries (vv. 1–8). Probably not the same as the other men named Gaius in the New Testament.
- **The brothers (missionaries):** who went out "for the sake of the name", taking nothing from unbelievers (vv. 5–8).
- **Diotrephes:** a church leader, apparently an elder, who loves to be first, rejects John's authority, and expels those who welcome the brothers (vv. 9–10).
- **Demetrius:** commended by everyone, probably the bearer of the letter (v. 12).

## Key events

- Gaius' welcome of travelling missionaries (vv. 5–6)
- Diotrephes' rejection of John's letter and of the brothers (vv. 9–10)
- John's planned visit (vv. 10, 14)

## Themes

- **Walking in the truth:** truth believed and lived, John's greatest joy (vv. 3–4).
- **Hospitality and mission support:** Christians should support those who go out for Christ's name (vv. 5–8). Supporting them makes us "fellow workers for the truth".
- **Pride in leadership:** Diotrephes' love of first place, against the servant leadership Jesus, Peter and Paul taught. Hill compares Matthew 23:6 and 1 Peter 5:3.
- **Imitating good:** "Do not imitate evil but imitate good" (v. 11). Whatever someone's office, you know them by their fruit (Matt 7:15–20).
- **Rewarded hospitality:** caring for the Lord's messengers is caring for Christ himself, and he will reward it (Matt 25:34–40).
- **Love for the brothers:** Hill shows 3 John is a real-life example of 1 John's teaching that hating the brothers shows you aren't of God.
- **Supporting true teachers:** the Reformation Heritage KJV Study Bible (RH) sums up the letter as love for the truth shown in practical support for faithful travelling missionaries (vv. 5–8).

## How it points to Christ

Christ isn't named in 3 John, but the missionaries went out "for the sake of the name" (v. 7), the name of Jesus (compare Acts 5:41; Phil 2:9–11). Gaius' generous love reflects Christ's love for his people, and John's standard, "whoever does good is from God" (v. 11), echoes 1 John's teaching that those born of God love their brothers. Diotrephes, loving to be first, is the opposite of Christ, who came not to be served but to serve (Mark 10:42–45).

## Common mix-ups

- "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth" is 3 John 4.
- "That you may be in good health, as it goes well with your soul" (v. 2) is a standard ancient letter greeting, not a promise of health and wealth.
- 3 John is about welcoming true teachers. 2 John is about not welcoming false ones.
- Diotrephes isn't accused of false doctrine, but of pride and lovelessness.
- 3 John is the only New Testament book that doesn't mention the name of Jesus or Christ, though it refers to "the name" (v. 7).

## Going deeper

- **Supporting missionaries (vv. 5–8).** What does it mean to send them "in a manner worthy of God"? How does your church do this?
- **Diotrephes (vv. 9–10).** What made his leadership so harmful? Compare 1 Peter 5:1–4 and 1 Timothy 3:1–7. Hill discusses whether he held an office or hosted a house church.
- **The health-and-wealth reading of v. 2.** Why is this a misreading? How does context protect against it?
- **Imitation (v. 11).** Whom are you imitating? Compare Paul's call to imitate him as he imitates Christ.
- **A good testimony (v. 12).** What would it mean to have a good testimony "from the truth itself"?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** To whom is 3 John written?

   **A:** Gaius, the elder's beloved friend (v. 1).

2. **Q:** What does John pray for Gaius in v. 2?

   **A:** That all may go well with him and he may be in good health, as it goes well with his soul.

3. **Q:** What is the elder's greatest joy?

   **A:** To hear that his children are walking in the truth (v. 4).

4. **Q:** Whom had Gaius been welcoming?

   **A:** Travelling brothers (missionaries), even though they were strangers (v. 5).

5. **Q:** Why should Christians support such workers?

   **A:** So that we may be fellow workers for the truth (v. 8).

6. **Q:** How does John describe Diotrephes?

   **A:** As one who likes to put himself first (v. 9).

7. **Q:** What did Diotrephes do to those who welcomed the brothers?

   **A:** Stopped them and put them out of the church (v. 10).

8. **Q:** What does John say about imitation?

   **A:** Don't imitate evil but imitate good (v. 11).

9. **Q:** Who received a good testimony from everyone?

   **A:** Demetrius (v. 12).

10. **Q:** How does John hope to finish the conversation?

    **A:** By seeing Gaius soon and talking face to face (vv. 13–14).
