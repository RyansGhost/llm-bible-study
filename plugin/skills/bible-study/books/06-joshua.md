## Summary

God keeps his promise to Abraham by bringing Israel into the land under Joshua. He fights for them, judges the Canaanites for their sin, and divides the land among the tribes. The conquest is left incomplete, and the book closes with Israel renewing the covenant and promising to serve the Lord.

## Setting

- **When:** right after Moses' death, at the end of the forty years. The Lutheran Study Bible dates the book's events to about 1406–1375 BC. Timmer uses the Amarna letters (mid-14th century BC) to show a Canaan split into feuding city-states under loose Egyptian control.
- **Where:** from the plains of Moab across the Jordan to Gilgal, Jericho and Ai, then the southern and northern campaigns, Shiloh, and finally Shechem.
- **Who wrote it:** the book doesn't say. Jewish tradition credits Joshua, with later additions such as his death; the Lutheran Study Bible lists "Joshua; an unknown writer". Timmer is cautious about theories that split it into many sources.
- **Where it sits:** the first of the "Former Prophets" (Joshua to Kings). It carries on directly from Deuteronomy and fulfils God's promise of the land (Gen 15:16–21).

## Structure

Timmer, following Koorevaar, traces four sections, each marked by a key verb:

- 1–5: **Cross:** preparing for entry and crossing the Jordan
- 6–12: **Take:** Jericho, Ai, Gibeon, and the southern and northern campaigns
- 13–21: **Divide:** allotting the land among the tribes
- 22–24: **Serve:** keeping the land by keeping the covenant

## Chapter by chapter

- **Joshua 1:** God commissions Joshua: be strong and courageous, and meditate on the Book of the Law day and night (1:6–9). The eastern tribes pledge to fight.
- **Joshua 2:** Two spies go to Jericho. Rahab hides them because she fears the Lord, and they promise to spare her household if she hangs a scarlet cord in her window.
- **Joshua 3:** The priests carrying the ark step into the flooded Jordan, the water stands up in a heap, and Israel crosses on dry ground.
- **Joshua 4:** Twelve stones from the riverbed are set up at Gilgal as a memorial for future generations.
- **Joshua 5:** The new generation is circumcised and keeps the Passover, and the manna stops. Joshua meets the commander of the Lord's army.
- **Joshua 6:** Israel marches around Jericho for seven days, the walls fall, and the city is devoted to destruction. Rahab and her family are spared.
- **Joshua 7:** Achan secretly takes devoted things, and Israel is beaten at Ai. Achan is exposed and stoned in the Valley of Achor.
- **Joshua 8:** Ai is taken by ambush. Joshua builds an altar on Mount Ebal and reads the whole law to the people.
- **Joshua 9:** The Gibeonites trick Israel into a treaty with worn-out clothes and mouldy bread. Israel didn't ask counsel of the Lord (9:14), but keeps its oath.
- **Joshua 10:** Five Amorite kings attack Gibeon. Israel rescues it, the Lord throws down hailstones, and the sun stands still until the battle is won. The southern campaign follows.
- **Joshua 11:** A northern coalition led by Jabin of Hazor is defeated at the waters of Merom. A summary of the whole campaign.
- **Joshua 12:** The list of thirty-one kings defeated.
- **Joshua 13:** Much land remains to be taken. The inheritance of the tribes east of the Jordan.
- **Joshua 14:** Caleb, at 85, asks for Hebron, the hill country he spied out forty-five years before.
- **Joshua 15:** Judah's territory. Caleb takes Hebron, Othniel wins Achsah, and the Jebusites stay in Jerusalem.
- **Joshua 16:** Ephraim's territory, where the Canaanites of Gezer remain.
- **Joshua 17:** Manasseh's territory. Zelophehad's daughters receive their inheritance, and Joseph's tribes are told to clear the forest hills.
- **Joshua 18:** The tabernacle is set up at Shiloh. The remaining land is surveyed, and Benjamin receives its lot.
- **Joshua 19:** The allotments for Simeon, Zebulun, Issachar, Asher, Naphtali and Dan. Joshua receives Timnath-serah.
- **Joshua 20:** The six cities of refuge are named.
- **Joshua 21:** Forty-eight towns for the Levites. "Not one word of all the good promises … had failed" (21:45).
- **Joshua 22:** The eastern tribes go home and build an altar by the Jordan. It nearly starts a war, until it is explained as a witness, not a rival altar.
- **Joshua 23:** Joshua's farewell to the leaders: hold fast to the Lord, and don't mix with the remaining nations.
- **Joshua 24:** Covenant renewal at Shechem: "choose this day whom you will serve … as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord" (24:15). Joshua dies, and Joseph's bones are buried.

## Key people

- **Joshua:** Moses' successor and one of the two faithful spies. Told again and again to be strong and courageous, he leads Israel into the land.
- **Rahab:** a Canaanite prostitute in Jericho who hides the spies and is saved by faith (Heb 11:31). She is in Jesus' genealogy (Matt 1:5).
- **Achan:** an Israelite who steals devoted things from Jericho and brings defeat at Ai.
- **Caleb:** the other faithful spy, who claims Hebron at 85.
- **The Gibeonites:** Canaanites who trick Israel into a treaty and are spared as woodcutters and water carriers.
- **Eleazar:** the high priest, who shares in dividing the land.
- **Phinehas:** the priest who confronts the eastern tribes over their altar (22).
- **Adoni-zedek:** king of Jerusalem, leader of the southern coalition (10).

## Themes

- **God keeps his promises:** the land is the Lord's gift to Abraham's descendants. Timmer prefers "giving" to "taking", because the book insists every victory is God's doing (21:43–45; 24:12–13).
- **God fights for his people:** "the Lord fought for Israel" (10:14). Jericho falls to marching and trumpets, not siege engines.
- **Faith versus unbelief:** Rahab, the Canaanite, believes and is saved; Achan, the Israelite, disobeys and dies.
- **Judgment on sin:** the conquest is God's judgment on the long-standing sin of the Canaanites (Gen 15:16). Timmer notes its language echoes the flood: "all that breathed" perished (10:40; Gen 7:22).
- **Rest:** God gives Israel rest from their enemies (21:44; 23:1). In Joshua this is rest from war, the setting for an obedient life, not yet the final rest (Heb 4:8).
- **Obedience to God's word:** success depends on meditating on and keeping the law (1:7–8; 23:6).
- **Keep your word, even at a cost:** Israel honours its oath to the Gibeonites despite the deception (9:19–20; compare 2 Sam 21 and Ps 15:4). This is our application, grounded in those texts.
- **Seek God before deciding:** the Gibeonite treaty went wrong because Israel "did not ask counsel from the Lord" (9:14).

## How it points to Christ

Joshua and Jesus share the same name, meaning "the Lord saves" (the Greek form of Joshua is Jesus). Hebrews says that if Joshua had given Israel real rest, God would not have spoken of another day. A Sabbath rest still remains for God's people, found in Christ (Heb 4:8–10).

Rahab, saved by faith and marked by a scarlet cord, appears in Jesus' genealogy and among the heroes of faith (Matt 1:5; Heb 11:31; Jas 2:25). The commander of the Lord's army (5:13–15) foreshadows the Lord himself fighting for his people. The conquest's judgment on sin and gift of an inheritance look ahead to Christ's final victory and the new creation, which the meek will inherit (Matt 5:5; Rev 21:1–4).

## Common mix-ups

- The conquest is not complete: much land remains (13:1), which sets up Judges.
- Moses' death is in Deuteronomy, not Joshua.
- Two different Ais are easy to mix up with Jericho: the walls fall at Jericho (6), and the defeat comes at Ai (7) because of Achan.
- The Gibeonites trick Israel; they aren't conquered. The sun stands still while Israel defends them (10).
- Caleb receives Hebron, not Joshua; Joshua's own town is Timnath-serah (19:50).
- "As for me and my house" is from Joshua 24:15, not Proverbs.

## Going deeper

- **The ethics of the conquest.** How can God command the destruction of the Canaanites? Timmer points out that:
    - God owns the land (1:3–4; 22:19).
    - The victory is his, not Israel's (24:12–13).
    - The judgment is for long-standing sin, like the flood.
    - Most Canaanite cities chose to fight.
    - Canaanites who turned to the Lord, like Rahab and the Gibeonites, were spared.
    - Israel itself faced the same judgment if unfaithful.

  Read: Timmer, The Ethical Problems Raised by the "Conquest".
- **Who belongs to Israel?** Timmer notes that ethnic lines are firm but covenant lines are porous: Rahab and the Gibeonites come in, while Achan, an Israelite, is cut off. How does this anticipate the gospel going to the nations?
- **Joshua and Judges in tension?** Joshua summarises sweeping victories, yet both books say much land remained. The Lutheran Study Bible discusses how critics set the two books against each other.
- **What kind of rest?** Compare rest in Joshua (21:44) with Psalm 95 and Hebrews 3–4. What rest did Joshua give, and what rest remains?
- **Keeping a deceptive oath.** Why did Israel honour a treaty obtained by fraud? Read Matthew Henry on Joshua 9, and see how God enforces the oath centuries later (2 Sam 21:1–14).

## Q&A

1. What did God tell Joshua to be, again and again? Strong and courageous (1:6–9).
2. Who hid the spies in Jericho? Rahab (2).
3. What sign marked Rahab's house for rescue? A scarlet cord in the window (2:18–21).
4. What happened when the priests stepped into the Jordan? The water stopped and stood in a heap, and Israel crossed on dry ground (3:15–17).
5. Why did Israel lose at Ai? Achan kept devoted things from Jericho (7).
6. How did the Gibeonites trick Israel? Worn-out clothes and mouldy bread, posing as people from a distant land (9).
7. What happened at Gibeon? The sun stood still until the battle was won (10:12–14).
8. How old was Caleb when he claimed Hebron? 85 (14:10).
9. Where was the tabernacle set up in the land? Shiloh (18:1).
10. What did Joshua say in his farewell? "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord" (24:15).
