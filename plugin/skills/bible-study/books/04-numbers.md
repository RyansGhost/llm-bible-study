# Numbers

## Summary

Numbers follows Israel from Mount Sinai to the edge of the promised land. The generation God rescued from Egypt refuses to trust him and dies in the wilderness over forty years; even Moses sins and may not enter. God stays faithful, though, and brings their children to the plains of Moab ready to enter Canaan. The book was likely written to warn that new generation to trust and obey where their parents had failed, and Paul uses it the same way for Christians (1 Cor 10:1–13).

## Setting

- **When:** from the second year after the exodus to the fortieth (about 1445–1406 BC in the Lutheran Study Bible's dating). Moses probably wrote it near the end of the wilderness years, before his death (Reformation Study Bible).
- **Where:** the wilderness of Sinai, Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin and Paran, the journey around Edom, and finally the plains of Moab across the Jordan from Jericho.
- **Who wrote it:** Moses, who is said to have recorded the journey (33:2). "The Lord spoke to Moses" appears more than forty times. A few notes may have been added after his death, such as the remark that Moses was the meekest man on earth (12:3) (RSB). Glodo argues it was written for the second generation on the plains of Moab, before Deuteronomy.
- **Where it sits:** the fourth book of Moses. Its Hebrew title, *Bemidbar*, "in the wilderness", names its setting; the English title comes from the Greek *Arithmoi*, "numbers", after the two censuses.

## Structure

Two censuses frame the book. One counts the generation that fails; the other counts the generation that will enter the land (Glodo, following Olson). The nested sections are the Reformation Study Bible's:

- 1–25: The end of the old generation
    - 1:1–10:10: preparing for the journey: the first census, the camp, the Levites, and laws for a holy people
    - 10:11–22:1: from Sinai to the plains of Moab: complaining, the spies, rebellion, Moses' sin at Meribah, forty years of wandering
    - 22:2–25:18: Balaam, hired to curse Israel, can only bless it; then the sin at Baal-peor
- 26–36: The birth of the new generation, preparing to enter Canaan: the second census, Joshua appointed, laws for life in the land, victory over Midian, the tribes east of the Jordan, the land's boundaries, and the cities of refuge

## Chapter by chapter

- **Numbers 1:** At Sinai, Moses counts every man able to go to war, tribe by tribe. The Levites are not counted, because they guard the tabernacle.
- **Numbers 2:** The tribes camp around the tabernacle in fixed order, with Judah leading on the east.
- **Numbers 3:** The Levites are taken in place of Israel's firstborn, and the extra firstborn are redeemed with money.
- **Numbers 4:** The Levite clans of Kohath, Gershon and Merari are given their duties carrying the tabernacle.
- **Numbers 5:** Unclean people are sent outside the camp. Laws on restitution and the test for a wife suspected of adultery.
- **Numbers 6:** The Nazirite vow, and the priestly blessing: "The Lord bless you and keep you" (6:24–26).
- **Numbers 7:** Over twelve days the tribal leaders bring gifts for the dedication of the altar.
- **Numbers 8:** The lampstand is set up, and the Levites are cleansed and presented to the Lord.
- **Numbers 9:** The second Passover, with a provision for those who were unclean. The cloud over the tabernacle tells Israel when to camp and when to move.
- **Numbers 10:** Silver trumpets signal the camp. Israel leaves Sinai in battle order, with Moses praying "Arise, O Lord" (10:35).
- **Numbers 11:** The people complain, and fire breaks out at Taberah. They crave meat. God puts his Spirit on seventy elders, then sends quail, with a plague.
- **Numbers 12:** Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses. God defends Moses, the humblest man on earth, and Miriam is struck with skin disease for seven days.
- **Numbers 13:** Twelve spies explore Canaan. Ten report giants and say Israel can't win; Caleb urges them to go up.
- **Numbers 14:** The people want to go back to Egypt. Moses pleads for them, but God decrees forty years in the wilderness until that generation dies, all except Caleb and Joshua. A presumptuous attack fails.
- **Numbers 15:** Laws on offerings for life in the land. A Sabbath-breaker is stoned, and tassels are given to help Israel remember the commands.
- **Numbers 16:** Korah, Dathan and Abiram rebel against Moses and Aaron. The earth swallows them, fire consumes their followers, and Aaron's incense stops a plague.
- **Numbers 17:** Aaron's staff buds, blossoms and bears almonds, confirming his priesthood.
- **Numbers 18:** The duties of priests and Levites, and their support from offerings and tithes.
- **Numbers 19:** The ashes of a red heifer are used to cleanse those who touch the dead.
- **Numbers 20:** Miriam dies at Kadesh. Moses strikes the rock instead of speaking to it, and is barred from entering the land. Edom refuses passage, and Aaron dies on Mount Hor.
- **Numbers 21:** Victory at Hormah. Venomous snakes bite the complaining people, and those who look at the bronze serpent live (21:8–9). Israel defeats Sihon and Og.
- **Numbers 22:** Balak, king of Moab, hires Balaam to curse Israel. Balaam's donkey sees the angel of the Lord and speaks.
- **Numbers 23:** Balaam tries to curse Israel but can only bless them: "God is not man, that he should lie" (23:19).
- **Numbers 24:** More blessings, including "a star shall come out of Jacob, and a sceptre shall rise out of Israel" (24:17).
- **Numbers 25:** Israel falls into sexual immorality and idol worship with Moab at Baal-peor. Phinehas' zeal stops the plague.
- **Numbers 26:** A second census, of the new generation. None of the first count remain except Caleb and Joshua.
- **Numbers 27:** Zelophehad's daughters win the right to inherit. Joshua is appointed to succeed Moses.
- **Numbers 28:** The daily, Sabbath, monthly and Passover offerings.
- **Numbers 29:** The offerings for the feasts of the seventh month.
- **Numbers 30:** Keeping vows, and when a father or husband may cancel a woman's vow.
- **Numbers 31:** War against Midian, in which Balaam is killed, and the division of the spoils.
- **Numbers 32:** Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh settle east of the Jordan, promising to fight alongside the other tribes.
- **Numbers 33:** The list of Israel's camps from Egypt to Moab, and a command to drive out Canaan's inhabitants and their idols.
- **Numbers 34:** The borders of the promised land, and the leaders who will divide it.
- **Numbers 35:** Forty-eight towns for the Levites, including six cities of refuge for someone who kills without intending to.
- **Numbers 36:** Daughters who inherit must marry within their tribe, so the land stays in the tribe.

## Key people

- **Moses:** leader, intercessor and lawgiver, "very meek" (12:3). He is barred from Canaan after striking the rock.
- **Aaron:** high priest, whose priesthood God confirms with his budding staff. He dies on Mount Hor.
- **Miriam:** prophetess and sister of Moses and Aaron. She is disciplined for opposing Moses and dies at Kadesh.
- **Caleb and Joshua:** the two faithful spies, the only adults of their generation allowed to enter the land.
- **Korah, Dathan and Abiram:** leaders of a rebellion against Moses and Aaron.
- **Balak:** king of Moab, who hires Balaam.
- **Balaam:** a foreign seer who can only speak the blessing God puts in his mouth, but who later leads Israel into sin (31:16).
- **Phinehas:** Aaron's grandson, whose zeal stops the plague at Peor.
- **Eleazar:** Aaron's son and successor as high priest.
- **The daughters of Zelophehad:** they ask Moses for their father's inheritance, and God grants it.

## Key events

- The first census, and the camp ordered around the tabernacle (1–4)
- The priestly blessing (6:22–27)
- Departure from Sinai (10)
- Complaining, quail, and the Spirit on the seventy elders (11)
- The twelve spies and Israel's refusal to enter Canaan (13–14)
- Korah's rebellion (16), and Aaron's budding staff (17)
- Moses strikes the rock (20)
- The bronze serpent (21)
- Balaam and his donkey; Balaam's blessings (22–24)
- Sin at Baal-peor (25)
- The second census of the new generation (26)
- Joshua appointed as Moses' successor (27)

## Themes

- **Unbelief and its cost:** the generation that saw the plagues and the sea refuses to trust God at Kadesh, and dies in the wilderness (14:11, 22–23).
- **God's faithfulness despite his people:** they grumble again and again, yet God keeps feeding, guiding and protecting them, and brings the next generation to the border.
- **Judged under the covenant:** the complaints about food and water echo Exodus, but now they are punished (compare Ex 16 with Num 11). Having sworn the covenant at Sinai, Israel is held to its terms (RSB).
- **Even the best fall short:** Moses himself is shut out of the land after Meribah (20:12), a reminder that all are saved by grace, not by their own record.
- **God's presence among his people:** the cloud over the tabernacle sets every move (9:15–23). The camp is ordered around God's dwelling, with the Levites guarding it.
- **Holy war and holiness:** Glodo shows the censuses are preparation for war, and the army must be holy because God marches with it (5:1–4; 10:35).
- **Intercession:** Moses and Aaron repeatedly stand between God's judgment and the people (14:13–19; 16:46–48).
- **God's blessing can't be overturned:** Balaam, hired to curse, can only bless (23:8, 19–20).

## How it points to Christ

Jesus says that as Moses lifted up the bronze serpent, the Son of Man must be lifted up, so that whoever believes in him may have eternal life (21:8–9; John 3:14–15). The rock that gave water was Christ (1 Cor 10:4), and the manna points to him as the bread from heaven (John 6:31–35). The star rising from Jacob in Balaam's prophecy (24:17) points to the Messiah-King, whose star the magi followed (Matt 2:2).

The ashes of the red heifer cleansed the body; Christ's blood cleanses the conscience (Heb 9:13–14). Where Israel failed forty years in the wilderness, Jesus stood firm through forty days of testing (Matt 4:1–11). Paul and Hebrews use Numbers as a warning against the unbelief that kept Israel out of God's rest (1 Cor 10:1–11; Heb 3:7–4:11).

## Common mix-ups

- The book is named for its censuses, but most of it is the wilderness story.
- Moses strikes the rock twice. In Exodus 17 he is told to strike it; in Numbers 20 he is told to speak to it, and striking it costs him entry to the land.
- Caleb and Joshua are the faithful spies; the other ten are not named in the stories.
- Korah's rebellion (Num 16) is different from the death of Nadab and Abihu (Lev 10).
- Balaam is not an Israelite prophet. He blesses Israel, but later leads them into sin at Peor (31:16; Rev 2:14).
- The forty years of wandering are a judgment imposed at Kadesh (Num 14), not the original plan.
- The priestly blessing "The Lord bless you and keep you" is in Numbers 6, not Leviticus.

## Going deeper

- **Why did God command censuses here but punish David's?** Glodo connects the Numbers censuses to holy war under God's command, and David's census (2 Sam 24) to a king trusting in his own numbers (Deut 17:14–20). Read: Glodo, Census of All Men of Fighting Age.
- **Are the census numbers too large?** The totals suggest about two million people. Some scholars reread the Hebrew word for "thousand" as "clan" or "unit". The Lutheran Study Bible points to God's miraculous provision instead. Weigh the options.
- **Why was Moses barred from the land?** Striking the rock instead of speaking to it was unbelief that failed to uphold God as holy (20:12). Why such a severe penalty for one act?
- **Balaam: prophet or false prophet?** He speaks true words from God, yet the New Testament remembers him for greed and for leading Israel into sin (2 Pet 2:15; Jude 11; Rev 2:14).
- **The Levites as guardians.** Glodo argues the Levites were priest-soldiers guarding the sanctuary against anyone approaching wrongly (1:50–53). How does that shape Korah's rebellion (16)?
- **A warning for the church.** Paul says these things happened "as examples for us" (1 Cor 10:6–11). Work through the grumbling stories with that lens.

## Q&A

1. **Q:** Why is the book called Numbers, and what is its Hebrew title?

   **A:** It is named for its two censuses of Israel's fighting men (1; 26); its Hebrew title means "In the wilderness" (1:1).

2. **Q:** What does the priestly blessing say?

   **A:** "The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make his face to shine upon you … and give you peace" (6:24–26).

3. **Q:** Which two spies trusted God?

   **A:** Caleb and Joshua (14:6–9).

4. **Q:** Why did Israel wander forty years?

   **A:** Because they refused to enter the land after the spies' report (14:33–34).

5. **Q:** What happened to Korah and his companions?

   **A:** The earth swallowed them, and fire consumed 250 of their followers (16:31–35).

6. **Q:** How did God confirm Aaron's priesthood?

   **A:** His staff budded, blossomed and produced almonds (17:8).

7. **Q:** Why could Moses not enter the promised land?

   **A:** He struck the rock in unbelief instead of speaking to it as God commanded (20:7–12).

8. **Q:** What healed those bitten by snakes?

   **A:** Looking at the bronze serpent Moses lifted up (21:8–9).

9. **Q:** Who spoke to Balaam besides God?

   **A:** His donkey (22:28).

10. **Q:** Who succeeded Moses as leader?

    **A:** Joshua (27:18–23).
