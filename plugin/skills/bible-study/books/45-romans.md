## Summary

Romans is Paul's fullest explanation of the gospel. Its theme is "the righteousness of God", revealed in the gospel "from faith for faith" (1:16–17). Paul shows that all people, Jew and Gentile, are sinners under God's judgment. He shows that God freely justifies sinners by grace through faith in Christ, who died as a sacrifice for sin. Those who are justified are united to Christ, set free from sin's rule, given the Spirit, and kept by God's love. Then he explains God's faithfulness to Israel, and how the gospel shapes everyday Christian life.

## Setting

- **When:** about AD 57, near the end of Paul's third missionary journey, before he had ever visited Rome (Waters; Acts 20:2–3).
- **Where:** written probably from Corinth, to the believers in Rome, a mixed community of Jewish and Gentile Christians meeting in house churches (16:5).
- **Who wrote it:** the apostle Paul, with Tertius as his scribe (16:22). Phoebe probably carried the letter (16:1–2).
- **Where it sits:** the first of Paul's letters in the New Testament, placed first because it is the longest. Waters notes Paul had several purposes: introducing himself and his gospel, preparing for a mission to Spain (15:24), and encouraging unity between Jewish and Gentile believers.

## Structure

Waters reads 1:1–7 and 1:16–17 as the keys to the letter, and divides the rest into four parts:

- 1:1–17: Introduction and thesis: the gospel reveals the righteousness of God
- 1:18–3:20: The need: all humanity, Gentile and Jew, lacks righteousness and stands under God's wrath
- 3:21–8:39: The gift: righteousness given through faith in Christ, leading to peace, union with Christ, freedom, and life in the Spirit
- 9–11: God's faithfulness to Israel: election, unbelief, and God's merciful plan for Jew and Gentile
- 12–16: The life of righteousness: worship, love, the state, the weak and the strong, Paul's plans, and greetings

## Chapter by chapter

- **Romans 1:** Paul greets the Romans with the gospel about God's Son. He is not ashamed of the gospel: it reveals God's righteousness, received by faith (1:16–17). God's wrath is revealed against people who suppress the truth, exchange God for idols, and are given over to their sins.
- **Romans 2:** Those who judge others condemn themselves. God judges everyone impartially. Having the law or circumcision doesn't save: true circumcision is of the heart.
- **Romans 3:** "None is righteous, no, not one" (3:10). "All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (3:23). But now God's righteousness has been revealed apart from the law. We are justified freely by his grace through the redemption in Christ, whom God put forward as a propitiation, received by faith (3:21–26).
- **Romans 4:** Abraham was counted righteous by faith before he was circumcised, so he is the father of all who believe, Jew and Gentile. David also speaks of the blessing of forgiveness.
- **Romans 5:** Justified by faith, we have peace with God and hope. God shows his love in that Christ died for us while we were still sinners (5:8). As sin and death came through Adam, righteousness and life come through Christ.
- **Romans 6:** Shall we sin so grace may increase? No: we died to sin with Christ in baptism and live a new life. "The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord" (6:23).
- **Romans 7:** We are freed from the law to belong to Christ. The law is holy, but sin uses it to bring death. "I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate" (7:15–25).
- **Romans 8:** There is no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus (8:1). The Spirit gives life and adoption ("Abba! Father!"). Creation groans for redemption. God works all things together for good for those who love him (8:28). Nothing can separate us from his love in Christ (8:38–39).
- **Romans 9:** Paul grieves for Israel. Not all descended from Israel belong to Israel. God's purpose in election depends on his mercy, not human will: Jacob and Esau, Pharaoh, the potter and the clay.
- **Romans 10:** Israel sought its own righteousness instead of God's. Christ is the end of the law for righteousness. If you confess Jesus as Lord and believe God raised him, you will be saved (10:9). How will they hear without a preacher?
- **Romans 11:** God hasn't rejected his people: there is a remnant chosen by grace. Gentiles are grafted into the olive tree. A partial hardening has come on Israel until the full number of Gentiles comes in, and so "all Israel will be saved". "Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God!" (11:33–36).
- **Romans 12:** Present your bodies as a living sacrifice and be transformed by renewing your mind (12:1–2). Use your gifts in the body, love genuinely, and overcome evil with good.
- **Romans 13:** Submit to governing authorities, as God's servants. Love fulfils the law. Put on the Lord Jesus Christ.
- **Romans 14:** The weak and the strong differ over food and days. Don't judge each other or cause a brother to stumble.
- **Romans 15:** Bear with the weak, as Christ welcomed you, so Jews and Gentiles may glorify God together. Paul's plans to visit Rome on his way to Spain, after delivering the collection to Jerusalem.
- **Romans 16:** Paul commends Phoebe, greets many co-workers including Priscilla and Aquila, warns against divisive people, and closes with praise.

## Key people

- **Paul:** apostle to the Gentiles, writing to a church he hadn't yet visited.
- **Phoebe:** a servant (deacon) of the church at Cenchreae, who likely carried the letter (16:1).
- **Priscilla and Aquila:** Paul's co-workers who risked their lives for him (16:3–4).
- **Tertius:** Paul's scribe (16:22).
- **Abraham:** the model of justification by faith (4).
- **Adam:** whose sin brought death to all, contrasted with Christ (5:12–21).
- **The Roman believers:** a mix of Jewish and Gentile Christians.

## Key events

- Paul's planned visit to Rome and Spain (1:10–15; 15:22–29)
- The collection for the poor in Jerusalem (15:25–27)

Romans is a letter of teaching rather than a narrative. Its "events" are redemptive history: Adam's fall, Abraham's faith, and Christ's death and resurrection.

## Themes

- **The righteousness of God:** Waters sees this as the main point of the letter (1:17). God's own righteousness, shown in judging sin and justifying sinners, and given as a gift to believers.
- **Universal sin:** Jew and Gentile alike are guilty before God (1:18–3:20).
- **Justification by faith:** God declares sinners righteous by grace through faith in Christ, apart from works of the law (3:21–4:25). The Reformers' rallying point; see the Westminster Confession, chapter 11.
- **Union with Christ:** believers died and rose with Christ and live a new life (6).
- **Life in the Spirit:** the Spirit frees, leads, adopts, helps and intercedes (8).
- **Assurance:** no condemnation, and no separation from God's love (8:1, 31–39).
- **God's sovereign election:** salvation rests on God's mercy and purpose (8:29–30; 9). See the Westminster Confession, chapter 3.
- **God's faithfulness to Israel:** God hasn't abandoned his promises (11).
- **Gospel-shaped living:** grace leads to worship, humility, love, submission and unity (12–15).

## How it points to Christ

Romans is centred on Christ:

- He is the Son of God, descended from David and declared Son of God in power by his resurrection (1:3–4).
- He is the propitiation for sin, through whom God is both just and the justifier of believers (3:25–26).
- He is the second Adam, whose obedience makes many righteous (5:19).
- He died and rose so that we die to sin and live to God (6).
- He is the end of the law for righteousness to all who believe (10:4).
- He is the Lord on whom all may call (10:12–13).
- He intercedes for us at God's right hand, and nothing can separate us from God's love in him (8:34, 39).

## Common mix-ups

- Paul wrote Romans before he had visited Rome. He arrived later as a prisoner (Acts 28).
- Romans 1:17 quotes Habakkuk 2:4, "the righteous shall live by faith".
- "All have sinned" (3:23) and "the wages of sin is death" (6:23) are from Romans, not from the Gospels.
- Romans 7:14–25 is debated: Paul as a believer struggling with sin (the classic Reformed and Lutheran view), or Paul describing life under the law before conversion.
- "All things work together for good" (8:28) is for "those who love God … called according to his purpose". It isn't a promise that everything will feel good.
- "All Israel will be saved" (11:26) has several interpretations: the whole elect people of God, Jew and Gentile; a future large-scale conversion of ethnic Israel; or the full number of elect Jews through history.

## Going deeper

- **The righteousness of God.** Is it God's own attribute, the status he gives believers, or his saving action? Waters makes it the key to the letter. Read: Waters, Romans 1:16–17.
- **Justification and imputation.** How does Romans 3–5 support the Reformation teaching that Christ's righteousness is credited to believers by faith alone (WCF 11.1)? Compare Luther's Preface to Romans.
- **The struggle of Romans 7.** Is "the wretched man" a Christian? What is at stake for the Christian life?
- **Election in Romans 9.** How do Reformed confessions read Paul on God's sovereign choice (WCF 3)? How does chapter 10 keep human responsibility and the call to preach in view?
- **Israel's future (11).** What do the olive tree and "all Israel will be saved" teach? How should Gentile believers think of Jewish people?
- **The strong and the weak (14–15).** What principles guide Christians who disagree on matters of conscience?

## Q&A

1. To whom did Paul write Romans? To all the believers in Rome, "loved by God and called to be saints" (1:7).
2. What is Paul not ashamed of? The gospel, the power of God for salvation (1:16).
3. What is revealed in the gospel, according to 1:17? The righteousness of God, from faith for faith.
4. What does 3:23 say about everyone? All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.
5. How are sinners justified, according to 3:24? Freely by God's grace, through the redemption in Christ Jesus.
6. Whom does Paul use as the example of justification by faith? Abraham (4).
7. How did God show his love, according to 5:8? Christ died for us while we were still sinners.
8. What are the wages of sin? Death; but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ (6:23).
9. What is there "now" for those in Christ Jesus? No condemnation (8:1).
10. What can separate us from the love of God in Christ? Nothing in all creation (8:38–39).
