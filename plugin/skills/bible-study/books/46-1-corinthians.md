# 1 Corinthians

## Summary

1 Corinthians is Paul's pastoral response to a gifted but troubled young church: divisions, pride, sexual immorality, lawsuits, disputes over food offered to idols, disorder in worship, and doubts about the resurrection. Paul answers each problem by bringing the gospel to bear: Christ crucified, the wisdom and power of God; the church as God's temple and Christ's body; love as the greatest gift; and Christ's resurrection as the guarantee of ours. Calvin called it a letter "not less obscure than useful" (quoted in Waters).

## Setting

- **When:** about AD 53–55, from Ephesus during Paul's third journey (16:8). Waters dates Paul's founding visit to about AD 49–51, using the inscription that dates Gallio's office (Acts 18:12–17).
- **Where:** Corinth, a wealthy, cosmopolitan port city in Achaia (southern Greece), known for commerce, competition and immorality.
- **Who wrote it:** Paul, with Sosthenes (1:1).
- **Where it sits:** Paul had founded the church during 18 months there (Acts 18:1–18). This letter answers reports from "Chloe's people" (1:11) and a letter the Corinthians sent him (7:1). Paul had written an earlier letter that is now lost (5:9).

## Structure

Waters' outline:

- 1:1–9: Greeting and thanksgiving
- 1:10–4:21: Divisions and factions: the wisdom of the world against the word of the cross
- 5–7: Sexual integrity: immorality, lawsuits, the body, marriage and singleness
- 8:1–11:1: Food offered to idols: knowledge, love, freedom and the weaker brother
- 11:2–14:40: Public worship: head coverings, the Lord's Supper, spiritual gifts and love
- 15: The bodily resurrection
- 16: The collection, travel plans and greetings

## Chapter by chapter

- **1 Corinthians 1:** Paul thanks God for the Corinthians' gifts, then confronts their factions ("I follow Paul … Apollos … Cephas … Christ"). The word of the cross is folly to the world but God's power. God chose the foolish and weak so no one may boast except in the Lord.
- **1 Corinthians 2:** Paul came in weakness, preaching only Jesus Christ and him crucified. God's hidden wisdom is revealed by his Spirit.
- **1 Corinthians 3:** Their jealousy shows they are infants. Paul planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. The only foundation is Christ, each worker's work will be tested by fire, and the church is God's temple.
- **1 Corinthians 4:** Apostles are servants and stewards; don't judge before the Lord comes. The apostles are treated as the world's scum. Paul writes as their father.
- **1 Corinthians 5:** A man is living with his father's wife, and the church is proud. Remove him, and clean out the old leaven: "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (5:7).
- **1 Corinthians 6:** Believers are suing each other before unbelievers. The unrighteous won't inherit the kingdom, "and such were some of you. But you were washed" (6:11). Your body is a temple of the Spirit. Flee sexual immorality, for you were bought with a price.
- **1 Corinthians 7:** Marriage, singleness, divorce, mixed marriages, and remaining in the situation one was called in.
- **1 Corinthians 8:** Food offered to idols. Knowledge puffs up but love builds up. Don't let your freedom make a weaker brother stumble.
- **1 Corinthians 9:** Paul gives up his rights as an apostle for the gospel's sake, becoming "all things to all people". Run so as to win.
- **1 Corinthians 10:** Israel's wilderness failures are warnings. God provides a way out of every temptation (10:13). Flee idolatry: you can't share both the Lord's table and the table of demons. Do everything to the glory of God (10:31).
- **1 Corinthians 11:** Head coverings in worship. The Corinthians abuse the Lord's Supper, with the rich shaming the poor. Paul passes on Jesus' words of institution: examine yourselves.
- **1 Corinthians 12:** There are many spiritual gifts but one Spirit. The church is one body with many members, each needed.
- **1 Corinthians 13:** Without love, gifts are nothing. Love is patient and kind. Faith, hope and love abide, but the greatest is love.
- **1 Corinthians 14:** Prophecy builds up the church more than uninterpreted tongues. "All things should be done decently and in order" (14:40).
- **1 Corinthians 15:** The gospel of first importance: Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised, and appeared to many (15:3–8). If Christ isn't raised, faith is futile, but he is the firstfruits. The resurrection body. "Death is swallowed up in victory" (15:54).
- **1 Corinthians 16:** The collection for Jerusalem, travel plans, and commendations. "Stand firm in the faith". "Our Lord, come!" (*Maranatha*).

## Key people

- **Paul:** the church's founder and spiritual father.
- **Sosthenes:** co-sender, possibly the synagogue ruler of Acts 18:17.
- **Apollos:** an eloquent teacher whom some Corinthians preferred.
- **Cephas (Peter):** another name some rallied around.
- **Chloe's people:** who reported the divisions (1:11).
- **Timothy:** sent to remind them of Paul's ways (4:17; 16:10).
- **Stephanas, Fortunatus and Achaicus:** Corinthian believers who visited Paul (16:17).
- **Priscilla and Aquila:** who host a church in their house (16:19).

## Key events

- The founding of the church in Corinth (Acts 18)
- Reports of divisions and immorality (1:11; 5:1)
- The Lord's Supper as Jesus instituted it (11:23–26)
- The resurrection appearances of Christ (15:3–8)
- The collection for the Jerusalem church (16:1–4)

## Themes

- **The cross against worldly wisdom:** Christ crucified looks foolish and weak, but it is God's wisdom and power (1:18–25). Waters shows Paul bringing the cross to bear on the church's divisions.
- **This age and the age to come:** Waters highlights Paul's framework. Believers already belong to the new age in Christ, but still live in the present age and must not be shaped by it.
- **Unity in the church:** one foundation, one temple, one body, one Spirit (3:11, 16; 12:12–13).
- **Holiness of the body:** the believer's body belongs to the Lord and is the Spirit's temple (6:12–20).
- **Christian freedom limited by love:** don't wound a weaker brother's conscience (8; 10:23–33).
- **Worship that builds up:** gifts are for the common good, exercised in love and order (12–14).
- **Love:** the more excellent way (13).
- **The resurrection:** the heart of the gospel and the hope of believers (15).

## How it points to Christ

Christ crucified is the centre of the letter (2:2). He is the power and wisdom of God, made our wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption (1:24, 30). He is the only foundation of the church (3:11), our Passover lamb (5:7), the spiritual rock that followed Israel (10:4), and the head of the body. His body and blood are proclaimed at the Lord's Supper "until he comes" (11:26). As the "last Adam" (15:45), his resurrection is the firstfruits that guarantees the resurrection of all who belong to him (15:20–23).

## Common mix-ups

- The "love chapter" is 1 Corinthians 13, often read at weddings but written to correct a proud, divided church.
- The great resurrection chapter is 1 Corinthians 15, not 2 Corinthians.
- Paul wrote at least four letters to Corinth. 1 Corinthians mentions an earlier one (5:9), and 2 Corinthians mentions a "tearful" letter (2 Cor 2:4).
- "No temptation has overtaken you that is not common to man" is 1 Corinthians 10:13.
- Corinth was in Greece (Achaia), not Asia Minor.
- "Cephas" is Peter's Aramaic name.

## Going deeper

- **Wisdom and foolishness (1–4).** Why does Paul refuse impressive rhetoric? What does "Christ crucified" say about the church's values today? Read: Waters, The Death of Christ.
- **Living between the ages.** Waters frames the letter in terms of "this age" and "the age to come". How does that explain Paul's teaching on marriage (7:29–31), food (8–10) and the resurrection (15)?
- **Food offered to idols (8–10).** How do knowledge, love and conscience fit together? What modern issues follow similar principles?
- **Spiritual gifts (12–14).** What are the gifts for? How do Reformed churches understand the gifts of tongues and prophecy today?
- **The Lord's Supper (11:17–34).** What does it mean to eat "in an unworthy manner" and to "discern the body"?
- **The resurrection body (15).** What continuity and difference does Paul describe between our present bodies and resurrection bodies?

## Q&A

1. What problem does Paul address first? Divisions and factions in the church (1:10–4:21).
2. What did Paul resolve to know among the Corinthians? Jesus Christ and him crucified (2:2).
3. Who planted and who watered, according to 3:6? Paul planted and Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.
4. What does Paul call Christ in 5:7? Our Passover lamb.
5. Why should believers flee sexual immorality? Because the body is a temple of the Holy Spirit and they were bought with a price (6:18–20).
6. What does God provide with every temptation? A way of escape (10:13).
7. What does Paul say we should do in everything? Do it all to the glory of God (10:31).
8. What is the greatest of faith, hope and love? Love (13:13).
9. What is "of first importance" in the gospel? That Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared (15:3–5).
10. What does Paul say about death in 15:54–55? Death is swallowed up in victory; "O death, where is your sting?"
