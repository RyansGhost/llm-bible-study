# Ecclesiastes

## Summary

Ecclesiastes records the search of "the Preacher" (Qohelet) for lasting meaning in life "under the sun". He tests pleasure, work, wealth and wisdom, and finds them all *hebel*: breath, vapour, fleeting and unable to satisfy. Death levels the wise and the fool. Yet he keeps returning to the simple gifts of God: food, work and love, enjoyed now. The book's final word sets it all in God's light: "Fear God and keep his commandments", for God will judge every deed (12:13–14).

## Setting

- **When:** traditionally Solomon's reign (10th century BC). Belcher notes that most scholars now date the writing much later, after the exile, partly because its Hebrew has features of late biblical Hebrew.
- **Where:** Jerusalem, from the viewpoint of a king who has had every resource to test life (1:12–2:11).
- **Who wrote it:** the speaker calls himself Qohelet, "son of David, king in Jerusalem" (1:1). Jewish and Christian tradition identified him with Solomon. Belcher thinks Solomon is not the author, and that the book uses a Solomon-like persona.
- **Where it sits:** a wisdom book that tackles the hard cases Proverbs doesn't. In Jewish tradition it is one of the five festival scrolls, read at the Feast of Booths.

## Structure

Belcher shows the book's frame: a narrator speaks about Qohelet in the third person at the start and end, around Qohelet's own first-person words.

- 1:1–11: Prologue (third person): title, motto ("vanity of vanities"), key question ("what gain?"), and a poem on the endless cycles of nature
- 1:12–12:7: Qohelet's search (first person): experiments with pleasure and work, reflections on time, injustice, wealth, wisdom and death, with recurring calls to enjoy God's gifts
- 12:8–14: Epilogue (third person): an evaluation of Qohelet's words, and the conclusion: fear God and keep his commandments

## Chapter by chapter

- **Ecclesiastes 1:** "Vanity of vanities! All is vanity" (1:2). What do people gain from all their toil? Nature goes round in circles, there is nothing new under the sun, and Qohelet finds that seeking wisdom is chasing the wind.
- **Ecclesiastes 2:** He tries pleasure, laughter, wine, great building projects, wealth and music, and finds no lasting gain. The wise and the fool both die. Yet to eat, drink and enjoy one's work is a gift from God's hand (2:24–25).
- **Ecclesiastes 3:** "For everything there is a season" (3:1–8). God has made everything beautiful in its time and put eternity into human hearts, though we can't grasp his work (3:11). People and animals both die.
- **Ecclesiastes 4:** Oppression with no comforter. Work driven by envy. "Two are better than one", and a threefold cord isn't quickly broken (4:9–12). Even a wise young king is soon forgotten.
- **Ecclesiastes 5:** Approach God's house carefully and keep your vows. Lovers of money are never satisfied (5:10). Being able to enjoy wealth and work is God's gift.
- **Ecclesiastes 6:** The sorrow of having wealth but no ability to enjoy it. The appetite is never filled.
- **Ecclesiastes 7:** "Better" sayings: a good name, the house of mourning, patience. No one on earth is righteous and never sins (7:20).
- **Ecclesiastes 8:** Obey the king. Justice is often delayed and the wicked prosper, yet it will be well with those who fear God (8:12–13). No one can fathom God's work.
- **Ecclesiastes 9:** The same end comes to all. So enjoy your food and life with the wife you love, and whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might (9:7–10). Time and chance happen to all. Wisdom is better than strength, but often ignored.
- **Ecclesiastes 10:** Proverbs on folly: a little folly outweighs much wisdom, fools are put in high places, and the words of the wise are gracious.
- **Ecclesiastes 11:** "Cast your bread upon the waters" (11:1). Act diligently despite uncertainty. Rejoice in your youth, but remember God will bring you into judgment.
- **Ecclesiastes 12:** "Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth", before old age and death (12:1–7). The epilogue: the words of the wise come from one Shepherd. "Fear God and keep his commandments", for God will judge every deed (12:13–14).

## Key people

- **Qohelet ("the Preacher" or "the Teacher"):** the speaker, a king in Jerusalem and son of David, who searches for meaning under the sun.
- **The narrator:** the voice of the prologue and epilogue, who presents and evaluates Qohelet's words (1:1–2; 12:8–14).
- **"One Shepherd":** the ultimate source of the wise sayings (12:11), understood as God.

## Key events

Ecclesiastes isn't a narrative. Its "events" are Qohelet's experiments:

- The search through wisdom (1:12–18)
- The search through pleasure, building and wealth (2:1–11)
- Reflection on time and seasons (3)
- Observations of injustice and oppression (3:16; 4:1–3)
- The poem of old age and death (12:1–7)
- The final verdict of the epilogue (12:13–14)

## Themes

- ***Hebel*, "vanity":** the book's key word, literally "breath" or "vapour". Belcher shows it can mean what is fleeting, but in Ecclesiastes often what lacks lasting significance.
- **Life "under the sun":** Qohelet looks at life from the human vantage point. Belcher contrasts this with Proverbs: the sages start from God's revelation, while Qohelet reasons from what he himself observes, and keeps hitting limits.
- **Death the leveller:** wise and foolish, human and animal, all die (2:14–16; 3:19–20; 9:2–3).
- **Enjoy God's gifts:** again and again Qohelet commends eating, drinking, work and marriage as gifts to receive from God (2:24–26; 3:12–13; 5:18–20; 9:7–10).
- **God's sovereignty and mystery:** God has a time for everything, but we can't see the whole (3:11; 8:17).
- **Fear God and remember judgment:** the epilogue gives the answer Qohelet's search couldn't reach on its own (12:13–14). Belcher argues the epilogue explains the problems of life in light of who God is.

## How it points to Christ

Ecclesiastes honestly faces a world "subjected to futility" (Rom 8:20, the same idea as *hebel* in the Greek Old Testament) and ruled by death. It leaves us longing for something that isn't vapour. Christ's resurrection answers that longing: because he lives, "your labour is not in vain in the Lord" (1 Cor 15:58). What looked like endless cycles under the sun now has a goal.

The "one Shepherd" who gives true wisdom (12:11) points to Christ, the Good Shepherd and the wisdom of God (John 10:11; 1 Cor 1:24). The final judgment of every hidden deed (12:14) will be carried out by the risen Christ (Acts 17:31; Rom 2:16).

## Common mix-ups

- "Vanity" here doesn't mean pride. It translates *hebel*: breath, vapour, something fleeting or empty.
- Ecclesiastes is not a book of hopeless nihilism. It honestly describes life without God's perspective, then gives God's verdict in the epilogue.
- "A time to be born, and a time to die" is Ecclesiastes 3, not Proverbs or Psalms.
- Qohelet is a title, not a personal name: "one who gathers an assembly", hence "Preacher" or "Teacher".
- Whether Solomon wrote it is debated. The book never names Solomon.
- "Eat, drink and be merry" in Ecclesiastes means receiving God's gifts with gratitude, not the godless "tomorrow we die" of 1 Corinthians 15:32.

## Going deeper

- **How should we read Qohelet?** Belcher outlines three approaches:
    - **Heterodox:** Qohelet rejected traditional wisdom, and later editors tried to correct him.
    - **Orthodox:** Qohelet fully agrees with Proverbs.
    - **Struggling:** the tensions are honest and left unresolved.

  Which fits 8:11–15 best? Read: Belcher, Different Approaches to Ecclesiastes.
- **What does *hebel* mean?** "Breath", "fleeting", "meaningless", "enigmatic"? Belcher discusses the options. Try each in 1:2, 2:11 and 11:10.
- **How does Qohelet know?** Belcher contrasts Qohelet's reasoning from personal observation with Proverbs' starting point in revelation. What can experience teach without God's word, and where does it run out?
- **The calls to enjoyment.** Are they resigned ("make the best of it") or genuine faith in God's gifts? Belcher surveys the debate.
- **The epilogue's answer.** Why does the book end with commandments and judgment (12:13–14)? How does that change everything before it?
- **Eternity in the heart (3:11).** What does it mean that God has put eternity into human hearts? Compare Augustine: "our heart is restless until it rests in you."

## Q&A

1. What is the motto of Ecclesiastes? "Vanity of vanities! All is vanity" (1:2; 12:8).
2. What does the Hebrew word translated "vanity" literally mean? Breath, or vapour (*hebel*).
3. What does "Qohelet" mean? One who gathers an assembly: "the Preacher" or "the Teacher".
4. What did Qohelet test in chapter 2? Pleasure, wine, great works, wealth and music.
5. Which chapter says there is a time for everything? Chapter 3.
6. What has God put into human hearts? Eternity (3:11).
7. Why are two better than one? If one falls, the other can lift him up; a threefold cord isn't quickly broken (4:9–12).
8. What does Qohelet repeatedly commend as God's gift? Eating, drinking and enjoying one's work and life (2:24; 3:13; 5:18; 9:7).
9. What should we remember in our youth? Our Creator (12:1).
10. What is the conclusion of the book? Fear God and keep his commandments, for God will bring every deed into judgment (12:13–14).
