# John

## Summary

John tells the story of Jesus from a distinctive angle. It begins before creation: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (1:1), and the Word became flesh. John selects seven signs and long conversations that reveal who Jesus is, including the seven "I am" sayings. He then slows down for Jesus' last night with his disciples, his death, and his resurrection. He writes so that readers "may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:31).

## Setting

- **When:** Jesus' ministry, which John shows spanning at least three Passovers (2:13; 6:4; 11:55). Kruger dates the writing late in the first century, after the temple's destruction in AD 70, consistent with early church tradition that John wrote near the end of his long life, about AD 90 (Reformation Study Bible). A papyrus fragment of John 18 from about AD 125 (the Rylands papyrus) has ended theories of a second-century date; some date it before AD 80 or even 70.
- **Where:** Judea and Jerusalem (especially at the feasts), Galilee (Cana, Capernaum, the Sea of Tiberias), Samaria, and Bethany.
- **Who wrote it:** "the disciple whom Jesus loved", who bears witness and wrote these things (21:20–24): a Jew who knew Palestine and its customs, and an eyewitness (19:35). He was at the Last Supper, so one of the Twelve, yet he isn't Peter or any disciple the Gospel names, and John son of Zebedee, one of Jesus' closest three, is never named in it. Irenaeus, who learned from Polycarp, says John wrote it (RSB). Kruger defends this traditional view.
- **Where it sits:** the fourth Gospel. It tells the same basic story as the first three, with a different style, many unique events (the man born blind, the raising of Lazarus) and long discourses (Kruger). Kruger argues its differences don't make it less historical. Clement of Alexandria called it a "spiritual Gospel", written knowing the others. About 40% of it covers Jesus' last week, mostly his private teaching of the disciples (13–17). It was written to bring readers to faith (20:31) and answers both Jewish objections to Jesus' deity and early ideas that God couldn't truly take on flesh (1:14).

## Structure

The widely accepted four-part outline (Kruger; the Reformation Study Bible calls the second half the book of the great sign, Jesus "lifted up"):

- 1:1–18: Prologue: the Word who was God becomes flesh
- 1:19–12:50: The Book of Signs: seven signs and Jesus' public teaching
- 13:1–20:31: The Book of the Passion (or of Glory): the upper room, the farewell discourse and prayer, the arrest, the cross and the resurrection
- 21: Epilogue: the risen Jesus by the sea, and Peter restored

## Chapter by chapter

- **John 1:** The Word, who was with God and was God, became flesh and dwelt among us. John the Baptist points to "the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world". Andrew, Peter, Philip and Nathanael follow Jesus.
- **John 2:** At a wedding in Cana, Jesus turns water into wine, his first sign. He clears the temple: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up".
- **John 3:** Jesus tells Nicodemus he must be born again of water and the Spirit. "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son" (3:16). John the Baptist: "He must increase, but I must decrease".
- **John 4:** At a well, Jesus offers a Samaritan woman living water and speaks of worship in spirit and truth. Many Samaritans believe. He heals an official's son, the second sign.
- **John 5:** He heals a man at the pool of Bethesda on the Sabbath, claims to work as his Father works, and calls witnesses to himself.
- **John 6:** He feeds five thousand and walks on the sea. "I am the bread of life". Many disciples leave, but Peter says, "You have the words of eternal life".
- **John 7:** At the Feast of Booths the crowds are divided. Jesus cries out, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink", promising rivers of living water (the Spirit).
- **John 8:** (A woman caught in adultery is forgiven, 7:53–8:11, a passage missing from early manuscripts.) "I am the light of the world". The truth will set you free. "Before Abraham was, I am" (8:58).
- **John 9:** Jesus heals a man born blind. The Pharisees interrogate him, and he worships Jesus while the "seeing" leaders remain blind.
- **John 10:** "I am the door" and "I am the good shepherd" who lays down his life for the sheep. "I and the Father are one", and they try to stone him.
- **John 11:** Lazarus dies. "I am the resurrection and the life". Jesus weeps, then raises Lazarus. The leaders plot to kill Jesus, and Caiaphas unknowingly prophesies that one man should die for the people.
- **John 12:** Mary anoints Jesus at Bethany, and Jesus enters Jerusalem. Greeks seek him. A grain of wheat must die to bear fruit. Unbelief fulfils Isaiah.
- **John 13:** Jesus washes his disciples' feet. Judas goes out into the night. "A new commandment … love one another".
- **John 14:** "I am the way, and the truth, and the life". Jesus is going to prepare a place in his Father's house, and promises the Helper, the Holy Spirit.
- **John 15:** "I am the true vine": abide in me. The world will hate you, and the Spirit will testify.
- **John 16:** The Spirit will convict the world and guide into truth. Sorrow will turn to joy. "I have overcome the world".
- **John 17:** Jesus prays to the Father: for his own glorification, for his disciples, and for all future believers to be one.
- **John 18:** Jesus is arrested in a garden, and Peter cuts off a servant's ear. Questioning before Annas and Caiaphas, Peter's denials, and Pilate's "What is truth?"
- **John 19:** Jesus is flogged, and Pilate says "Behold the man!" He is crucified. "It is finished". His side is pierced, and Joseph and Nicodemus bury him.
- **John 20:** Mary Magdalene finds the tomb empty and meets the risen Jesus. He appears to the disciples and breathes the Spirit on them. Thomas confesses, "My Lord and my God!" The purpose of the book (20:30–31).
- **John 21:** By the Sea of Tiberias, a great catch of 153 fish. Jesus restores Peter three times: "Feed my sheep". The beloved disciple's testimony is true.

## Key people

- **Jesus:** the Word made flesh, the Son of God, the great "I am".
- **John the Baptist:** a witness who points to the Lamb of God.
- **The beloved disciple:** the eyewitness behind the Gospel.
- **Peter:** who denies Jesus three times and is restored three times.
- **Andrew, Philip and Nathanael:** early disciples.
- **Nicodemus:** a Pharisee who comes by night and later helps bury Jesus.
- **The Samaritan woman:** who meets Jesus at Jacob's well.
- **Mary, Martha and Lazarus:** Jesus' friends in Bethany.
- **Thomas:** the doubter who makes the highest confession.
- **Judas Iscariot:** the betrayer.
- **Caiaphas and Annas:** high priests.
- **Pilate:** the Roman governor.
- **Mary Magdalene:** the first to see the risen Lord.

## Themes

- **Jesus is God:** John identifies Jesus with the one God of Israel as Creator (1:1–3), as "I am" (8:58), and as "my Lord and my God" (20:28). Kruger places this at the heart of John's Christology.
- **Life through believing:** Jesus brings new life (1:4; 3:16; 10:10), and "believe" is one of John's key words (20:31).
- **Contrasts:** light and darkness, life and death, truth and lies, above and below. Kruger shows John presenting salvation through these contrasts.
- **Signs:** seven miracles reveal Jesus' glory and call for faith (2:11; 20:30–31). Signs and teaching interpret each other: the feeding of the five thousand leads to "I am the bread of life" (6), the healing of the blind man to "the light of the world" (9). Faith must grasp who the signs show Jesus to be, and the written record is enough for those who haven't seen (20:29) (RSB).
- **"I am" sayings:** bread of life (6:35), light of the world (8:12), door (10:9), good shepherd (10:11), resurrection and life (11:25), way, truth and life (14:6), true vine (15:1).
- **Fulfilment of the Old Testament:** Jesus is the true temple, tabernacle, manna, Passover Lamb and bronze serpent, and the substance behind Israel's feasts (Kruger).
- **God's sovereign grace:** no one can come unless the Father draws him (6:44). Those the Father gives the Son are kept by him, and none will be lost (6:37–39; 10:27–29). New birth is God's work by his Spirit, not ours (1:12–13; 3:5–8).
- **The Holy Spirit:** the Helper who teaches, testifies and convicts, sent by the Father and the Son (14–16; 20:22).
- **Love and unity:** "love one another as I have loved you" (13:34–35; 17:21).

## How it points to Christ

John is the Gospel most explicitly about who Christ is. He is the eternal Word who made all things and "became flesh and dwelt among us" (1:14, literally "tabernacled"), the fulfilment of the tabernacle where God's glory lived. He is the Lamb of God (1:29), the true temple raised in three days (2:19–21), the one lifted up like the bronze serpent so that whoever believes may live (3:14–15), the true bread from heaven (6:32–35), and the good shepherd who lays down his life (10:11).

On the cross he declares, "It is finished" (19:30). After rising he breathes the Spirit on his disciples and sends them as the Father sent him (20:21–22).

## Common mix-ups

- The author is John the apostle, son of Zebedee, not John the Baptist.
- John places a temple cleansing early in Jesus' ministry (2). The other Gospels place one in the last week. Many think there were two.
- The woman caught in adultery (7:53–8:11) isn't in the earliest manuscripts, and most Bibles mark it.
- John records at least three Passovers, which is why Jesus' ministry is usually reckoned about three years.
- John has no parables in the Synoptic style, no account of the Lord's Supper itself (it has the foot washing instead), and no transfiguration.
- "For God so loved the world" (3:16) may be Jesus' words or John's comment. The quotation marks are an editorial decision.

## Going deeper

- **The prologue (1:1–18).** How does "in the beginning" echo Genesis 1? What do "Word", "life", "light" and "dwelt" teach about Christ? Read: Kruger, Christ as the One and Only God.
- **Is John historical?** Kruger addresses the charge that John is theology rather than history, given its differences from the Synoptics. What answers does he give?
- **The seven signs.** List them (2:1–11; 4:46–54; 5:1–9; 6:1–14; 6:16–21; 9:1–7; 11:1–44). How does each reveal Jesus' glory?
- **The "I am" sayings.** Connect them to God's name in Exodus 3:14, and to Isaiah 43:10–13.
- **Jesus and Israel's feasts.** Kruger shows John presenting Jesus as fulfilling Passover, Booths and Dedication (chs 2; 6; 7–8; 10). How does that shape the meaning of each?
- **The farewell discourse (13–17).** What does Jesus teach about the Spirit, prayer, abiding and love, and why on his last night?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** How does John's Gospel begin?

   **A:** "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (1:1).

2. **Q:** What did John the Baptist call Jesus?

   **A:** "The Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" (1:29).

3. **Q:** What was Jesus' first sign?

   **A:** Turning water into wine at Cana (2:1–11).

4. **Q:** What did Jesus tell Nicodemus?

   **A:** That he must be born again, of water and the Spirit (3:3–5).

5. **Q:** What are Jesus' seven "I am" sayings?

   **A:** The bread of life (6:35), the light of the world (8:12), the door (10:9), the good shepherd (10:11), the resurrection and the life (11:25), the way, the truth and the life (14:6), and the true vine (15:1).

6. **Q:** Whom did Jesus raise after four days in the tomb?

   **A:** Lazarus (11:38–44).

7. **Q:** What did Jesus do for his disciples at the Last Supper?

   **A:** Washed their feet, as an example of humble love (13:1–15).

8. **Q:** What were Jesus' last words on the cross in John?

   **A:** "It is finished" (19:30).

9. **Q:** What did Thomas say when he saw the risen Jesus?

   **A:** "My Lord and my God!" (20:28).

10. **Q:** Why did John write his Gospel?

    **A:** So that readers may believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name (20:31).
