# Exodus

## Summary

Exodus is the story of God keeping his promise to Abraham (Gen 15:13–14). He rescues Abraham's enslaved descendants from Egypt by mighty acts of judgment and brings them to Mount Sinai. There he binds them to himself by covenant, gives them his law, and comes to live among them in the tabernacle.

## Setting

- **When:** after more than four hundred years in Egypt. The traditional date for the exodus is about 1446 BC (from 1 Kgs 6:1); many critical scholars prefer about 1260 BC (Lutheran Study Bible, Challenges for Readers).
- **Where:** Egypt (Goshen), Midian, the Red Sea, the wilderness, and Mount Sinai (also called Horeb).
- **Who wrote it:** Moses, by Jewish and Christian tradition (Lutheran Study Bible). Currid shows the author knew Egyptian life and religion closely, as someone raised in Pharaoh's court would.
- **Where it sits:** the second book of Moses. It picks up where Genesis ends, "in Egypt", and its Hebrew title comes from its opening words, "These are the names".

## Structure

Currid describes Exodus as a "diptych", two panels hinged together. In Egypt Israel served Pharaoh; at Sinai they become the Lord's servants.

- 1–18: Israel in Egypt and the road to Sinai (mostly narrative)
    - 1–2: slavery, and Moses' birth and flight
    - 3–4: the burning bush and Moses' call
    - 5–11: confrontation with Pharaoh and the ten plagues
    - 12–15: Passover, the exodus, and the crossing of the sea
    - 16–18: the wilderness: manna, water, Amalek, Jethro
- 19–40: Israel at Sinai (mostly law)
    - 19–24: the covenant, the Ten Commandments, and the Book of the Covenant
    - 25–31: instructions for the tabernacle and the priesthood
    - 32–34: the golden calf, Moses' intercession, and the covenant renewed
    - 35–40: the tabernacle built and filled with God's glory

## Chapter by chapter

- **Exodus 1:** Israel multiplies in Egypt. A new Pharaoh enslaves them and orders the Hebrew baby boys killed, but the midwives Shiphrah and Puah fear God and refuse.
- **Exodus 2:** Moses is hidden in a basket on the Nile and raised by Pharaoh's daughter. Grown up, he kills an Egyptian, flees to Midian, and marries Zipporah. God hears Israel's groaning and remembers his covenant.
- **Exodus 3:** At the burning bush on Horeb, God calls Moses to lead Israel out and reveals his name: "I AM WHO I AM", the Lord (Yahweh).
- **Exodus 4:** God gives Moses signs and, when Moses keeps objecting, appoints Aaron to speak for him. On the way back to Egypt, Zipporah circumcises their son.
- **Exodus 5:** Pharaoh refuses: "Who is the Lord?" He makes the slaves gather their own straw, and the people turn on Moses.
- **Exodus 6:** God renews his promises: "I will take you to be my people, and I will be your God" (6:7). The family line of Moses and Aaron.
- **Exodus 7:** Aaron's staff swallows the magicians' staffs. First plague: the Nile turns to blood.
- **Exodus 8:** Frogs, gnats ("This is the finger of God", 8:19), and flies, which spare the land of Goshen.
- **Exodus 9:** The livestock die, then boils, then hail. Pharaoh hardens his heart.
- **Exodus 10:** Locusts, then three days of darkness. Pharaoh drives Moses away.
- **Exodus 11:** The last plague is announced: the death of every firstborn in Egypt.
- **Exodus 12:** God institutes the Passover. The firstborn of Egypt die, the Lord passes over houses marked with the lamb's blood, and Israel leaves after 430 years.
- **Exodus 13:** The firstborn are consecrated to the Lord and the Feast of Unleavened Bread is set. Israel carries Joseph's bones, led by a pillar of cloud and fire.
- **Exodus 14:** Pharaoh pursues Israel. The Lord parts the sea, Israel crosses on dry ground, and the Egyptian army drowns.
- **Exodus 15:** Moses and Miriam sing of the Lord's victory. Bitter water at Marah is made sweet.
- **Exodus 16:** The people grumble for food, and God sends quail and daily manna, with a double portion before the Sabbath.
- **Exodus 17:** Water from the rock at Massah and Meribah. Israel defeats Amalek while Moses' hands are held up.
- **Exodus 18:** Jethro visits and advises Moses to appoint judges to share the load.
- **Exodus 19:** At Sinai God calls Israel to be "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (19:6), and comes down on the mountain in fire and smoke.
- **Exodus 20:** God speaks the Ten Commandments. The terrified people ask Moses to speak to them instead.
- **Exodus 21:** The Book of the Covenant begins: laws on servants and on violence and injury ("eye for eye", 21:24).
- **Exodus 22:** Laws on theft, property and restitution, and protection for the sojourner, the widow and the orphan.
- **Exodus 23:** Justice in court, the Sabbath year, and the three yearly feasts. God promises an angel to lead them.
- **Exodus 24:** The covenant is sealed with sacrificial blood: "the blood of the covenant" (24:8). The elders eat before God, and Moses goes up the mountain for forty days.
- **Exodus 25:** Offerings for the tabernacle; the ark of the covenant, the table and the lampstand.
- **Exodus 26:** The tabernacle's curtains and frames, and the veil before the Most Holy Place.
- **Exodus 27:** The bronze altar, the courtyard, and oil for the lamp.
- **Exodus 28:** The priests' garments: the ephod, and the breastpiece with the Urim and Thummim.
- **Exodus 29:** The ordination of Aaron and his sons, and the daily offerings. "I will dwell among the people of Israel" (29:45).
- **Exodus 30:** The altar of incense, the atonement money, the bronze basin, the anointing oil and the incense.
- **Exodus 31:** Bezalel and Oholiab are filled with the Spirit for the work. The Sabbath is a sign of the covenant. God gives Moses the stone tablets.
- **Exodus 32:** The golden calf. Moses pleads for the people, breaks the tablets, and the Levites stand with him.
- **Exodus 33:** God threatens not to go with them, but Moses intercedes. Moses asks to see God's glory and is hidden in the rock.
- **Exodus 34:** New tablets. The Lord proclaims his name, "merciful and gracious … forgiving iniquity" yet just (34:6–7). The covenant is renewed, and Moses' face shines.
- **Exodus 35:** The Sabbath, and a call for willing offerings for the tabernacle.
- **Exodus 36:** The craftsmen begin; the people give more than enough. The curtains and frames are made.
- **Exodus 37:** The ark, the table, the lampstand and the altar of incense are made.
- **Exodus 38:** The altar of burnt offering, the basin and the courtyard are made, and the metals are counted.
- **Exodus 39:** The priestly garments are made. Moses inspects the finished work and blesses the people.
- **Exodus 40:** The tabernacle is set up, the cloud covers it, and the glory of the Lord fills it.

## Key people

- **Moses:** born a Hebrew, raised in Pharaoh's household, called at the burning bush; Israel's deliverer, lawgiver and mediator.
- **Aaron:** Moses' older brother and spokesman, first high priest. He makes the golden calf.
- **Miriam:** their sister, a prophetess who leads the women in song after the sea crossing.
- **Pharaoh:** the king of Egypt, whose heart is hardened against God's command.
- **Jethro (Reuel):** priest of Midian and Moses' father-in-law.
- **Zipporah:** Moses' wife.
- **Shiphrah and Puah:** the Hebrew midwives who fear God more than Pharaoh.
- **Joshua:** Moses' assistant, who leads Israel against Amalek.
- **Bezalel and Oholiab:** craftsmen filled with God's Spirit to build the tabernacle.

## Key events

- Slavery in Egypt, and Moses' rescue as a baby (1–2)
- The burning bush, and God's name revealed (3)
- The ten plagues (7–11)
- The first Passover (12)
- Crossing the Red Sea (14)
- Manna, and water from the rock (16–17)
- The Ten Commandments at Sinai (20)
- The covenant sealed with blood (24)
- Instructions for the tabernacle (25–31)
- The golden calf (32)
- The Lord proclaims his name (34:6–7)
- The tabernacle filled with God's glory (40)

## Themes

- **God keeps his promises:** he "remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob" (2:24) and acts to fulfil Genesis 15.
- **Redemption:** God brings his people out of slavery "with an outstretched arm and with great acts of judgment" (6:6), and the exodus becomes the pattern for all later salvation.
- **The Lord against the gods of Egypt:** the plagues are a contest over who is the one true God (12:12). Currid shows how each one strikes something Egypt worshipped, from the Nile to the sun.
- **God's sovereignty and the hardened heart:** both God and Pharaoh are said to harden Pharaoh's heart (4:21; 8:15; 9:12), so that God's power is shown (9:16; Rom 9:17).
- **Covenant and law:** saved first, then given the law: "I am the Lord your God, who brought you out … You shall have no other gods" (20:2–3).
- **God dwelling with his people:** the tabernacle is the place where the holy God lives among sinners (25:8; 40:34).
- **Idolatry and intercession:** the golden calf breaks the covenant almost at once, and Moses' pleading shows the need for a mediator.

## How it points to Christ

Christ is our Passover Lamb, sacrificed so that judgment passes over us (12; 1 Cor 5:7; John 1:29). The manna points to Jesus, the true bread from heaven (John 6:32–35), and "the Rock was Christ" (1 Cor 10:4). The "blood of the covenant" at Sinai (24:8) is echoed in Jesus' words at the Last Supper (Matt 26:28).

The tabernacle, God living among his people, is fulfilled when the Word "dwelt" (literally "tabernacled") among us (John 1:14), and its priesthood and sacrifices point to Christ's (Heb 8–10). Moses the mediator is surpassed by Jesus, the mediator of a better covenant (Heb 3:1–6). Jesus speaks of his death in Jerusalem as his "exodus" (Luke 9:31, Greek *exodos*), and takes God's name "I am" for himself (John 8:58).

## Common mix-ups

- Mount Sinai and Mount Horeb are the same mountain.
- Aaron is Moses' older brother, not his younger one. Miriam is their sister.
- Moses' father-in-law has two names, Jethro and Reuel.
- The Ten Commandments appear twice: Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5. The first tablets are broken at the golden calf; God writes them again in chapter 34.
- Numbering of the commandments differs. The Reformed numbering treats "no graven images" as the second commandment; Lutheran and Roman Catholic numbering fold it into the first and split the last.
- Passover is in chapter 12, before the sea crossing in chapter 14.
- Manna starts in Exodus 16, not in Numbers, though Numbers tells of Israel growing tired of it.

## Going deeper

- **The plagues as war on Egypt's gods.** Currid links each plague to an Egyptian deity: the Nile god Hapi, the frog-headed Heqet, the sun god Re, and Pharaoh himself as a god. He also shows the plagues undoing creation, sending Egypt back into chaos while Israel is kept safe. Read: Currid, The Plague Account.
- **Who hardened Pharaoh's heart?** God says he will harden it (4:21), and Pharaoh hardens it himself (8:15). In Egyptian belief Pharaoh's heart was the controlling centre of the world, which gives the theme extra force. Compare Romans 9:17–18.
- **When was the exodus?** 1 Kings 6:1 implies about 1446 BC; many scholars argue for the 13th century. Read: Lutheran Study Bible, Challenges for Readers.
- **The Decalogue and the case laws.** Currid notes that God spoke the Ten Commandments directly and wrote them himself, while Moses wrote down the case laws that applied them to Israel's life (21:1; 24:4). Compare the Westminster Confession's distinction between moral, ceremonial and civil (judicial) law (19.3–5).
- **The golden calf and the second commandment.** Aaron calls a festival "to the Lord" (32:5): Currid sees blending true worship with idolatry rather than simply worshipping another god. What does that teach about worship today?
- **The Lord's name (34:6–7).** God describes himself as merciful, gracious and slow to anger, yet by no means clearing the guilty. How do mercy and justice meet, and where do they meet in the New Testament?

## Q&A

1. What did God call himself at the burning bush? "I AM WHO I AM": the Lord, Yahweh (3:14–15).
2. Who were the midwives who refused Pharaoh's order? Shiphrah and Puah (1:15–17).
3. What was the tenth plague? The death of the firstborn (11–12).
4. What protected Israel's homes at the Passover? The blood of a lamb on the doorposts (12:13).
5. What did God feed Israel with in the wilderness? Manna, and quail (16).
6. Where did Israel receive the Ten Commandments? Mount Sinai (19–20).
7. What did God call Israel at Sinai? A kingdom of priests and a holy nation (19:6).
8. Who made the golden calf? Aaron (32:2–4).
9. What happened to Moses' face after he spoke with God? It shone (34:29).
10. How does Exodus end? The glory of the Lord fills the finished tabernacle (40:34).
