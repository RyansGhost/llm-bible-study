## Summary

James is a practical, punchy letter about living out real faith. It is written to Jewish Christians scattered abroad, facing trials, favouritism, quarrels and the pull of wealth. James calls them to count trials as joy, ask God for wisdom, be doers of the word and not hearers only, refuse to favour the rich over the poor, tame the tongue, humble themselves before God, and wait patiently for the Lord's coming. His best-known teaching is that "faith apart from works is dead" (2:26). Lowe notes the letter can feel like a "stream of consciousness", but it is carefully built and full of theology, with many echoes of Jesus' Sermon on the Mount.

## Setting

- **When:** probably early, in the 40s AD (the Lutheran Study Bible gives about AD 50), before James' martyrdom in AD 62. It may be one of the first New Testament books written.
- **Where:** from Jerusalem, "to the twelve tribes in the Dispersion" (1:1): Jewish Christians living outside Palestine, probably in several congregations (Lowe).
- **Who wrote it:** James, the brother of Jesus and leader of the Jerusalem church, known as "James the Just". Lowe weighs the arguments for pseudonymity and finds them unconvincing.
- **Where it sits:** the first of the letters often called the "General" or "Catholic" Epistles, addressed to a wide audience. It is very Jewish in flavour, and calls the Christian assembly a "synagogue" (2:2).

## Structure

Lowe follows Richard Bauckham's simple outline, noting that chapter 1 introduces almost every theme expounded later:

- 1:1: Greeting
- 1:2–27: Introduction: trials, wisdom, rich and poor, temptation, hearing and doing
- 2:1–26: Faith and works, especially in how rich and poor are treated
- 3:1–4:12: The tongue, true wisdom, and quarrels from worldly desires
- 4:13–5:6: Warnings to the boastful and the rich
- 5:7–20: Patience until the Lord comes, oaths, prayer, and restoring the wanderer

## Chapter by chapter

- **James 1:** Count it all joy when you meet trials, because testing produces steadfastness. If you lack wisdom, ask God in faith, without doubting. The lowly brother should boast in his exaltation, the rich in his humiliation. Blessed is the one who remains steadfast: he will receive the crown of life. God tempts no one. Every good and perfect gift comes from the Father of lights, who brought us forth by the word of truth. Be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger. Be doers of the word, and not hearers only. True religion is caring for orphans and widows and keeping unstained from the world (1:27).
- **James 2:** Don't show favouritism to the rich man in fine clothes over the poor man in shabby clothes. God has chosen the poor to be rich in faith. Love your neighbour as yourself, the royal law. Whoever fails in one point is guilty of all. What good is faith without works? Even the demons believe, and shudder. Abraham was justified by works when he offered Isaac, and Rahab when she hid the spies. "Faith apart from works is dead" (2:26).
- **James 3:** Not many should become teachers. The tongue is small but, like a bit or a rudder or a spark, it controls great things. It is a fire, a restless evil, full of deadly poison. With it we bless God and curse people made in his likeness. Wisdom from above is pure, peaceable, gentle, full of mercy and good fruits.
- **James 4:** Quarrels come from passions at war within you. Friendship with the world is enmity with God. "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (4:6). Submit to God, resist the devil and he will flee. Draw near to God and he will draw near to you. Don't speak evil against one another. Don't boast about tomorrow: "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that" (4:15).
- **James 5:** Woe to the rich who hoard wealth and cheat their workers. Be patient until the Lord's coming, like the farmer waiting for rain, and like Job and the prophets. Let your yes be yes and your no be no. If anyone is sick, let him call the elders to pray over him, anointing him with oil. Confess your sins to one another and pray. "The prayer of a righteous person has great power" (5:16), as Elijah's did. Whoever brings back a wanderer will save his soul from death.

## Key people

- **James:** the brother of the Lord, leader of the Jerusalem church (Acts 15:13; Gal 1:19), and a servant of God and of Christ (1:1).
- **The scattered believers:** Jewish Christians facing trials and temptations.
- **The rich and the poor:** both in the church and outside it (1:9–11; 2:1–7; 5:1–6).
- **Abraham:** whose faith was shown in offering Isaac (2:21–23).
- **Rahab:** whose faith was shown in hiding the spies (2:25).
- **Job:** an example of steadfastness (5:11).
- **Elijah:** an example of powerful prayer (5:17–18).
- **The elders:** who pray for the sick (5:14).

## Key events

- James' leadership at the Jerusalem council (Acts 15; not in this letter)
- Abraham's offering of Isaac (Gen 22; James 2:21)
- Rahab's welcome of the spies (Josh 2; James 2:25)
- Elijah's prayer for drought and rain (1 Kgs 17–18; James 5:17–18)

## Themes

- **Trials and steadfastness:** testing produces endurance and maturity (1:2–4, 12; 5:7–11).
- **Wisdom from God:** given generously to those who ask, and shown in peaceable, merciful lives (1:5; 3:13–18). Lowe compares James' teaching on wisdom with Paul's teaching on the Spirit.
- **The one God who doesn't change:** the Father of lights, with no variation or shadow (1:17). Lowe sets this against the fickle gods of the Greco-Roman world.
- **Hearing and doing:** true faith acts on God's word (1:22–25).
- **Faith and works:** saving faith is a living faith that shows itself in deeds (2:14–26).
- **Rich and poor:** no favouritism; God's care for the poor; warnings to the rich (2:1–7; 5:1–6).
- **The tongue:** speech reveals the heart and must be tamed (1:19, 26; 3:1–12).
- **Humility and prayer:** submit to God, draw near to him, and pray in faith (4:6–10; 5:13–18).

## How it points to Christ

James names Jesus only twice: "the Lord Jesus Christ" (1:1) and "our glorious Lord Jesus Christ" (2:1). But the letter is soaked in his teaching. The Lutheran Study Bible lists many parallels with the Sermon on the Mount: joy in trials (Matt 5:12), being doers of the word (Matt 7:24), mercy triumphing over judgment (Matt 5:7), the peacemaker (Matt 5:9), treasures that rot (Matt 6:19), and plain yes and no (Matt 5:37). James awaits "the coming of the Lord", the Judge standing at the door (5:7–9). Every good gift comes from the Father of lights (1:17), and the greatest is that he "brought us forth by the word of truth" (1:18), the new birth through the gospel of Christ.

## Common mix-ups

- James and Paul don't contradict each other. Paul is answering the question "How is a sinner made right with God?": by faith alone, apart from works (Rom 3:28). James is answering "What does real faith look like?": it works (2:18). "Justified" in James means shown to be righteous. The Reformers' summary: we are justified by faith alone, but the faith that justifies is never alone (WCF 11.2).
- Luther called James "an epistle of straw" in his 1522 preface. The Lutheran Study Bible explains he was comparing it with Paul's letters, not denying its truth, and he elsewhere called it "a good book" that promotes God's law.
- This James is the Lord's brother, not James the son of Zebedee (one of the Twelve), who was martyred in about AD 44 (Acts 12:2).
- "Faith without works is dead" is James 2:26 (and 2:17).
- "Resist the devil, and he will flee from you" is James 4:7.
- Anointing with oil (5:14) isn't the Roman Catholic "last rites". It goes with the elders' prayer for healing.

## Going deeper

- **Faith and works (2:14–26).** Lowe reads James 2 as answering people misusing Paul's language. Compare Romans 4 and James 2 on Abraham. How does WCF 11.2 hold them together?
- **Luther and James.** Read the Lutheran Study Bible's "Luther on James". What was Luther's worry? How did Reformed theologians such as Calvin handle James?
- **Favouritism (2:1–13).** Where might churches today show partiality to the wealthy, influential or attractive?
- **The tongue (3:1–12).** Why does James give teachers a special warning? What practical steps help tame the tongue?
- **"If the Lord wills" (4:13–17).** How does this shape the way Christians make plans?
- **Prayer for the sick (5:13–18).** What does James teach about the elders' role, confession, and the power of prayer?

## Q&A

1. To whom is James written? "To the twelve tribes in the Dispersion" (1:1): Jewish Christians scattered abroad.
2. How should we respond to trials? Count it all joy, because testing produces steadfastness (1:2–3).
3. What should anyone lacking wisdom do? Ask God, who gives generously, in faith without doubting (1:5).
4. From whom does every good and perfect gift come? The Father of lights (1:17).
5. What should we be, besides hearers of the word? Doers of the word (1:22).
6. What is pure religion, according to 1:27? Visiting orphans and widows in their affliction, and keeping oneself unstained from the world.
7. What sin does James 2:1–7 warn against? Showing favouritism to the rich.
8. What does James say about faith without works? It is dead (2:17, 26).
9. What small part of the body does James compare to a fire? The tongue (3:5–6).
10. What does James say about the prayer of a righteous person? It has great power as it is working (5:16).
