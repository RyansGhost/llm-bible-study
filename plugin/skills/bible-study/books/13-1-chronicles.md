# 1 Chronicles

## Summary

1 Chronicles retells Israel's story for the Jews who had come back from exile in Babylon. It opens with genealogies from Adam to the returned exiles, showing who belongs to God's people. Then it focuses on David: his kingship over "all Israel", bringing the ark to Jerusalem, God's covenant with his house, and his careful preparations for the temple his son Solomon will build. The message to a small, struggling community: God's promises to David's line and his presence in the temple still stand.

## Setting

- **When:** written after the exiles returned (538 BC) and before Greek influence arrived with Alexander (331 BC). The Reformation Study Bible places it between about 515 and 400 BC, probably after 460 BC, since David's line is traced at least two generations past Zerubbabel (3:17–24). It covers from Adam to David's death (about 970 BC).
- **Where:** Jerusalem is the centre: David's city and the site of the future temple.
- **Who wrote it:** "the Chronicler", unnamed, perhaps from a priestly or Levitical family, given his interest in the temple. Jewish tradition names Ezra, but most scholars now doubt that one author wrote Chronicles and Ezra–Nehemiah (RSB; Pratt).
- **Why:** to encourage the returned exiles, who faced poverty, opposition and division, and asked: Are we still God's people? Which institutions matter now? Is there hope for David's line?
- **Where it sits:** 1 and 2 Chronicles were one book, called in Hebrew "the events of the days", and they are the last book of the Hebrew Bible. In English Bibles they follow Kings. The Chronicler draws on Samuel and Kings, other Scripture, and royal and prophetic records he names; where his numbers differ from Samuel and Kings, copying errors or textual differences are the likely cause (RSB). Pratt stresses that he is both a careful historian and a theologian.

## Structure

The Reformation Study Bible divides Chronicles into the genealogies (1 Chr 1:1–9:34), the united monarchy (1 Chr 9:35 – 2 Chr 9:31), and the kings of Judah (2 Chr 10–36). In 1 Chronicles:

- 1–9: Genealogies: from Adam to Israel's tribes, with Judah (David's line) and Levi (the priests) given the most space, ending with those who returned to Jerusalem
- 10: Saul's death, and why he died (Saul's reign is otherwise left out)
- 11–29: David's reign
    - 11–12: David king over all Israel; his mighty men
    - 13–16: the ark brought to Jerusalem; worship arranged
    - 17: God's covenant with David
    - 18–20: David's victories
    - 21: the census, the plague, and the temple site
    - 22–29: David prepares for the temple and organises the priests, Levites, musicians and officials; Solomon becomes king

## Chapter by chapter

- **1 Chronicles 1:** Family lines from Adam through Noah's sons to Abraham, Isaac, Esau and the kings of Edom.
- **1 Chronicles 2:** The sons of Israel, then Judah's line down to David's family.
- **1 Chronicles 3:** David's descendants, through the kings of Judah, into and beyond the exile.
- **1 Chronicles 4:** More of Judah, including the prayer of Jabez (4:9–10), and the tribe of Simeon.
- **1 Chronicles 5:** Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh east of the Jordan, exiled by Assyria for unfaithfulness.
- **1 Chronicles 6:** Levi: the high priests, the temple singers, and the Levites' towns.
- **1 Chronicles 7:** Issachar, Benjamin, Naphtali, Manasseh, Ephraim and Asher.
- **1 Chronicles 8:** Benjamin again, including Saul's family.
- **1 Chronicles 9:** The people who resettled Jerusalem after the exile: priests, Levites and gatekeepers.
- **1 Chronicles 10:** Saul dies on Mount Gilboa. He died for his unfaithfulness and for not seeking the Lord (10:13–14).
- **1 Chronicles 11:** All Israel makes David king at Hebron. He captures Jerusalem. His mighty men.
- **1 Chronicles 12:** Warriors from every tribe join David, even from Saul's own tribe of Benjamin.
- **1 Chronicles 13:** The first attempt to bring the ark to Jerusalem ends when Uzzah is struck down.
- **1 Chronicles 14:** David's palace and family grow, and he defeats the Philistines.
- **1 Chronicles 15:** The ark is brought up properly, carried by the Levites, with music and joy.
- **1 Chronicles 16:** David's psalm of thanksgiving, and regular worship appointed before the ark.
- **1 Chronicles 17:** God promises David a house and an everlasting kingdom, and David prays in humble wonder.
- **1 Chronicles 18:** David's victories and his officials.
- **1 Chronicles 19:** The Ammonites humiliate David's envoys, and David defeats Ammon and Syria.
- **1 Chronicles 20:** Rabbah is captured, and Philistine giants are killed.
- **1 Chronicles 21:** Satan incites David to count Israel. A plague follows, and David buys Ornan's threshing floor and builds an altar there.
- **1 Chronicles 22:** David gathers materials for the temple and charges Solomon to build it.
- **1 Chronicles 23:** Solomon is made king, and the Levites are counted and assigned duties.
- **1 Chronicles 24:** The 24 divisions of priests.
- **1 Chronicles 25:** The temple musicians.
- **1 Chronicles 26:** Gatekeepers, treasurers and other officials.
- **1 Chronicles 27:** Army divisions and tribal leaders.
- **1 Chronicles 28:** David charges Israel's leaders and Solomon, and gives Solomon the temple plans, which the Spirit had given him.
- **1 Chronicles 29:** The leaders give generously. David prays: "all things come from you, and of your own have we given you" (29:14). Solomon is confirmed as king, and David dies.

## Key people

- **David:** the central figure: king over all Israel, organiser of worship, and recipient of God's covenant.
- **Solomon:** chosen to build the temple.
- **Saul:** his death introduces David's reign and serves as a warning.
- **The Levites:** priests, singers, gatekeepers and treasurers, given great attention.
- **Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun:** leaders of temple music.
- **Jabez:** remembered for his prayer (4:9–10).
- **Joab:** David's commander.
- **Nathan:** the prophet who brings God's covenant promise.
- **Ornan (Araunah):** owner of the threshing floor that becomes the temple site.

## Key events

- The genealogies from Adam to the return from exile (1–9)
- Saul's death, and the reason given (10)
- David made king over all Israel; Jerusalem captured (11)
- The ark brought to Jerusalem with music and praise (13; 15–16)
- God's covenant with David (17)
- The census, and the purchase of the temple site (21)
- David's preparations for the temple (22–29)
- David's final prayer and death (29)

## Themes

- **All Israel:** the Chronicler loves the phrase "all Israel". Pratt shows him longing for every tribe, north and south, to be united again around God's king and temple.
- **David's line:** God's covenant with David (17) gave the returned exiles hope that God had not abandoned his promise.
- **The temple and worship:** much of the book concerns the ark, the Levites, music and temple service. Worship done God's way is at the heart of Israel's life.
- **Seeking the Lord:** Saul died because he didn't seek the Lord (10:13–14). Faithfulness brings blessing and unfaithfulness brings judgment, often quickly; yet repentance and prayer restore (21; 2 Chr 7:14). The Chronicler presses this on his readers.
- **God's purpose through history:** starting from Adam reminds a small community on the edge of the Persian Empire that they stand at the centre of God's plan (RSB).
- **Generous, joyful giving:** David and the people give freely for God's house, confessing that everything already belongs to God (29:10–19).
- **Who belongs to God's people:** the genealogies answer it. The returned remnant is the continuation of the Israel God chose, from Adam down.

## How it points to Christ

The genealogies that start with Adam and run through David's line set up the New Testament's opening: "The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham" (Matt 1:1; compare Luke 3:23–38). God's promise of a son on David's throne forever (17:11–14) is fulfilled in Jesus (Luke 1:32–33; Heb 1:5).

David buys the temple site at the place where judgment was stopped by sacrifice (21:26–22:1). That is Mount Moriah (2 Chr 3:1), where Abraham offered Isaac. It points to Christ, the true temple and final sacrifice, where God's judgment is turned away for good.

## Common mix-ups

- Chronicles isn't simply a repeat of Samuel and Kings. It retells the history for the returned exiles, leaving out some things and adding others (Pratt).
- 1 Chronicles leaves out David's sin with Bathsheba, not because the writer denies it, but because his purpose is different. Readers knew Samuel.
- In 1 Chronicles 21 Satan incites the census; in 2 Samuel 24 the Lord does. Both are true: God in judgment allows Satan's work.
- The threshing floor owner is called Ornan in Chronicles and Araunah in Samuel.
- The prayer of Jabez is two verses in a genealogy (4:9–10), not a major theme of the book.
- In the Hebrew Bible, Chronicles is the last book, not one of the history books in the middle.

## Going deeper

- **Why start with nine chapters of names?** Pratt explains how the genealogies answer the exiles' urgent question: are we still God's people? Notice how much space Judah and Levi receive, and why.
- **History or theology?** Pratt argues the two are inseparable in Chronicles. Compare 1 Chronicles 21 with 2 Samuel 24, or 1 Chronicles 10 with 1 Samuel 31. What does the Chronicler add, and why?
- **The northern tribes and the nations.** Pratt shows a balanced outlook: openness to northerners and foreigners who turn to God, with warnings against compromise. How does this fit the problems of Ezra and Nehemiah's day?
- **Worship and music.** David organises singers and instruments in great detail (15–16; 25). What does this teach about the place of music in worship? Compare 1 Chronicles 16 with Psalms 96, 105 and 106.
- **Seeking God.** Trace "seek" through the book. How does Saul's failure (10:13–14) prepare for the call in 2 Chronicles 7:14?

## Q&A

1. Where do the genealogies in 1 Chronicles begin? With Adam (1:1).
2. Why, according to the Chronicler, did Saul die? For his unfaithfulness: he didn't keep God's word, consulted a medium, and didn't seek the Lord (10:13–14).
3. Who carried the ark properly the second time? The Levites (15:2, 15).
4. What did God promise David in chapter 17? A house: a son whose throne God would establish forever (17:11–14).
5. Who incited David's census in Chronicles? Satan (21:1).
6. Why didn't David build the temple? He had shed much blood as a warrior; his son Solomon, a man of peace, would build it (22:8–10).
7. Where did the plans for the temple come from? David received them from the Spirit and gave them to Solomon (28:11–12, 19).
8. What did David confess about the people's offerings? That all things come from God, and they had only given back what was his (29:14).
9. When was Chronicles written? After the return from exile in Babylon, after 539 BC.
10. Where does Chronicles come in the Hebrew Bible? Last.
