# 2 Thessalonians

## Summary

2 Thessalonians is a short follow-up letter, written soon after 1 Thessalonians. The church is still suffering persecution, and some have been unsettled by a claim that the day of the Lord has already come. Paul encourages them that God will justly repay their persecutors when Christ is revealed from heaven. He explains that the day of the Lord won't come until the rebellion and the "man of lawlessness" are revealed, whom Christ will destroy at his coming. He thanks God for choosing them, asks for prayer, and firmly corrects those who have stopped working and are living in an unruly way.

## Setting

- **When:** about AD 51–52, from Corinth, a few months after 1 Thessalonians. Cara reasons that Paul, Silas and Timothy are still together, as in Acts 18:5.
- **Where:** to the church at Thessalonica, in Macedonia.
- **Who wrote it:** Paul, with Silvanus and Timothy (1:1), signed in Paul's own hand (3:17). Many critical scholars deny that Paul wrote it, claiming it imitates 1 Thessalonians or contradicts its teaching on the end. Cara firmly defends Paul's authorship. The Reformation Study Bible notes that Ignatius, Polycarp and Justin Martyr all accepted it; that 1 Thessalonians stresses the day's suddenness for the unprepared, not that it must come at once; and that a forger would hardly appeal to what Paul taught in person (2:5; 3:10) or rebuke his readers so sharply.
- **Where it sits:** Cara suggests that whoever carried 1 Thessalonians returned with news of how the church had received it. Some were confused about the second coming, possibly misled by a letter falsely claiming to be from Paul (2:2).

## Structure

The Reformation Study Bible outlines it as greeting (1:1–2), Paul's boasting in their faith under persecution (1:3–12), instruction (2:1–3:15), and closing blessing (3:16–18). Cara's fuller outline:

- 1:1–2: Opening
- 1:3–12: Thanksgiving, and God's just judgment at Christ's revelation
- 2:1–12: The man of lawlessness: the day of the Lord hasn't come
- 2:13–17: Second thanksgiving: chosen for salvation
- 3:1–5: Prayer request
- 3:6–15: Warning against idleness
- 3:16–18: Closing

## Chapter by chapter

- **2 Thessalonians 1:** Paul thanks God that their faith and love are growing and boasts of their steadfastness under persecution. God will repay those who afflict them, when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels in flaming fire. Those who don't know God or obey the gospel will suffer eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord (1:9). Paul prays that God will make them worthy of his calling.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:** Don't be shaken by claims that the day of the Lord has come. First the rebellion must come and the man of lawlessness be revealed, who sets himself up in God's temple, proclaiming himself to be God. Something is restraining him now. The Lord Jesus will kill him with the breath of his mouth at his coming. Those who refuse to love the truth will be deceived. But God chose you to be saved, through sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth (2:13). Stand firm and hold to the traditions you were taught.
- **2 Thessalonians 3:** Pray that the word of the Lord may spread quickly. The Lord is faithful and will guard you from the evil one. Keep away from any brother walking in idleness. Paul worked night and day to set an example: "If anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat" (3:10). Don't grow weary in doing good. Warn the disobedient as a brother, not an enemy. Paul's greeting in his own hand is the mark of a genuine letter.

## Key people

- **Paul:** writing again to correct and encourage.
- **Silvanus (Silas) and Timothy:** co-senders (1:1).
- **The Thessalonian believers:** persecuted, growing, but some confused and some idle.
- **The "man of lawlessness":** a future figure of rebellion who opposes God and claims to be God, to be destroyed by Christ (2:3–10).
- **The idle or unruly:** church members who refused to work (3:6–12).

## Key events

- Continuing persecution of the church (1:4–7)
- A false report, perhaps a forged letter, that the day of the Lord had come (2:2)
- Paul's example of working night and day while among them (3:7–9)

## Themes

- **God's just judgment:** God will repay persecutors and give relief to his afflicted people at Christ's return (1:5–10).
- **Christ the divine warrior:** Cara shows how Paul draws on Old Testament pictures of God as a warrior (Exod 15; Isa 11:4; 66:15) and applies them to Jesus, who comes in flaming fire and destroys the lawless one with the breath of his mouth.
- **Eternal punishment:** one of the clearest statements in the Bible about eternal destruction away from the presence of the Lord (1:9).
- **The day of the Lord hasn't yet come:** events must come first, so believers shouldn't be shaken or alarmed (2:1–12).
- **Election and perseverance:** God chose them from the beginning to be saved and will establish and guard them (2:13–14; 3:3).
- **Holding to apostolic teaching:** stand firm in what Paul taught by word and letter (2:15; 3:6, 14).
- **Wrong beliefs lead to wrong living:** many think some had stopped working because they expected Christ to return any day (RSB).
- **The day of mercy now:** this is the time when the gospel goes out and God's chosen are brought to faith (2:13–14); when Christ returns, that day of mercy closes and the unrepentant meet him as Judge (1:7–10).
- **Work and order:** Christians should work quietly and earn their own living. Cara prefers "unruly" to "idle" for the Greek word (3:6–12).

## How it points to Christ

The letter shows the returning Christ in his power and glory. He will be "revealed from heaven with his mighty angels in flaming fire" (1:7), taking up the role of the Lord as divine warrior from the Old Testament. He comes "to be glorified in his saints, and to be marvelled at among all who have believed" (1:10). He will destroy the lawless one simply "with the breath of his mouth" (2:8; Isa 11:4). The Lord Jesus Christ, with the Father, loved us and gave us eternal comfort and good hope through grace (2:16), and the Lord is faithful to guard his people (3:3).

## Common mix-ups

- "If anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat" (3:10) is about church members who could work but refused. It isn't about people unable to work.
- The "man of lawlessness" (2:3) isn't named "antichrist" here, though many connect him with John's antichrist and the beasts of Revelation. Interpreters differ on who or what he is. Reformers such as Luther and Calvin, and the original Westminster Confession (25.6), identified him with the papacy.
- The "restrainer" (2:6–7) isn't identified. Suggestions include the Roman state, the rule of law, the preaching of the gospel, or the Holy Spirit.
- 2 Thessalonians doesn't contradict 1 Thessalonians. The first says the day comes unexpectedly for unbelievers. The second says believers can know it hasn't come yet.
- Paul's signature in his own hand (3:17) guards against forged letters (compare 2:2).

## Going deeper

- **Who wrote 2 Thessalonians?** Cara reviews why many critical scholars think it is pseudonymous, and why he holds that Paul wrote it. What is at stake?
- **The divine warrior (1:7–10).** Cara traces God as warrior from Exodus 15 through the Psalms and prophets. What does it mean that Jesus fills this role?
- **Eternal destruction (1:9).** How does this verse shape the doctrine of hell? How does the Reformed view answer annihilationism?
- **The man of lawlessness and the restrainer (2:1–12).** Survey the main views. Why did the Reformers see the papacy here, and why have many later Reformed interpreters taken a broader view?
- **Chosen and called (2:13–14).** How do election, the Spirit's sanctifying work and belief in the truth fit together?
- **Work and church discipline (3:6–15).** What does Paul's response to the unruly teach about work and about correcting a brother "not as an enemy"?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** Why did Paul write 2 Thessalonians so soon after 1 Thessalonians?

   **A:** Some were shaken by a claim that the day of the Lord had already come, and some were refusing to work (2:1–2; 3:11).

2. **Q:** What will happen when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven?

   **A:** He will come with mighty angels in flaming fire, bringing justice on those who don't know God or obey the gospel (1:7–8).

3. **Q:** What punishment does 1:9 describe?

   **A:** Eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord.

4. **Q:** What must come before the day of the Lord?

   **A:** The rebellion and the revealing of the man of lawlessness (2:3).

5. **Q:** How will the Lord Jesus destroy the lawless one?

   **A:** With the breath of his mouth, by the appearance of his coming (2:8).

6. **Q:** What did God choose the Thessalonians for?

   **A:** To be saved, through sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth (2:13).

7. **Q:** What should they hold to?

   **A:** The traditions (teaching) they were taught by Paul, by word or by letter (2:15).

8. **Q:** What rule did Paul give about work?

   **A:** If anyone isn't willing to work, let him not eat (3:10).

9. **Q:** How should they treat a disobedient brother?

   **A:** Have nothing to do with him so he may be ashamed, but warn him as a brother, not an enemy (3:14–15).

10. **Q:** How did Paul show his letters were genuine?

    **A:** By writing the greeting in his own hand (3:17).
