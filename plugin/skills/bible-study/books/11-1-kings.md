# 1 Kings

## Summary

1 Kings opens in glory and ends in division. Solomon, given wisdom by God, builds the temple, and the Lord's glory fills it. But his many foreign wives turn his heart to other gods, and after his death the kingdom splits in two: Judah in the south under David's line, and Israel in the north under Jeroboam, who sets up golden calves. In the north, wicked Ahab and Jezebel promote Baal, and God sends the prophet Elijah to confront them.

## Setting

- **When:** from David's last days to the death of Ahab (about 853 BC). Fixed points (Reformation Study Bible): Solomon reigned about 970–930 BC, and the kingdom divided in 930 BC. Dating each later king is hard, because the two kingdoms may have counted years differently and some reigns overlapped as co-regencies.
- **Where:** Jerusalem and its temple; after the split, Judah (capital Jerusalem) and Israel (capitals Shechem, Tirzah, and then Samaria). Elijah's ministry ranges from Carmel to Sinai (Horeb).
- **Who wrote it:** unknown. Jewish tradition named Jeremiah, but there's no firm evidence. 1 and 2 Kings were one book, completed during the exile, about 550 BC: its last event is 561 BC (2 Kgs 25:27), and it doesn't mention the return in 538 BC. Some think a first edition was written under Josiah. The writer used older sources, naming the "Book of the Acts of Solomon" (11:41) and the chronicles of the kings of Israel and of Judah.
- **Where it sits:** the last of the Former Prophets (Joshua to Kings), which share the outlook of Deuteronomy. 1 Kings finishes the story of David's court begun in 2 Samuel; the Greek Bible calls 1 and 2 Kings "3 and 4 Kingdoms". Fullilove calls Kings "selective and theological history": it chooses events to show why the kingdoms fell.

## Structure

The Reformation Study Bible's outline of 1 Kings:

- 1–11: Solomon: his succession, wisdom, temple, glory, and fall
- 12–16: The early divided kingdom: Rehoboam and Jeroboam, and the kings of Judah and Israel to Ahab
- 17–22: Prophets and kings: Elijah and Ahab (this section runs on to 2 Kgs 8:15)

After the split, the writer moves back and forth between the two kingdoms, telling the story of one people of God. Each reign has a set frame: when the king began (dated by the other kingdom's king), his age, how long he reigned, his mother's name (in Judah), and a verdict on him; then his death, burial and successor. Fullilove's outline of all of Kings: Solomon (1 Kgs 1–11), the divided kingdom (1 Kgs 12 – 2 Kgs 17), and Judah alone (2 Kgs 18–25).

## Chapter by chapter

- **1 Kings 1:** David is old and frail, and Adonijah tries to seize the throne. Nathan and Bathsheba intervene, and David has Solomon anointed king.
- **1 Kings 2:** David charges Solomon to keep God's law and dies. Solomon secures the throne by dealing with Adonijah, Joab and Shimei, and banishing Abiathar.
- **1 Kings 3:** At Gibeon, Solomon asks God for wisdom and receives riches and honour as well. He wisely settles the case of two mothers and one baby.
- **1 Kings 4:** Solomon's officials, his peaceful and prosperous kingdom, and his wisdom: 3,000 proverbs and 1,005 songs.
- **1 Kings 5:** Hiram king of Tyre supplies cedar, and Solomon raises a workforce to build the temple.
- **1 Kings 6:** The temple is built in the 480th year after the exodus (6:1), taking seven years.
- **1 Kings 7:** Solomon's own palace takes thirteen years. Hiram the craftsman casts the bronze pillars, the great basin (the "sea") and the temple furnishings.
- **1 Kings 8:** The ark is brought into the temple, the cloud of God's glory fills it, and Solomon prays a long prayer of dedication.
- **1 Kings 9:** God appears to Solomon again, warning that disobedience will bring the temple's ruin. Solomon's building projects and forced labour.
- **1 Kings 10:** The Queen of Sheba visits and is overwhelmed by Solomon's wisdom and wealth. He gathers vast gold, horses and chariots.
- **1 Kings 11:** Solomon's hundreds of foreign wives turn his heart to other gods. God raises adversaries, and the prophet Ahijah gives ten tribes to Jeroboam. Solomon dies.
- **1 Kings 12:** Rehoboam answers the people harshly, and the ten northern tribes break away under Jeroboam, who sets up golden calves at Bethel and Dan.
- **1 Kings 13:** A man of God from Judah condemns the altar at Bethel, but he disobeys God's instructions on the way home and is killed by a lion.
- **1 Kings 14:** Ahijah pronounces judgment on Jeroboam's house. Judah also falls into idolatry, and Pharaoh Shishak plunders Jerusalem.
- **1 Kings 15:** In Judah, Abijam, then faithful Asa, who removes idols. In Israel, Nadab, overthrown by Baasha.
- **1 Kings 16:** In Israel, a string of short reigns and coups: Elah, Zimri (seven days), Omri, who builds Samaria. Ahab marries Jezebel and serves Baal.
- **1 Kings 17:** Elijah announces a drought. He is fed by ravens and by a widow at Zarephath, whose flour and oil don't run out, and he raises her son.
- **1 Kings 18:** On Mount Carmel Elijah challenges 450 prophets of Baal. Fire falls from heaven on his sacrifice, the people cry "The Lord, he is God!", and the rain returns.
- **1 Kings 19:** Elijah flees Jezebel to Horeb, where God speaks in a low whisper, not wind, earthquake or fire. Seven thousand haven't bowed to Baal. Elijah calls Elisha.
- **1 Kings 20:** God gives Ahab victories over Ben-hadad of Syria, but Ahab lets him go and is rebuked by a prophet.
- **1 Kings 21:** Jezebel has Naboth killed so Ahab can take his vineyard. Elijah pronounces doom, and Ahab humbles himself.
- **1 Kings 22:** The prophet Micaiah alone foretells Ahab's defeat. Ahab dies at Ramoth-gilead, struck by an arrow shot at random. Jehoshaphat reigns in Judah.

## Key people

- **David:** in his last days, he names Solomon king.
- **Solomon:** David and Bathsheba's son. The wisest king, builder of the temple, but led astray by his wives.
- **Bathsheba and Nathan:** they secure Solomon's succession.
- **Adonijah:** David's son who tries to take the throne.
- **Hiram of Tyre:** Solomon's ally, who supplies cedar for the temple.
- **The Queen of Sheba:** a foreign queen who comes to test Solomon's wisdom.
- **Rehoboam:** Solomon's son, whose harshness splits the kingdom.
- **Jeroboam:** the first king of northern Israel, who sets up the golden calves.
- **Asa and Jehoshaphat:** faithful kings of Judah.
- **Omri:** king of Israel, founder of Samaria.
- **Ahab and Jezebel:** king and queen of Israel, promoters of Baal worship.
- **Elijah:** the prophet who confronts Ahab and Baal.
- **Elisha:** Elijah's successor, called in chapter 19.
- **Micaiah:** a true prophet who stands alone against 400 false ones.
- **Naboth:** murdered for his vineyard.

## Themes

- **Covenant faithfulness is the measure of every king:** Kings judges each ruler by Deuteronomy, especially worship at the one place God chose (Deut 12) and the covenant's blessings and curses (Deut 28) (Fullilove).
- **Like king, like nation:** as a king goes, so the people usually go. Every northern king "walked in the way of Jeroboam"; in Judah, David is the standard (15:3, 11, 26).
- **One God, one sanctuary:** the Lord alone rules all nations, and he chose one place for worship (Deut 12). The kings' usual sin isn't dropping the Lord altogether, but mixing his worship with other gods at unauthorised high places (RSB).
- **The divided heart:** even wise Solomon breaks the law of the king: horses, gold and many wives (Deut 17:16–17; 1 Kgs 10–11). "His heart was not wholly true to the Lord" (11:4).
- **The temple and God's presence:** God's glory fills the house Solomon builds (8:10–11), yet "heaven … cannot contain you" (8:27).
- **The Davidic promise holds:** despite Solomon's sin, God keeps "a lamp" for David in Jerusalem (11:36; 15:4).
- **The prophetic word:** prophets confront kings, and every word they speak comes true, often after a delay (Fullilove). Besides Elijah there are Nathan, Ahijah, Shemaiah and Micaiah.
- **Why the kingdom split:** the old rivalry between Judah and Ephraim (Gen 49; Judg 8–9; 2 Sam 20) breaks out when Jeroboam, an Ephraimite, leads the north away from David's house (12:16) (RSB).
- **The Lord, not Baal, is God:** the Carmel contest shows that only the Lord answers (18:39).

## How it points to Christ

Solomon's wisdom and glory point beyond him: Jesus says "something greater than Solomon is here" (Matt 12:42). The temple where God's glory dwelt is fulfilled in Jesus, who spoke of his body as the temple (John 2:19–21). He is the true Son of David whose kingdom never divides or falls.

Elijah's ministry anticipates the gospel. Jesus points to the widow of Zarephath as an outsider God helped (Luke 4:25–26). He raises a widow's son as Elijah did (Luke 7:11–16). Elijah himself appears with Jesus at the transfiguration (Matt 17:3). John the Baptist comes "in the spirit and power of Elijah" (Luke 1:17).

## Common mix-ups

- After Solomon, "Israel" usually means the northern kingdom (ten tribes, capital Samaria). "Judah" is the southern kingdom (with Benjamin, capital Jerusalem).
- Rehoboam (Solomon's son, king of Judah) and Jeroboam (first king of the north) are easy to confuse.
- There are two Jeroboams: Jeroboam I (1 Kgs 12) and Jeroboam II (2 Kgs 14).
- Elijah's great contest is on Mount Carmel; he then flees to Mount Horeb (Sinai).
- Ahab is a king of Israel, not Judah. Jehoshaphat of Judah allies with him.
- Ahab dies in 1 Kings 22. Jezebel dies later, in 2 Kings 9.
- Elisha is called in 1 Kings 19, but his ministry mostly fills 2 Kings.

## Going deeper

- **Solomon's fall and Deuteronomy 17.** Line up 1 Kings 10–11 with the law of the king (Deut 17:14–20). How does Kings show that the wisest man's wisdom couldn't keep his heart?
- **The temple prayer (8).** Solomon asks God to hear when Israel sins, is defeated, suffers drought, or is exiled, and turns back and prays toward the temple. How does the rest of Kings play this out?
- **Why the kingdom divided.** The split is caused by Solomon's sin (11:11), Rehoboam's folly (12:15), and God's own decree. How do human responsibility and God's sovereignty fit here?
- **Jeroboam's sin.** Every northern king is measured against "the sins of Jeroboam": the golden calves at Bethel and Dan (12:28–30). Why was this so serious? Compare Exodus 32.
- **Elijah's despair (19).** After his greatest victory, Elijah wants to die. How does God care for him: sleep, food, presence, a word, a task, and companions?
- **Prophecy fulfilled.** Fullilove notes how Kings records predictions and their fulfilments: 1 Kings 13:2 and 2 Kings 23:15–16; 1 Kings 21:23 and 2 Kings 9:36. What does that teach about God's word?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** What did Solomon ask God for at Gibeon?

   **A:** An understanding mind to govern well: wisdom (3:9).

2. **Q:** How did Solomon solve the dispute between two mothers?

   **A:** He ordered the baby cut in two, and gave him to the woman who begged to spare him (3:16–28).

3. **Q:** What filled the temple at its dedication?

   **A:** The cloud of the Lord's glory (8:10–11).

4. **Q:** Why did Solomon's heart turn from the Lord?

   **A:** His many foreign wives turned him to their gods (11:1–8).

5. **Q:** Who were the first kings of the divided kingdoms?

   **A:** Rehoboam in Judah; Jeroboam in Israel (12:16–20).

6. **Q:** What did Jeroboam set up at Bethel and Dan?

   **A:** Golden calves (12:28–29).

7. **Q:** Who fed Elijah during the drought?

   **A:** Ravens, and then a widow of Zarephath (17:6, 9–16).

8. **Q:** What happened on Mount Carmel?

   **A:** Fire from the Lord consumed Elijah's sacrifice after Baal's prophets failed (18:24–39).

9. **Q:** How did God speak to Elijah at Horeb?

   **A:** In a low whisper, not in the wind, earthquake or fire (19:11–12).

10. **Q:** Why did Ahab and Jezebel kill Naboth?

    **A:** To take his vineyard (21:1–16).
