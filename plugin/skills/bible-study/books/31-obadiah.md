# Obadiah

## Summary

Obadiah, the shortest book of the Old Testament, is a single vision against Edom. When Jerusalem fell, Edom, descended from Jacob's brother Esau, gloated, looted and handed fleeing Judeans to the enemy. God declares that Edom's pride will be brought down and that it will be done to Edom as it did. On the day of the Lord, Mount Zion will be delivered and "the kingdom shall be the Lord's" (v. 21).

## Setting

- **When:** probably soon after Babylon destroyed Jerusalem in 586 BC (the Lutheran Study Bible gives about 587–553 BC), when Edom took advantage of Judah's disaster (vv. 11–14; Ps 137:7).
- **Where:** Edom, the rugged mountain country south-east of the Dead Sea, with cities in the rocks (around Sela, later Petra), and Jerusalem.
- **Who wrote it:** Obadiah, whose name means "servant of the Lord". Nothing else is known about him.
- **Where it sits:** among the Twelve. Timmer discusses how the Twelve hold the nations responsible for their sins against God's people.

## Structure

One chapter of 21 verses:

- vv. 1–9: Edom's pride will be humbled
- vv. 10–14: Edom's crime against his brother Jacob
- vv. 15–21: The day of the Lord: judgment on the nations and deliverance on Mount Zion

## Chapter by chapter

Obadiah has a single chapter, summarised here by section.

- **Verses 1–4:** God summons the nations against Edom. Though Edom nests high in the rocks like an eagle, God will bring it down: "the pride of your heart has deceived you" (v. 3).
- **Verses 5–9:** Edom will be plundered completely, betrayed by its allies, and its famed wise men will be destroyed.
- **Verses 10–14:** Because of violence against "your brother Jacob", shame will cover Edom. It stood aloof, gloated, looted and handed over survivors in Jerusalem's day of disaster.
- **Verses 15–16:** The day of the Lord is near on all nations: "as you have done, it shall be done to you" (v. 15).
- **Verses 17–21:** On Mount Zion there will be deliverance and holiness. The house of Jacob will possess its land again, deliverers will rule Edom from Zion, and "the kingdom shall be the Lord's" (v. 21).

## Key people

- **Obadiah:** the prophet, "servant of the Lord".
- **Edom (Esau):** the nation descended from Jacob's twin brother Esau (Gen 25; 36).
- **Jacob:** standing for Israel and Judah, Edom's "brother".

## Key events

- Jerusalem's fall to Babylon (586 BC)
- Edom's betrayal of fleeing Judeans (vv. 11–14)
- The announced judgment of Edom on the day of the Lord (vv. 15–16)

## Themes

- **Pride before a fall:** Edom trusted its high, rocky strongholds and its wisdom, but God will bring it low (vv. 3–4, 8).
- **Betraying a brother:** the old rivalry of Jacob and Esau shows up as Edom's cruelty to Judah in its worst hour (vv. 10–14).
- **Just retribution:** "as you have done, it shall be done to you" (v. 15).
- **The day of the Lord:** a day of judgment on all the nations that oppose God and of deliverance for his people. Timmer calls it the overarching theme of the Twelve.
- **The Lord's kingdom:** the book ends with God's reign established (v. 21).

## How it points to Christ

The rivalry of Esau and Jacob runs through the Bible, all the way to Herod the Great, an Idumean (Edomite) king who tried to kill the infant Jesus (Matt 2:16). Obadiah's final word, "the kingdom shall be the Lord's", is fulfilled in Christ's kingdom (Rev 11:15). From Zion the Saviour comes, and his deliverance reaches the nations. The God who judges pride gives grace to the humble (Jas 4:6; 1 Pet 5:5).

## Common mix-ups

- Obadiah is the shortest book in the Old Testament: one chapter, 21 verses.
- This Obadiah is not the Obadiah who hid prophets from Jezebel in 1 Kings 18.
- Edom descends from Esau, Jacob's twin. That is why Edom is called Jacob's "brother".
- Obadiah 1–9 closely resembles Jeremiah 49:7–22.
- The date isn't certain. Most link it to the fall of Jerusalem in 586 BC, but some date it earlier.

## Going deeper

- **Brothers at war.** Trace Jacob and Esau from Genesis 25–33 through Numbers 20:14–21, Psalm 137:7 and Obadiah. How does family history become national history?
- **Gloating over others' misfortune (vv. 12–14).** Why is standing aloof, gloating and looting so serious to God? Compare Proverbs 24:17–18.
- **"As you have done."** How does Obadiah's principle of just retribution relate to Jesus' teaching on judgment and mercy (Matt 7:2; Gal 6:7)?
- **The nations in the Twelve.** Timmer explains that the Twelve both condemn nations that resist God and welcome those who turn to him. Where does Edom fit? Compare Amos 9:12.
- **"The kingdom shall be the Lord's."** How does this ending reframe a short, harsh book?

## Q&A

1. How long is Obadiah? One chapter of 21 verses, the shortest book in the Old Testament.
2. Against which nation does Obadiah prophesy? Edom.
3. From whom were the Edomites descended? Esau, Jacob's brother.
4. What had deceived Edom? The pride of its heart (v. 3).
5. Where did Edom think it was safe? High in the rocks, like an eagle's nest (vv. 3–4).
6. What did Edom do when Jerusalem fell? Stood aloof, gloated, looted, and handed over survivors (vv. 11–14).
7. What principle of judgment does verse 15 state? "As you have done, it shall be done to you."
8. Where will there be deliverance? On Mount Zion (v. 17).
9. What does Obadiah's name mean? "Servant of the Lord".
10. How does the book end? "The kingdom shall be the Lord's" (v. 21).
