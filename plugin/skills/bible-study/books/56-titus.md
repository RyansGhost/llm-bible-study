# Titus

## Summary

Titus is a short letter from Paul to his co-worker Titus, whom he left on the island of Crete to "put what remained into order" (1:5). Titus must appoint elders in every town, men sound in doctrine and life, and silence false teachers who are upsetting whole households. Barcley highlights the word "sound" (healthy): sound doctrine must produce sound living. Paul shows how older men, older women, younger women, young men and slaves should live, grounded in two rich summaries of the gospel: the grace of God that trains us to live godly lives (2:11–14), and God's mercy in saving us by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit (3:4–7). Luther called it a short epistle but "a model of Christian doctrine" (Lutheran Study Bible).

## Setting

- **When:** probably the early to mid-60s AD, around the same time as 1 Timothy, after Paul's release from his first Roman imprisonment (Barcley).
- **Where:** to Titus on Crete, a large Greek island in the Mediterranean. Paul plans to winter at Nicopolis (3:12).
- **Who wrote it:** Paul (1:1). Barcley defends Paul's authorship of all three Pastoral Epistles.
- **Where it sits:** one of the Pastoral Epistles, very close to 1 Timothy in its concerns: false teaching with a Jewish flavour, qualified elders, and godly living. Paul's ministry on Crete isn't mentioned in Acts, which only records his ship passing Crete on the way to Rome (Acts 27).

## Structure

- 1:1–4: Greeting: Paul's apostleship for the faith of God's elect
- 1:5–16: Appoint qualified elders; silence the false teachers
- 2:1–15: Teach what accords with sound doctrine, to every group in the church; the grace of God that trains us
- 3:1–11: Be ready for every good work; saved by God's mercy; avoid foolish controversies
- 3:12–15: Final instructions and greetings

## Chapter by chapter

- **Titus 1:** Paul greets Titus, his "true child in a common faith". Titus is to appoint elders in every town: above reproach, the husband of one wife, with believing children, not arrogant or greedy, hospitable, self-controlled, holding firm to the trustworthy word so he can teach sound doctrine and rebuke those who contradict it. Many rebellious people, especially "of the circumcision party", are upsetting whole families for shameful gain. They profess to know God but deny him by their works.
- **Titus 2:** Teach what accords with sound doctrine. Older men should be sober and sound in faith. Older women should be reverent and teach what is good, training the younger women to love their husbands and children. Young men should be self-controlled, and Titus himself a model of good works. Slaves should be trustworthy, so as to "adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour". The grace of God has appeared, training us to renounce ungodliness, while we wait for "the appearing of the glory of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ, who gave himself for us to redeem us" (2:11–14).
- **Titus 3:** Be submissive to rulers and ready for every good work, gentle and courteous to all. We were once foolish and enslaved, but when God's kindness appeared, "he saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit" (3:5), so that, justified by his grace, we might become heirs of eternal life. Avoid foolish controversies and genealogies. Warn a divisive person twice, then have nothing more to do with him. Send Zenas and Apollos on their way.

## Key people

- **Paul:** the apostle, writing to organise the churches on Crete.
- **Titus:** a Greek believer and trusted co-worker (Gal 2:3; 2 Cor 7–8), left on Crete to set the churches in order.
- **The false teachers:** "the circumcision party", empty talkers and deceivers, teaching Jewish myths for gain (1:10–16).
- **The Cretans:** described by one of their own prophets as "always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons" (1:12).
- **Artemas and Tychicus:** one of whom will be sent to relieve Titus (3:12).
- **Zenas the lawyer and Apollos:** travellers Titus is to help on their way (3:13).

## Key events

- Paul and Titus' ministry on Crete, and Titus being left to finish the work (1:5)
- Paul's plan to winter at Nicopolis (3:12)

## Themes

- **Sound doctrine and sound living:** Barcley notes "sound" appears four times in this short letter. True teaching is healthy and produces healthy lives (1:9, 13; 2:1–2, 8).
- **Qualified elders:** leaders must be sound in both doctrine and life (1:5–9).
- **False teaching:** shown up by its fruit, profession without godly works (1:16).
- **Grace that trains:** God's grace both saves and teaches us to live godly lives now (2:11–12).
- **Adorning the gospel:** Christians' conduct makes the gospel attractive to outsiders (2:5, 8, 10).
- **Salvation by mercy:** not by our works, but by God's mercy, regeneration by the Spirit and justification by grace (3:4–7).
- **Good works:** the fruit, not the root, of salvation (2:14; 3:8, 14).

## How it points to Christ

Titus has two of the New Testament's richest short summaries of the gospel. In 2:11–14, God's grace has appeared in Christ, and we wait for "the appearing of the glory of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ", one of the clearest New Testament statements that Jesus is God. He "gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works". In 3:4–7, the Father's kindness appeared, the Spirit was poured out "richly through Jesus Christ our Saviour", and we are justified by his grace and made heirs of eternal life. The title "Saviour" moves back and forth between God and Christ throughout the letter (1:3–4; 2:10, 13; 3:4, 6).

## Common mix-ups

- "Cretans are always liars" (1:12) is a quote from a Cretan poet (traditionally Epimenides), not Paul's own insult.
- "Our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ" (2:13) refers to one person, Jesus. It isn't two people, God and Christ.
- "The washing of regeneration" (3:5). Lutherans read this as baptism, which they hold works regeneration (Lutheran Study Bible). The Reformed confessions see a reference to baptism as the sign and seal, but teach that the Spirit regenerates, and that his work isn't tied to the moment of baptism (WCF 28.5–6).
- Titus is not mentioned in Acts. We know him from Galatians, 2 Corinthians and this letter.
- Elders and overseers (bishops) are the same office here (1:5–7).

## Going deeper

- **Sound doctrine, sound life (1:9; 2:1).** Why does Paul insist that doctrine and life can't be separated? Where does the church today tend to separate them?
- **Elders (1:5–9).** Compare this list with 1 Timothy 3. What does it say about the Presbyterian view that elder and overseer are one office?
- **Training in the church (2:1–10).** What does Paul expect older believers to teach younger ones? How could your church do this?
- **Grace that trains (2:11–14).** How does grace teach us to say no to ungodliness? How does this answer the charge that grace leads to careless living?
- **The washing of regeneration (3:5).** Compare the Lutheran and Reformed readings. What do the Westminster Standards say about how baptism relates to regeneration?
- **Luther on Titus.** Luther saw this short letter as covering all a Christian needs to know and live (Lutheran Study Bible). Do you agree?

## Q&A

1. Why did Paul leave Titus in Crete? To put what remained into order and appoint elders in every town (1:5).
2. What must an elder be able to do with sound doctrine? Give instruction in it and rebuke those who contradict it (1:9).
3. What did a Cretan prophet say about Cretans? "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons" (1:12).
4. How do the false teachers deny God? By their works, though they profess to know him (1:16).
5. Whom should older women train? The younger women (2:3–4).
6. What does the grace of God train us to do? Renounce ungodliness and worldly passions and live self-controlled, upright and godly lives (2:11–12).
7. What does 2:13 call Jesus Christ? Our great God and Saviour.
8. Why did Christ give himself for us, according to 2:14? To redeem us from all lawlessness and purify a people for his own possession, zealous for good works.
9. How did God save us, according to 3:5? Not because of works done by us, but according to his mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit.
10. How should Titus treat a divisive person? Warn him once and then twice, then have nothing more to do with him (3:10).
