# Malachi

## Summary

Malachi, the last book of the Old Testament, speaks to a returned people who have grown weary and cynical. They doubt God's love, offer him blemished sacrifices, break faith in marriage, rob him of tithes, and complain that serving him is useless. Through a series of disputes, God answers their objections and calls them back. He promises to send his messenger, and then to come himself to his temple, and before the great day of the Lord to send "Elijah the prophet" to turn hearts back.

## Setting

- **When:** in the Persian period, when Judah had a governor (1:8) and the rebuilt temple was in use (1:10), around the time of Ezra and Nehemiah. Some date it between Ezra's arrival (458 BC) and Nehemiah's (445 BC); others between Nehemiah's two visits, about 433 BC (Reformation Study Bible). The Lutheran Study Bible gives about 430 BC. The sins Malachi attacks, foreign marriages, unpaid tithes, corrupt priests and injustice, closely match Nehemiah 13.
- **Where:** Jerusalem and Judah under Persian rule.
- **Who wrote it:** "Malachi" means "my messenger". Some ancient translations took it as a title (the Greek has "his messenger"; an Aramaic paraphrase identifies him with Ezra). The RSB takes it as the prophet's name, since every other prophetic book is named after its prophet. Nothing else is known about him.
- **Where it sits:** last of the Twelve and, in English Bibles, the last book of the Old Testament. It speaks to a discouraged people: poor, under Persian rule, and doubting God's promises because the glorious age the prophets foretold hadn't come. About four hundred years of prophetic silence follow, until John the Baptist.

## Structure

Six disputes, each following the same pattern: God makes a statement, the people object "How?" or "But you say…", and God replies. The people's objections sum up their attitude, whether or not they said the words aloud, and God speaks unusually often in the first person (RSB). Timmer notes this dispute structure makes repentance especially prominent. The Reformation Study Bible's divisions:

- 1:1–5: "I have loved you": Jacob and Esau
- 1:6–2:9: Priests who despise God's name with blemished offerings
- 2:10–16: Faithlessness in marriage, and divorce
- 2:17–3:5: "Where is the God of justice?": the messenger and the refiner
- 3:6–12: Robbing God in tithes
- 3:13–4:6: The righteous and the wicked distinguished: "It is vain to serve God", the book of remembrance, the coming day, and the closing charge to remember Moses' law, for Elijah will come

## Chapter by chapter

- **Malachi 1:** "I have loved you, says the Lord." "How have you loved us?" God points to his choice of Jacob over Esau (Edom). The priests despise his name by offering blind, lame and sick animals. God's name will be great among the nations (1:11).
- **Malachi 2:** The priests have corrupted the covenant with Levi, whose lips should guard knowledge. The people break faith with one another, marry women who worship foreign gods, and divorce the wives of their youth: "I hate divorce" (2:16). They weary God by asking, "Where is the God of justice?"
- **Malachi 3:** God will send his messenger to prepare the way, and then the Lord will suddenly come to his temple, like a refiner's fire, to purify the sons of Levi (3:1–3). "I the Lord do not change" (3:6). They rob God in tithes and offerings: "test me", and see if I won't open the windows of heaven (3:10). Those who fear the Lord are written in a book of remembrance and will be his treasured possession.
- **Malachi 4:** The day is coming, burning like an oven, for the arrogant. For those who fear his name "the sun of righteousness shall rise with healing in its wings" (4:2). Remember the law of Moses. God will send Elijah before the great day of the Lord, to turn the hearts of fathers to children and children to fathers (4:5–6).

## Key people

- **Malachi:** "my messenger", the last Old Testament prophet.
- **The priests (sons of Levi):** corrupt in their worship and teaching.
- **Jacob and Esau:** the brothers whose descendants show God's electing love (1:2–3).
- **"My messenger":** the one who prepares the Lord's way (3:1).
- **Elijah the prophet:** who will come before the day of the Lord (4:5).
- **Those who fear the Lord:** the faithful remnant, recorded in God's book (3:16–18).

## Key events

- God's dispute with his people over love, worship, marriage, justice and tithes (1–3)
- The promise of the messenger and the Lord's coming (3:1)
- The book of remembrance (3:16)
- The promise of Elijah (4:5–6)

## Themes

- **God's covenant love:** the book begins with "I have loved you" (1:2). God's love is shown in his electing choice.
- **Covenant faithfulness:** Malachi names the covenant with Levi, the covenant of the fathers, the marriage covenant, and "the messenger of the covenant" (2:5, 10, 14; 3:1). Unfaithful priests led to a people who broke faith with God and one another, and stayed under the covenant curse unless they returned (3:7–9).
- **Honouring God in worship:** God deserves our best, not leftovers (1:6–14).
- **Faithful leaders:** priests must teach truth and walk with God (2:4–9).
- **Faithfulness in marriage:** God witnesses the marriage covenant and hates the treachery of divorce (2:13–16).
- **God's unchanging faithfulness:** "I the Lord do not change; therefore you … are not consumed" (3:6).
- **Repentance:** "Return to me, and I will return to you" (3:7). Timmer stresses Malachi's call to return.
- **The day of the Lord:** judgment for the arrogant, and healing for those who fear God (4:1–3). Timmer distinguishes the returned community from the final, purified remnant that day will reveal.

## How it points to Christ

The New Testament opens where Malachi ends. The messenger who prepares the way (3:1) is John the Baptist (Mark 1:2; Matt 11:10), and Jesus says John is the promised "Elijah" (4:5; Matt 11:14; 17:10–13). The angel told Zechariah that John would go "in the spirit and power of Elijah, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children" (Luke 1:17, quoting 4:6).

The Lord who suddenly comes to his temple (3:1) is Jesus, presented there as a baby and later cleansing it (Luke 2:22–32; John 2:13–17). Zechariah's song calls him "the sunrise from on high" who visits his people (Luke 1:78), echoing "the sun of righteousness" (4:2). Paul quotes "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated" in explaining God's sovereign grace (Rom 9:13).

## Common mix-ups

- Malachi is the last book of the English Old Testament, but in the Hebrew Bible the last book is Chronicles.
- In Hebrew Bibles Malachi has three chapters; the English chapter 4 is Hebrew 3:19–24.
- "I hate divorce" (2:16) is God speaking against treacherous divorce, not condemning divorced people.
- "Test me … the windows of heaven" (3:10) is about tithes withheld from God's house, not a guarantee of wealth.
- The promised "Elijah" isn't Elijah returning in person. Jesus identifies him as John the Baptist (Matt 11:14), though Elijah also appears at the transfiguration.

## Going deeper

- **The dispute pattern.** Malachi's hearers keep answering God with "How?" Timmer notes this structure highlights repentance. Which of their objections sound familiar today?
- **"Jacob I loved, Esau I hated" (1:2–3).** What does this say about God's electing love? How does Paul use it in Romans 9?
- **Giving God our best (1:6–14).** God says even a governor wouldn't accept such gifts. What does "blemished offerings" look like in worship and service today?
- **Marriage as covenant (2:13–16).** How does Malachi tie faithfulness to one's spouse with faithfulness to God? Compare Jesus in Matthew 19:3–9.
- **The messenger and Elijah (3:1; 4:5–6).** How do the Gospels show John the Baptist fulfilling these promises? Why does the Old Testament end looking forward?

## Q&A

1. What does "Malachi" mean? "My messenger".
2. What is God's first statement to the people? "I have loved you" (1:2).
3. What were the priests offering to God? Blind, lame and sick animals (1:8).
4. What does God say he hates in 2:16? Divorce: the treacherous breaking of the marriage covenant.
5. Who will God send to prepare his way? His messenger (3:1), fulfilled in John the Baptist.
6. Like what will the Lord be when he comes to purify his people? A refiner's fire and fullers' soap (3:2).
7. How were the people robbing God? In tithes and offerings (3:8).
8. What was written for those who feared the Lord? A book of remembrance (3:16).
9. What will rise with healing in its wings? The sun of righteousness (4:2).
10. Whom will God send before the great day of the Lord? Elijah the prophet (4:5), whom Jesus identified as John the Baptist.
