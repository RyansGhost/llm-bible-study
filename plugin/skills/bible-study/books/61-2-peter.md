# 2 Peter

## Summary

2 Peter is Peter's farewell. He knows his death is near (1:14), so he writes to remind believers of the truth and warn them against false teachers. God's divine power has given them everything they need for life and godliness, so they must add virtue upon virtue to their faith and make their calling and election sure. Their faith rests not on clever myths but on eyewitness testimony to Christ's majesty and on the prophetic word, which came from God as men were carried along by the Holy Spirit. False teachers will arise, greedy and immoral, and God will judge them as surely as he judged the fallen angels, the ancient world and Sodom. Scoffers mock the promise of Christ's coming, but the Lord isn't slow: he is patient, not wanting any to perish. The day of the Lord will come like a thief, so grow in grace and knowledge.

## Setting

- **When:** in the mid-60s AD, probably AD 65–67, shortly before Peter's martyrdom under Nero (Kistemaker[^btn]; *RSB*[^rsb]). Peter refers to an earlier letter, probably 1 Peter (3:1), which would make the readers the same churches in Asia Minor.
- **Where:** the letter doesn't say where it was written. Tradition places Peter in Rome at the end of his life, the likeliest setting.
- **Who wrote it:** Simon Peter (1:1), who was on the mount of transfiguration (1:16–18). Kistemaker[^btn] notes most critical scholars think it was written by someone else in the late first or early second century. He makes the case for Peter, explaining the difference in style from 1 Peter by the use of a different secretary and a different subject. The letter's own details fit Peter: his coming death as Jesus foretold (1:14; John 21:18–19) and his presence at the transfiguration. The *RSB*[^rsb] adds that the early church rejected letters written under false names, which were almost always heretical.
- **Where it sits:** one of the General Epistles. Kistemaker[^btn] notes it had perhaps the hardest road into the canon of any New Testament book, being used rarely in the earliest centuries, though some early writers knew it; Origen is the first to name Peter as its author, while noting others' doubts. It combines a letter with a farewell "testament", a known Jewish form. It shares much material with Jude.

## Structure

Kistemaker's[^btn] outline (the *RSB*[^rsb] treats 1:3–3:18a as one body: the truth of the gospel as the foundation for standing against false teachers):

- 1:1–2: Greeting
- 1:3–11: Promises and virtues: growth and assurance
- 1:12–21: Divine revelation: memory, eyewitnesses and prophecy
- 2:1–22: False teachers: their heresies, conduct, condemnation and doom
- 3:1–13: The day of the Lord: scoffers, God's patience, and the new heavens and earth
- 3:14–18: Final exhortations: be blameless, accept God's truth, reject error, grow in grace

## Chapter by chapter

- **2 Peter 1:** God's divine power has granted us all things that pertain to life and godliness, and precious promises so that we may become partakers of the divine nature. So add to your faith virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection and love (1:5–7). Make your calling and election sure. Peter will soon put off his body, so he reminds them. They didn't follow cleverly devised myths: Peter was an eyewitness of Christ's majesty on the holy mountain and heard the Father's voice. "No prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit" (1:21).
- **2 Peter 2:** As there were false prophets in Israel, there will be false teachers among you, denying the Master who bought them. In greed they will exploit you. God didn't spare the angels who sinned, the ancient world in Noah's flood, or Sodom and Gomorrah, but he rescued Noah and righteous Lot. So the Lord knows how to rescue the godly and keep the unrighteous for judgment. The false teachers are bold, sensual and greedy, following the way of Balaam. They promise freedom but are slaves of corruption. It would have been better for them never to have known the way of righteousness.
- **2 Peter 3:** Scoffers will come saying, "Where is the promise of his coming?" They forget the world was formed by God's word and destroyed by water. The present heavens and earth are stored up for fire. With the Lord one day is as a thousand years. The Lord isn't slow to fulfil his promise but is patient, not wishing that any should perish (3:9). The day of the Lord will come like a thief, and the heavens will pass away. We wait for new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells. Paul's letters contain some things hard to understand, which the ignorant twist. Grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.

## Key people

- **Simon Peter:** a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, facing death (1:1, 14).
- **The false teachers:** greedy and immoral, denying the Master who bought them (2:1–3).
- **The scoffers:** who mock the promise of Christ's return (3:3–4).
- **Noah:** "a herald of righteousness", preserved through the flood (2:5).
- **Lot:** righteous, distressed by Sodom's wickedness, and rescued (2:7–8).
- **Balaam:** the prophet who loved gain from wrongdoing, rebuked by a donkey (2:15–16).
- **Paul:** "our beloved brother", whose letters are Scripture (3:15–16).

## Key events

- The transfiguration of Jesus, witnessed by Peter (1:16–18)
- The judgment of the angels who sinned (2:4)
- The flood in Noah's day (2:5; 3:6)
- The destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah and the rescue of Lot (2:6–8)
- The coming day of the Lord and the new heavens and new earth (3:10–13)

## Themes

- **Growing in godliness:** God has given everything needed, so believers must make every effort to grow (1:3–11; 3:18).
- **Remembering the truth:** Peter writes to stir them up by way of reminder before he dies (1:12–15; 3:1–2).
- **The reliability of Scripture:** apostolic eyewitness and Spirit-inspired prophecy (1:16–21). Paul's letters are counted as Scripture (3:16).
- **False teachers:** marked by denying Christ, greed, sensuality and contempt for authority (2). Kistemaker[^btn] identifies refuting them as the main purpose of the letter. They came from within the church, and their teaching may have been an early form of what became Gnosticism, treating the body as unimportant and so indulging it (*RSB*[^rsb]).
- **The last days have begun:** the false teachers and scoffers are themselves a sign that the "last days" foretold by the prophets, Jesus and the apostles have arrived (3:2–3).
- **God's judgment and rescue:** God knows how to rescue the godly and keep the unrighteous for judgment (2:9).
- **The certainty of Christ's return:** scoffers are wrong; God's timing is patient, not slow (3:3–10).
- **New creation:** new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells (3:13).
- **Grow, and watch out:** the *Reformation Heritage KJV Study Bible* (*RH*[^rh]) pairs the letter's two concerns: believers must keep growing in grace (1:5–11; 3:18), and must see through the false teachers who had slipped in among them (2:1–3). *RH*[^rh] answers the arguments against Peter's authorship, and dates it near his death, about AD 64–67.

## How it points to Christ

From its first verse, 2 Peter calls Jesus "our God and Saviour Jesus Christ" (1:1). His divine power gives us everything for life and godliness (1:3). Peter saw his majesty on the mount of transfiguration and heard the Father say, "This is my beloved Son" (1:17). Christ is the "morning star" who rises in our hearts (1:19), and the Master who bought his people (2:1). His coming is certain, and his apparent delay is God's patience giving time for repentance (3:9, 15). The letter ends with praise to him: "To him be the glory both now and to the day of eternity" (3:18).

## Common mix-ups

- "With the Lord one day is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day" (3:8) is 2 Peter, echoing Psalm 90:4. It is about God's patience, not a formula for dating creation or the end.
- "Not wishing that any should perish" (3:9). Reformed interpreters often read "any" in context as any of God's people ("patient toward you"), or as God's genuine desire that doesn't override his decree. Either way, it doesn't teach that everyone will be saved.
- "Partakers of the divine nature" (1:4) means sharing in God's moral character and life, not becoming God.
- "Men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit" (1:21) is 2 Peter. "All Scripture is breathed out by God" is 2 Timothy 3:16.
- 2 Peter 2 and Jude cover much the same ground. Scholars debate which used the other.
- "Denying the Master who bought them" (2:1) is debated. Reformed interpreters who hold to definite atonement read it as the false teachers' profession of being bought, or as Christ's ownership as Master, not as proof that Christ died to save people who are finally lost.

## Going deeper

- **Who wrote 2 Peter?** Kistemaker[^btn] sets out the case against Peter's authorship and his answers. How did the early church come to accept it as canon?
- **Adding to faith (1:5–11).** Why does Peter list these virtues? How does "make your calling and election sure" relate to assurance of salvation (WCF 18)?
- **Eyewitness and prophecy (1:16–21).** What two grounds does Peter give for trusting the gospel? How does 1:21 shape the doctrine of Scripture?
- **Marks of false teachers (2).** What does Peter say false teachers deny, love and promise? Where do these marks appear today?
- **The delay of the second coming (3:3–10).** How does Peter answer the scoffers? How should we think about God's timing?
- **Paul as Scripture (3:15–16).** What does it mean that Peter places Paul's letters alongside "the other Scriptures"?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** Why did Peter write 2 Peter?

   **A:** To remind believers of the truth before his death and to warn them against false teachers (1:12–15; 2:1).

2. **Q:** What has God's divine power granted us?

   **A:** All things that pertain to life and godliness (1:3).

3. **Q:** What qualities should believers add to their faith?

   **A:** Virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection and love (1:5–7).

4. **Q:** What event did Peter witness on the holy mountain?

   **A:** The transfiguration, when he heard the Father's voice (1:16–18).

5. **Q:** How was prophecy produced, according to 1:21?

   **A:** Men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

6. **Q:** Which three examples of God's judgment does chapter 2 give?

   **A:** The angels who sinned, the ancient world in the flood, and Sodom and Gomorrah (2:4–6).

7. **Q:** Which two righteous people did God rescue?

   **A:** Noah and Lot (2:5–8).

8. **Q:** What do the scoffers ask?

   **A:** "Where is the promise of his coming?" (3:4).

9. **Q:** Why hasn't the Lord come yet?

   **A:** He is patient, not wishing that any should perish but that all should reach repentance (3:9).

10. **Q:** What do believers wait for, according to 3:13?

    **A:** New heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells.
