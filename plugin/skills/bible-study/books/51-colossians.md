# Colossians

## Sources

- Benjamin Gladd, "Colossians," in Michael J. Kruger, ed., *A Biblical-Theological Introduction to the New Testament: The Gospel Realized* (Crossway, 2016): Background Issues (occasion, authorship, date), Structure and Outline, Message and Theology. Print pages to verify.
- Reformation Study Bible, introduction to Colossians, p. ___ (to verify)

## Summary

Colossians is short but rich, and its message is simple: Christ is better (Gladd). He is better than any human philosophy, outdated religious regulations, or spiritual powers. Paul celebrates Christ as the image of the invisible God, Creator and Lord of all, in whom all the fullness of God dwells bodily. Through his cross Christ has forgiven our sins and triumphed over every power. So believers don't need to add anything to him: they are to set their minds on things above and live out their new life in Christ in every relationship.

## Setting

- **When:** about AD 60–62, from prison (4:3, 18). Gladd discusses which imprisonment: Rome is the traditional view; Ephesus and Caesarea have also been proposed.
- **Where:** Colossae, a small town in the Lycus Valley of Asia Minor, near Laodicea and Hierapolis. Paul hadn't visited it. Epaphras had planted the church (1:7; 2:1).
- **Who wrote it:** Paul, with Timothy (1:1). Gladd notes that some modern scholars dispute this because of differences in style and theology and its similarity to Ephesians, while defending Paul's authorship.
- **Where it sits:** one of the Prison Epistles, closely tied to Philemon (sharing many names, and Onesimus) and to Ephesians (similar wording). Tychicus carried it (4:7–9).

## Structure

- 1:1–14: Greeting, thanksgiving and prayer
- 1:15–23: The supremacy of Christ, and reconciliation through him
- 1:24–2:5: Paul's ministry of the mystery: "Christ in you, the hope of glory"
- 2:6–23: Warning against false teaching: you are complete in Christ
- 3:1–4:6: The new life: seek things above; put off and put on; households; prayer and wise speech
- 4:7–18: Greetings and instructions

## Chapter by chapter

- **Colossians 1:** Paul thanks God for their faith, love and hope from the gospel, which is bearing fruit worldwide. He prays they will be filled with knowledge and walk worthy of the Lord. Christ is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation: all things were created through him and for him, he is head of the church, and in him all fullness dwells (1:15–20). Through his cross he reconciles all things. Paul serves the mystery: "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27).
- **Colossians 2:** In Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. Don't be taken captive by philosophy and empty deceit. In him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily (2:9), and you are filled in him. You were buried with him in baptism and raised with him. He cancelled the record of debt, nailing it to the cross, and disarmed the powers (2:14–15). Don't let anyone judge you over food, festivals or Sabbaths, which are shadows, or impose self-made religion and angel worship.
- **Colossians 3:** Since you have been raised with Christ, seek the things above (3:1–4). Put to death what is earthly and put off anger and lies. Put on compassion, kindness, humility, patience, forgiveness, and above all love. Let Christ's peace rule and his word dwell in you richly. Wives, husbands, children, fathers and servants: whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord.
- **Colossians 4:** Masters must treat servants justly. Continue in prayer. Walk wisely toward outsiders and season your speech with grace. Greetings from Tychicus, Onesimus, Aristarchus, Mark, Luke, Demas and Epaphras. Exchange letters with Laodicea.

## Key people

- **Paul:** a prisoner writing to a church he hadn't visited.
- **Timothy:** co-sender.
- **Epaphras:** who founded the church at Colossae and prays earnestly for it (1:7; 4:12–13).
- **Tychicus:** the letter-carrier (4:7).
- **Onesimus:** a runaway slave, now a "faithful and beloved brother" (4:9; see Philemon).
- **Aristarchus, Mark, Justus, Luke and Demas:** Paul's companions who send greetings (4:10–14).
- **Archippus:** told to fulfil his ministry (4:17).
- **Nympha:** who hosts a church in her house (4:15).

## Key events

- The founding of the church by Epaphras (1:7)
- Paul's imprisonment (4:3, 18)
- Tychicus and Onesimus sent with the letter (4:7–9)

## Themes

- **The supremacy of Christ:** Gladd sums up the letter as "Christ is better". He is Creator, sustainer, head of the church, and firstborn from the dead (1:15–20).
- **The fullness of God in Christ:** in him all the fullness of deity dwells bodily (2:9). Believers lack nothing in him.
- **Warning against false teaching:** human philosophy, Jewish regulations (food, festivals, Sabbaths) seen as shadows now that Christ has come, angel worship and harsh self-denial (2:8, 16–23).
- **Union with Christ:** died, buried, raised and hidden with Christ (2:12; 3:1–3).
- **The cross's victory:** our debt cancelled and the powers disarmed (2:13–15).
- **New life:** put off the old self, put on the new (3:5–17).
- **Christ in everyday life:** households and work done for the Lord (3:18–4:1).
- **Prayer and witness:** steadfast prayer and gracious speech to outsiders (4:2–6).

## How it points to Christ

Colossians contains one of the New Testament's highest descriptions of Christ (1:15–20):

- the image of the invisible God
- the firstborn over all creation
- the one through whom and for whom all things were created, and in whom they hold together
- head of the church
- firstborn from the dead, so that he is pre-eminent in everything
- the one in whom all God's fullness was pleased to dwell
- the one through whom God reconciled all things by the blood of his cross

The Old Testament food laws, festivals and Sabbaths were "a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ" (2:17). Christ in us is the hope of glory (1:27). When he appears, we will appear with him in glory (3:4).

## Common mix-ups

- "Firstborn over all creation" (1:15) means Christ's supreme rank and inheritance, not that he was created. Verse 16 says all things were created through him.
- Colossians and Ephesians share much wording and were sent together. Colossians focuses on Christ's supremacy, Ephesians on the church.
- Paul hadn't visited Colossae. Epaphras founded the church.
- The "letter from Laodicea" (4:16) hasn't survived, though some think it may be Ephesians.
- Onesimus in Colossians 4:9 is the same runaway slave as in Philemon.
- "Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord" is Colossians 3:23.

## Going deeper

- **The Christ hymn (1:15–20).** What does each line claim about Christ? How did the early church use texts like this against those who denied his full deity?
- **What was the "Colossian heresy"?** Gladd notes it seems to have mixed Jewish regulations, human philosophy, angel worship and asceticism (2:8–23). Why does Paul answer every element with Christ?
- **Shadow and substance (2:16–17).** How does Paul view Old Testament food laws, festivals and Sabbaths? How do Reformed churches apply this to the Lord's Day?
- **Baptism and circumcision (2:11–12).** How does this passage shape the Reformed understanding of baptism as the sign of the new covenant?
- **Seeking things above (3:1–4).** What does it mean in practice to set your mind on things above, while living fully on earth?
- **Who wrote Colossians?** Gladd surveys the case for and against Paul's authorship. What evidence decides it?

## Q&A

1. Who founded the church at Colossae? Epaphras (1:7).
2. What does Colossians 1:15 call Christ? The image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.
3. Through whom and for whom were all things created? Christ (1:16).
4. What is the mystery Paul proclaims? "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27).
5. What dwells bodily in Christ? The whole fullness of deity (2:9).
6. What did God do with the record of debt against us? Cancelled it, nailing it to the cross (2:14).
7. What are food laws, festivals and Sabbaths, compared with Christ? A shadow; the substance belongs to Christ (2:17).
8. Since we have been raised with Christ, what should we seek? The things above, where Christ is seated (3:1).
9. What should we put on above all? Love, which binds everything together in harmony (3:14).
10. Which runaway slave is mentioned in Colossians? Onesimus (4:9).
