# Hebrews

## Summary

Hebrews is a sermon in letter form (the author calls it a "word of exhortation", 13:22), written to Jewish Christians tempted to drift back from Christ to the old ways. Its message is that Jesus is better: better than the angels, Moses, Joshua and Aaron, with a better priesthood, a better covenant, a better sanctuary and a better sacrifice. Kistemaker notes that Hebrews fills a gap in the New Testament: it is the only book that fully explains Jesus as our High Priest, after the order of Melchizedek, who offered himself once for all. Theology and warning alternate throughout: don't drift, don't harden your hearts, don't fall away, but hold fast, draw near, and run the race looking to Jesus.

## Setting

- **When:** probably shortly before AD 70. Kistemaker notes that Clement of Rome quotes it about AD 96, and the author speaks of temple sacrifices in the present tense (9:6–7; 10:11), suggesting the temple still stands.
- **Where:** unknown. "Those who come from Italy send you greetings" (13:24) probably means Italians abroad greeting friends at home, so the readers may have been in or near Rome (Kistemaker).
- **Who wrote it:** unknown. It was long regarded as Paul's, but Kistemaker gives strong reasons against that: Hebrews is anonymous, lacks Paul's usual greetings and phrases, and its author heard the gospel from those who heard the Lord (2:3), unlike Paul. Suggestions include Barnabas, Apollos, Luke and others. Origen's verdict, "only God knows", still stands.
- **Where it sits:** the first of the General Epistles. The readers were second-generation believers who had endured persecution earlier (10:32–34), but some were now neglecting to meet together (10:25) and growing weak (12:12).

## Structure

Kistemaker structures Hebrews around the superiority of Christ:

- 1:1–2:18: Jesus' superiority to the angels; Saviour and High Priest
- 3:1–4:13: Jesus' superiority to Moses; the warning against unbelief; God's rest
- 4:14–5:10: Jesus' high priesthood
- 5:11–6:20: Exhortations: don't fall away; hold on to God's promise
- 7:1–28: Jesus, a priest like Melchizedek
- 8:1–10:18: Jesus, High Priest and sacrifice: the heavenly sanctuary, the new covenant, his once-for-all sacrifice
- 10:19–39: More exhortations: persevere, take warning, keep going
- 11:1–40: The heroes of faith
- 12:1–13:25: Discipline, warning, community life, and closing blessing

## Chapter by chapter

- **Hebrews 1:** Long ago God spoke by the prophets, but in these last days by his Son, heir of all things, through whom he made the world. The Son is the radiance of God's glory and the exact imprint of his nature. After making purification for sins, he sat down at the right hand of God (1:1–3). He is superior to the angels, who worship him.
- **Hebrews 2:** So don't drift away from so great a salvation. Jesus was made for a little while lower than the angels and tasted death for everyone. He shared our flesh and blood to destroy the devil and free those enslaved by fear of death. He became like his brothers to be a merciful and faithful high priest.
- **Hebrews 3:** Jesus is worthy of more glory than Moses, as a son over a house is greater than a servant in it. "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts" (Ps 95). Israel's generation in the wilderness couldn't enter God's rest because of unbelief.
- **Hebrews 4:** A Sabbath rest remains for the people of God. Strive to enter it. God's word is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword. We have a great high priest who sympathises with our weaknesses, tempted in every way yet without sin, so draw near to the throne of grace with confidence (4:14–16).
- **Hebrews 5:** Every high priest is chosen from among men and appointed by God. Christ was appointed a priest "after the order of Melchizedek" (Ps 110:4). He learned obedience through what he suffered and became the source of eternal salvation. The readers have become dull of hearing and need milk, not solid food.
- **Hebrews 6:** Go on to maturity. It is impossible to restore again to repentance those who have tasted the heavenly gift and then fallen away (6:4–6). But the author is sure of better things for them. God's promise to Abraham, confirmed by an oath, is a sure anchor for the soul.
- **Hebrews 7:** Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, received tithes from Abraham, so he is greater than Levi. A change of priesthood means a change of law. Jesus holds his priesthood permanently, by the power of an indestructible life, and always lives to make intercession for us (7:25).
- **Hebrews 8:** Christ ministers in the true tabernacle, of which the earthly one was a copy and shadow. He mediates a better covenant: the new covenant promised in Jeremiah 31, with the law written on hearts and sins remembered no more. The old is becoming obsolete.
- **Hebrews 9:** The earthly tabernacle and its yearly Day of Atonement couldn't perfect the conscience. Christ entered the heavenly holy place once for all by his own blood, securing eternal redemption. Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. He appeared once to put away sin, and will appear a second time to save those who wait for him (9:28).
- **Hebrews 10:** The law's sacrifices, repeated every year, could never take away sins. Christ offered one sacrifice for all time and sat down. So draw near with a true heart, hold fast your confession, and don't neglect meeting together (10:19–25). There is no sacrifice left for those who go on sinning deliberately. Don't throw away your confidence.
- **Hebrews 11:** "Faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen" (11:1). Without faith it is impossible to please God. By faith Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Rahab, and many more lived and died, looking for a better country. They didn't receive what was promised, so that apart from us they shouldn't be made perfect.
- **Hebrews 12:** Surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, run with endurance, looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith (12:1–2). God disciplines those he loves, as a father. Strive for peace and holiness. Don't be like Esau. You haven't come to fiery Mount Sinai but to Mount Zion, to Jesus and his sprinkled blood. Our God is a consuming fire.
- **Hebrews 13:** Let brotherly love continue. Show hospitality, remember prisoners, honour marriage, be content. "I will never leave you nor forsake you". Remember and obey your leaders. "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:8). Go to him outside the camp. Offer a sacrifice of praise. A closing blessing from the God of peace, who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep. Timothy has been released.

## Key people

- **Jesus:** the Son, the great High Priest, mediator of the new covenant.
- **The angels:** ministering spirits, inferior to the Son (1:4–14).
- **Moses:** faithful as a servant in God's house (3:1–6).
- **Joshua:** who didn't give Israel the final rest (4:8).
- **Aaron:** the first high priest, whose priesthood was imperfect (5:4; 7:11).
- **Melchizedek:** king of Salem and priest of God Most High, a type of Christ (Gen 14:18–20; Heb 7).
- **The heroes of faith:** Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Rahab, Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel and the prophets (11).
- **Esau:** a warning example, who sold his birthright (12:16–17).
- **Timothy:** recently released from prison (13:23).

## Key events

- The giving of the law through angels at Sinai (2:2; 12:18–21)
- Israel's unbelief in the wilderness (3:7–19)
- Abraham's meeting with Melchizedek (7:1–10)
- The Day of Atonement ritual (9:6–10)
- Christ's once-for-all sacrifice and ascension (9:11–14, 24–28; 10:12)
- The readers' earlier persecution (10:32–34)

## Themes

- **The superiority of Christ:** better than angels, Moses, Joshua and Aaron, with a better covenant, better promises, a better sacrifice and a better hope (Kistemaker's macro-theme).
- **Christ's high priesthood:** Kistemaker shows Hebrews is the only New Testament book to develop it fully: a priest after the order of Melchizedek, sympathetic, sinless, eternal (4:14–5:10; 7).
- **The once-for-all sacrifice:** Christ's single offering completes what the repeated sacrifices couldn't (9:11–10:18).
- **The new covenant:** promised in Jeremiah 31 and inaugurated by Christ's blood (8:6–13; 10:15–18).
- **Shadow and reality:** the tabernacle and its rituals were copies and shadows of heavenly realities (8:5; 10:1).
- **Warning and perseverance:** five warning passages urge the readers not to drift, harden their hearts or fall away (2:1–4; 3:7–4:13; 5:11–6:12; 10:26–31; 12:25–29).
- **Faith:** trusting God's promises for what is unseen and future (11).
- **God's word:** God has spoken finally in his Son (1:1–2), and his word is living and active (4:12).

## How it points to Christ

Hebrews is perhaps the most thoroughly Christ-centred interpretation of the Old Testament in the Bible. Jesus is God's final word (1:1–2), the radiance of God's glory (1:3), the Son whom angels worship. He is the true rest Joshua couldn't give (4:8–10). He is the priest after the order of Melchizedek promised in Psalm 110, holding his office forever (7:24–25). He is both the priest and the sacrifice, entering the heavenly sanctuary with his own blood (9:11–12). The tabernacle, the Day of Atonement, the covenant at Sinai and the faith of the patriarchs all pointed to him. He is "the founder and perfecter of our faith" (12:2), and "the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:8).

## Common mix-ups

- Paul probably didn't write Hebrews, though the King James Version's title says he did. The author is unknown.
- "Faith is the substance of things hoped for" is Hebrews 11:1.
- "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" is Hebrews 13:8.
- Hebrews 6:4–6 is hard. Reformed interpreters usually read it as describing people who had shared in the church's blessings without true saving faith, so it doesn't teach that the truly regenerate can be lost (WCF 17). Lutherans hold that true believers can fall from faith. All agree the warning is real and meant to be heeded.
- Melchizedek isn't an angel or a pre-incarnate Christ in most Reformed readings. He was a real king-priest whose lack of a recorded genealogy makes him a type of Christ's eternal priesthood (7:3).
- "Do not neglect to meet together" (10:25) is Hebrews, not Acts.
- "Entertained angels unawares" (13:2) refers back to Abraham (Gen 18).

## Going deeper

- **Who wrote Hebrews?** Kistemaker sets out the case against Paul and the main alternatives. Why might God have left the author unnamed?
- **Why a priest?** Kistemaker notes the Gospels present Jesus as prophet and king, and Hebrews fills in the priest. How does the threefold office of Christ (WSC 23–26) draw on Hebrews?
- **Melchizedek (Gen 14; Ps 110; Heb 7).** Trace the thread from Genesis to Hebrews. Why does the author make so much of such a brief figure?
- **The warning passages.** Read 6:4–8 and 10:26–31 with 6:9–12 and 10:39. How do Reformed and Lutheran readers understand these warnings? How do warnings and assurance work together?
- **Shadow and substance.** What does Hebrews teach about how Christians should read Leviticus and the tabernacle?
- **The cloud of witnesses (11–12).** How do the heroes of chapter 11 encourage endurance? What do they have in common?

## Q&A

1. How has God spoken in these last days? By his Son (1:2).
2. What does 1:3 call the Son? The radiance of God's glory and the exact imprint of his nature.
3. To whom is Jesus shown superior in chapters 1–4? The angels, Moses and Joshua.
4. What does 4:15 say about our high priest? He sympathises with our weaknesses and was tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin.
5. After whose order is Jesus a priest? Melchizedek's (5:6; Ps 110:4).
6. Which Old Testament prophecy of the new covenant does Hebrews quote? Jeremiah 31:31–34 (Heb 8:8–12).
7. How many times did Christ offer himself for sins? Once for all (9:26–28; 10:10–12).
8. How does Hebrews 11:1 define faith? The assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen.
9. Who is the founder and perfecter of our faith? Jesus (12:2).
10. What does 13:8 say about Jesus Christ? He is the same yesterday and today and forever.
