# 2 Timothy

## Summary

2 Timothy is Paul's last surviving letter, written from a Roman prison as he waits to be executed. Barcley sums up its theme as "a call to faithfulness". Paul wants the gospel passed on faithfully after the apostles are gone, and he wants Timothy to persevere despite suffering and false teaching. He urges Timothy not to be ashamed, to guard the good deposit, to entrust it to faithful men, to endure hardship like a soldier, and to preach the word, which is breathed out by God. Paul faces death with confidence: he has fought the good fight, finished the race and kept the faith. Luther called it a farewell letter (Lutheran Study Bible).

## Setting

- **When:** about AD 64–67 (the Lutheran Study Bible gives AD 68), during Paul's second Roman imprisonment, shortly before his martyrdom under Nero.
- **Where:** from prison in Rome (1:8, 16–17) to Timothy, probably still in Ephesus (1:18; 4:19).
- **Who wrote it:** Paul (1:1). Barcley notes that even some scholars who deny Paul wrote 1 Timothy and Titus accept 2 Timothy because it is so personal.
- **Where it sits:** the last of the Pastoral Epistles, more intimate than 1 Timothy and Titus, and less about church order.

## Structure

Barcley's outline:

- 1:1–18: Faithfulness in ministry: guard the good deposit
- 2:1–13: Faithfulness in hardship: soldier, athlete, farmer
- 2:14–4:5: Faithfulness in the face of false teaching: rightly handling the word, the last days, Scripture, "preach the word"
- 4:6–22: Paul's faithfulness and his faithful Lord

## Chapter by chapter

- **2 Timothy 1:** Paul thanks God for Timothy's sincere faith, first found in his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice. Fan into flame the gift of God, for God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power, love and self-control (1:7). Don't be ashamed of the gospel or of Paul. God saved us by his own purpose and grace, given in Christ before the ages began. Guard the good deposit. Everyone in Asia deserted Paul, but Onesiphorus refreshed him.
- **2 Timothy 2:** Entrust what you heard to faithful men who will teach others also (2:2). Share in suffering as a good soldier, an athlete who competes by the rules, a hard-working farmer. Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead. "If we are faithless, he remains faithful" (2:13). Rightly handle the word of truth. Avoid irreverent babble like that of Hymenaeus and Philetus, who say the resurrection has already happened. Flee youthful passions and correct opponents with gentleness.
- **2 Timothy 3:** In the last days people will be lovers of self, lovers of money, having the appearance of godliness but denying its power. All who desire to live a godly life will be persecuted. Continue in what you have learned. From childhood you have known the sacred writings, which make you wise for salvation. "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness" (3:16).
- **2 Timothy 4:** "Preach the word; be ready in season and out of season" (4:2), because people will gather teachers to suit their own passions. Paul is being poured out as a drink offering: "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith" (4:7). A crown of righteousness awaits him and all who love Christ's appearing. Demas has deserted him; only Luke is with him. Bring Mark, the cloak and the parchments. At his first defence no one stood by him, but the Lord stood by him.

## Key people

- **Paul:** in chains in Rome, facing death.
- **Timothy:** Paul's "beloved child" (1:2), urged to come quickly (4:9, 21).
- **Lois and Eunice:** Timothy's grandmother and mother, whose sincere faith he shares (1:5).
- **Onesiphorus:** who searched for Paul in Rome and wasn't ashamed of his chains (1:16–18).
- **Hymenaeus and Philetus:** false teachers who said the resurrection had already happened (2:17–18).
- **Demas:** who deserted Paul, "in love with this present world" (4:10).
- **Luke:** the only one with Paul (4:11).
- **Mark:** once a cause of division, now "very useful" to Paul (4:11; Acts 15:37–39).
- **Alexander the coppersmith:** who did Paul great harm (4:14).

## Key events

- Paul's second imprisonment in Rome and his "first defence" (4:16–17)
- The desertion of Paul by those in Asia and by Demas (1:15; 4:10)
- Paul's expectation of his coming execution (4:6–8)

## Themes

- **A call to faithfulness:** Barcley's summary of the letter, faithful in ministry, in hardship, against false teaching, to the end.
- **Guarding and passing on the gospel:** the "good deposit" handed from Paul to Timothy to faithful men to others (1:13–14; 2:2).
- **Not ashamed:** of the gospel, of the Lord, or of those who suffer for him (1:8, 12, 16).
- **Suffering:** all who live godly lives will be persecuted, and Christ is the pattern of suffering before glory (2:8–12; 3:12).
- **The authority and sufficiency of Scripture:** God-breathed and able to make us wise for salvation and equip us for every good work (3:14–17).
- **Preaching:** the central task of the minister (4:1–5).
- **God's faithfulness:** he remains faithful even when we are faithless, and he will rescue his people and bring them into his heavenly kingdom (2:13; 4:18).

## How it points to Christ

Paul begins with the grace given to us in Christ Jesus before the ages began, now revealed through his appearing: he "abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel" (1:9–10). Timothy is told to "remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, the offspring of David" (2:8), the summary of Paul's gospel. "If we have died with him, we will also live with him; if we endure, we will also reign with him" (2:11–12). Christ is the judge of the living and the dead (4:1) and the Lord who stood by Paul when everyone else left (4:17). He will give the crown of righteousness to all who have loved his appearing (4:8).

## Common mix-ups

- "All Scripture is breathed out by God" (3:16) is 2 Timothy, not 2 Peter (2 Peter 1:21 says the prophets spoke from God as carried along by the Holy Spirit).
- "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith" is 2 Timothy 4:7. "Fight the good fight of the faith" is 1 Timothy 6:12.
- "God gave us a spirit not of fear" is 2 Timothy 1:7.
- 2 Timothy is Paul's last letter, not 1 Timothy. Paul is near death.
- "Study to shew thyself approved" (2:15, KJV) is about rightly handling God's word, not academic study in general.
- Mark, whom Paul had refused to take on his second journey (Acts 15), is now called "very useful" (4:11).

## Going deeper

- **Passing on the faith (1:13–14; 2:2).** Count the generations in 2:2. What does this mean for how churches train teachers and pastors?
- **Scripture (3:14–17).** What does "breathed out by God" mean? How does this passage support WCF 1 on the authority and sufficiency of Scripture?
- **Preach the word (4:1–5).** Why does Paul charge Timothy so solemnly? What happens when people "accumulate teachers to suit their own passions"?
- **Faith in families (1:5; 3:15).** Timothy learned the Scriptures from his mother and grandmother. How does God use covenant households?
- **Facing death (4:6–8).** What does Paul's attitude teach about how a Christian can face death? Compare Philippians 1:21–23.
- **Luther on 2 Timothy.** Luther called it a farewell letter warning that many would fall away and false teachers spring up (Lutheran Study Bible). How does that fit Barcley's "call to faithfulness"?

## Q&A

1. Where was Paul when he wrote 2 Timothy? In prison in Rome, expecting to die (1:8; 4:6).
2. Who were Timothy's grandmother and mother? Lois and Eunice (1:5).
3. What kind of spirit did God give us? Not of fear but of power, love and self-control (1:7).
4. To whom should Timothy entrust what he heard from Paul? Faithful men who will be able to teach others also (2:2).
5. What three pictures of endurance does Paul use in chapter 2? A soldier, an athlete and a farmer.
6. What did Hymenaeus and Philetus teach? That the resurrection had already happened (2:17–18).
7. What does 3:16 say about Scripture? All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, reproof, correction and training in righteousness.
8. What does Paul charge Timothy to do in 4:2? Preach the word, in season and out of season.
9. What did Paul say about his life in 4:7? "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith."
10. Who deserted Paul, and who alone was with him? Demas deserted him; only Luke was with him (4:10–11).
