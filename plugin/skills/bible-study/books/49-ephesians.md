# Ephesians

## Summary

Ephesians unfolds God's eternal plan to unite all things in Christ, and the church's place in it. The first half (1–3) praises God for every spiritual blessing in Christ: chosen, adopted, redeemed, sealed by the Spirit. Sinners dead in sin are saved by grace through faith, and Jews and Gentiles are made one new people. The second half (4–6) shows how to "walk in a manner worthy" of that calling: in unity, holiness and love, in marriage, family and work, and standing firm in the armour of God. The church father Chrysostom said Paul entrusted the Ephesians with his deepest thoughts (quoted in Waters).

## Setting

- **When:** about AD 60–62, during Paul's imprisonment, probably his house arrest in Rome (3:1; 4:1; 6:20; Acts 28), soon after he wrote Colossians, which shares much of its wording (Reformation Study Bible).
- **Where:** Ephesus, a leading city of Asia Minor, where Paul had ministered for about three years (Acts 19–20). Ephesus was the capital of the province of Asia and one of the five greatest cities of the empire, famous for its temple of Artemis (Diana), one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world (Acts 19:23–41). Waters notes that "in Ephesus" (1:1) is missing from some early manuscripts, so the letter may also have been meant to circulate among nearby churches; that would explain its lack of personal greetings and Paul's "hearing" of their faith (1:15).
- **Who wrote it:** Paul (1:1; 3:1). Waters observes that for about eighteen centuries no one doubted this. Many modern critical scholars call it pseudonymous, pointing to its fuller style and closeness to Colossians. Waters and the RSB defend Paul's authorship: an imitator would hardly copy Colossians word for word yet boldly move beyond it, and the overlap is best explained by Paul writing both at nearly the same time.
- **Where it sits:** one of the four "Prison Epistles" (Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, Philemon), carried by Tychicus (6:21) along with Colossians. Waters sees it as a broad summary of the gospel teaching the church had received, rather than a response to a crisis.

## Structure

Waters describes the body of the letter as two halves: the great indicatives of the gospel (1:3–3:21) and the great imperatives (4:1–6:20). The word "walk" links them: once we walked in sin (2:1–2), but now in good works (2:10), and each main division of the second half opens with a call to walk (4:1, 17; 5:2, 8, 15). The first half's sections follow the Reformation Study Bible.

- 1:1–2: Greeting
- 1:3–3:21: Doctrine: our riches in Christ
    - 1:3–14: praise for every blessing in Christ: the Father chooses, the Son redeems, the Spirit seals
    - 1:15–23: prayer to know God's power in the risen Christ
    - 2:1–3:13: our position in Christ: saved by grace; Jew and Gentile made one; the mystery revealed to Paul
    - 3:14–21: prayer to know Christ's love, and praise
- 4:1–6:20: Practice: our walk in Christ
    - 4:1–16: walk worthily: unity and gifts in the body
    - 4:17–5:1: no longer walk as the Gentiles do: put off the old self, put on the new
    - 5:2–7: walk in love
    - 5:8–14: walk as children of light
    - 5:15–6:9: walk wisely: filled with the Spirit, in marriage, family and work
    - 6:10–20: stand firm in the armour of God
- 6:21–24: Final greetings

## Chapter by chapter

- **Ephesians 1:** God has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing. He chose us before the foundation of the world, adopted us, redeemed us by Christ's blood, revealed his plan to unite all things in Christ, and sealed us with the Spirit. Paul prays they will know the hope, riches and power that raised Christ and made him head over all things for the church.
- **Ephesians 2:** We were dead in sins, but God, rich in mercy, made us alive with Christ. "By grace you have been saved through faith … not a result of works" (2:8–9), and created for good works. Christ broke down the dividing wall between Jew and Gentile, making one new humanity, a holy temple for God.
- **Ephesians 3:** The mystery revealed to Paul: Gentiles are fellow heirs. Paul prays that they may know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, and praises God who can do far more than we ask.
- **Ephesians 4:** Walk worthy of your calling, keeping the unity of the Spirit: one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father. Christ gives apostles, prophets, evangelists, and shepherds and teachers to equip the saints until all reach maturity. Put off the old self and put on the new: truthful, slow to anger, forgiving.
- **Ephesians 5:** Imitate God and walk in love, as children of light, wisely. Be filled with the Spirit, singing and giving thanks, and submit to one another. Wives and husbands reflect the church and Christ, who loved the church and gave himself for her.
- **Ephesians 6:** Children and parents, slaves and masters. Be strong in the Lord and put on the whole armour of God against spiritual forces of evil: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, the word of God. Pray at all times. Tychicus will bring news.

## Key people

- **Paul:** the apostle, a prisoner for Christ.
- **The Ephesian believers (and nearby churches):** a mixed Jewish and Gentile church.
- **Tychicus:** the trusted messenger carrying the letter (6:21–22).

## Key events

- Paul's three-year ministry in Ephesus (Acts 19; 20:31)
- Paul's farewell to the Ephesian elders (Acts 20:17–38)
- Paul's imprisonment (3:1; 6:20)

## Themes

- **Every spiritual blessing in Christ:** election, adoption, redemption, forgiveness and sealing by the Spirit (1:3–14), the work of the Father, Son and Spirit. This passage has long shaped Reformed teaching on the doctrines of grace.
- **The mystery revealed:** "mystery" comes seven times. God's plan, hidden before Christ and now made known, is to unite all things in him, and its sign is the church, where Gentiles are fellow heirs with Jews (1:9–10; 3:3–6).
- **God's sovereign grace:** chosen before the world began, saved by grace through faith, not works (1:4–5; 2:8–9).
- **Union with Christ:** "in Christ" and "in him" appear again and again, and believers are seated with him in the heavenly places (2:6).
- **One new people:** Jew and Gentile reconciled to God and each other in one body (2:11–22).
- **The church:** Christ's body, God's temple, and Christ's bride. The church displays God's wisdom to the heavenly powers (3:10).
- **Unity and maturity:** gifted leaders equip the saints to build up the body in love (4:1–16).
- **A new way of life:** put off the old self, put on the new (4:22–24).
- **Christ-shaped households:** marriage reflects Christ and the church (5:22–33). Love like Christ's (5:2) and mutual submission out of reverence for him (5:21) are the foundation for every role in the home and at work, and remembering the cross keeps this from becoming mere moralism.
- **Spiritual warfare:** stand firm in God's armour and pray (6:10–20).

## How it points to Christ

Christ is at the centre of God's plan: "to unite all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth" (1:10). In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins (1:7). God raised him and seated him far above every power, as head over all things for the church (1:20–22). He is our peace, who reconciled Jew and Gentile to God through the cross (2:14–16). He is the cornerstone of God's temple (2:20), and the husband who loved the church and gave himself up for her (5:25–27). His love surpasses knowledge (3:19).

## Common mix-ups

- "By grace you have been saved through faith" is Ephesians 2:8.
- The armour of God is Ephesians 6, not 1 Thessalonians (which mentions only a breastplate and helmet, 5:8).
- Ephesians and Colossians are very similar and were carried by the same messenger, Tychicus.
- Ephesus is in Asia Minor (modern Turkey), not Greece.
- "Do not let the sun go down on your anger" is Ephesians 4:26.
- "Submit to one another" (5:21) introduces the household instructions. They also give husbands, parents and masters duties of love and care.

## Going deeper

- **Who wrote Ephesians, and to whom?** Waters reviews the arguments against Paul's authorship and the missing "in Ephesus". What is at stake?
- **Election and grace (1:3–14; 2:1–10).** How do these passages shape Reformed teaching on predestination (WCF 3) and salvation by grace alone?
- **The dividing wall (2:11–22).** What did Christ abolish, and what does the one new humanity look like in a church today?
- **The gifts of 4:11–16.** Who are apostles and prophets "foundations" (2:20) for, and how do shepherds and teachers equip the saints now?
- **Marriage as a mystery (5:22–33).** How does Paul ground husbands' and wives' roles in Christ and the church?
- **The armour of God (6:10–20).** What does each piece represent? Why is the only offensive weapon the word of God?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** Where was Paul when he wrote Ephesians?

   **A:** In prison (3:1; 4:1; 6:20).

2. **Q:** With what has God blessed us in Christ?

   **A:** Every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places (1:3).

3. **Q:** When did God choose us in Christ?

   **A:** Before the foundation of the world (1:4).

4. **Q:** With what were believers sealed?

   **A:** The promised Holy Spirit (1:13).

5. **Q:** How are we saved, according to 2:8–9?

   **A:** By grace through faith, not by works.

6. **Q:** What did Christ break down between Jew and Gentile?

   **A:** The dividing wall of hostility (2:14).

7. **Q:** What are the "ones" of 4:4–6?

   **A:** One body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all.

8. **Q:** Why does Christ give apostles, prophets, evangelists, and shepherds and teachers?

   **A:** To equip the saints for ministry and build up the body (4:11–12).

9. **Q:** How should husbands love their wives?

   **A:** As Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her (5:25).

10. **Q:** What is the sword of the Spirit?

    **A:** The word of God (6:17).
