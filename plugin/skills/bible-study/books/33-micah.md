# Micah

## Summary

Micah, a country prophet from Judah, condemns the corrupt leaders of both Samaria and Jerusalem: rulers who devour the poor, prophets who preach for pay, and people who think temple rituals will cover injustice. Jerusalem itself will be ploughed like a field. Yet Micah also sees the nations streaming to God's mountain, a ruler coming from little Bethlehem, and a God who delights to forgive and throws his people's sins into the depths of the sea.

## Setting

- **When:** in the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah of Judah (1:1), about 735–700 BC (the Lutheran Study Bible gives about 750–686 BC). This spans the fall of Samaria (722 BC) and Sennacherib's invasion (701 BC).
- **Where:** Judah and Jerusalem, with oracles against Samaria. Micah was from Moresheth, a village in Judah's lowlands.
- **Who wrote it:** Micah of Moresheth, a contemporary of Isaiah (in Jerusalem) and Hosea (in the north).
- **Where it sits:** among the Twelve. Timmer notes its heading is very similar to those of Hosea, Amos and Zephaniah. A century later, elders in Jeremiah's day remembered Micah's warning against Jerusalem (Jer 26:18).

## Structure

Three cycles, each moving from judgment to hope, each opening with "Hear":

- 1–2: Judgment on Samaria and Jerusalem; a remnant gathered by its king
- 3–5: Corrupt leaders condemned; Zion exalted; the ruler from Bethlehem
- 6–7: God's lawsuit with his people; Micah's lament and confident hope

## Chapter by chapter

- **Micah 1:** The Lord comes in judgment on Samaria and Jerusalem. Micah laments over the towns of Judah, using wordplays on their names.
- **Micah 2:** Woe to those who lie awake plotting to seize fields and houses. False preachers want only pleasant words. Yet God will gather a remnant like sheep, led by their king (2:12–13).
- **Micah 3:** Rulers tear the flesh off the people, and prophets preach for money. Because of them, "Zion shall be plowed as a field" (3:12).
- **Micah 4:** In the latter days the nations stream to the Lord's mountain and beat swords into ploughshares. Everyone sits under his own vine and fig tree. Zion will go to Babylon, and be rescued there (4:10).
- **Micah 5:** From Bethlehem Ephrathah, small among Judah's clans, will come a ruler "whose coming forth is from of old" (5:2). He will shepherd in the Lord's strength and be their peace.
- **Micah 6:** God brings a case against his people: "what have I done to you?" Not thousands of rams but this is what he requires: "to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God" (6:8). Dishonest scales condemned.
- **Micah 7:** Micah grieves that the godly have vanished and families turn on each other. Yet "I will look to the Lord". "Who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity?" He will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea, faithful to Abraham and Jacob (7:18–20).

## Key people

- **Micah of Moresheth:** a prophet from rural Judah.
- **Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah:** kings of Judah in his day.
- **The rulers, priests and prophets:** corrupt leaders of Jerusalem (3:9–11).
- **The ruler from Bethlehem:** the coming shepherd-king (5:2–5).
- **The remnant:** those God gathers and preserves (2:12; 4:6–7; 5:7–8).

## Key events

- The judgment on Samaria and Jerusalem announced (1)
- The vision of the nations coming to Zion (4)
- The promise of the ruler from Bethlehem (5)
- God's covenant lawsuit (6)

## Themes

- **Justice for the poor:** Micah attacks land-grabbing, bribery and exploitation by the powerful (2:1–2; 3:1–3, 9–11; 6:10–12).
- **Leaders held to account:** rulers, priests and prophets who use their office for gain (3).
- **True religion:** God wants justice, steadfast love and humble walking with him, not empty sacrifice (6:6–8).
- **Judgment and hope:** each section moves from judgment to restoration. Timmer stresses how the Twelve hold both historical judgment and ultimate deliverance together.
- **The remnant:** God preserves and gathers a remnant (2:12–13; 4:6–8; 7:18).
- **The nations at Zion:** peoples will come to learn God's ways (4:1–4).
- **God's forgiving character:** a God who delights in steadfast love (7:18–20). The name Micah means "Who is like the Lord?"

## How it points to Christ

When the magi asked where the Messiah would be born, the chief priests quoted Micah 5:2: from Bethlehem would come a ruler who would shepherd God's people (Matt 2:5–6; John 7:42). Micah adds that his origins are "from of old, from ancient days", hinting at more than a mere man. He will "be their peace" (5:5), and Christ "is our peace" (Eph 2:14). Jesus quotes Micah 7:6 about families divided by his coming (Matt 10:35–36).

The God who casts sins into the depths of the sea (7:19) does so through Christ's cross, keeping his oath to Abraham (7:20; Luke 1:72–73).

## Common mix-ups

- Micah 4:1–3 is almost word for word the same as Isaiah 2:2–4. Both prophets shared the vision.
- "Do justice, love kindness, walk humbly" is Micah 6:8, not Proverbs.
- Micah (the Minor Prophet) is not Micaiah, who opposed Ahab in 1 Kings 22.
- Micah's prophecy against Jerusalem (3:12) is quoted in Jeremiah 26:18.
- The Bethlehem prophecy is Micah 5:2 (5:1 in Hebrew Bibles).

## Going deeper

- **What does the Lord require (6:6–8)?** Micah dismisses rams, oil and even a firstborn as ways to buy God's favour. What does he ask instead, and how does that fit with grace?
- **Leaders under judgment (3).** What does Micah say about rulers, priests and prophets who serve themselves? How does this apply to leaders today (1 Pet 5:1–4)?
- **The ruler from Bethlehem (5:2–5).** What do "from of old, from ancient days" and "he shall be their peace" suggest about this ruler?
- **The remnant and the nations.** Timmer traces how the Twelve distinguish a faithful remnant within Israel and include some from the nations who turn to God. Where do Micah 4 and 5 fit?
- **Who is a God like you? (7:18–20).** Micah's name is a question; the book ends with the answer. How does this ending reframe the judgments before it?

## Q&A

1. Where was Micah from? Moresheth, a village in Judah (1:1).
2. Which kings reigned in Micah's time? Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah of Judah (1:1).
3. What would happen to Zion because of its corrupt leaders? It would be ploughed like a field, and Jerusalem become a heap of ruins (3:12).
4. What will the nations do with their swords in the latter days? Beat them into ploughshares (4:3).
5. Where will the coming ruler come from? Bethlehem Ephrathah (5:2).
6. What does the Lord require, according to 6:8? To do justice, love kindness, and walk humbly with your God.
7. What does Micah's name mean? "Who is like the Lord?"
8. Where will God cast his people's sins? Into the depths of the sea (7:19).
9. Which prophet shared Micah's vision of the mountain of the Lord? Isaiah (Isa 2:2–4).
10. Who quoted Micah 5:2 to Herod? The chief priests and scribes (Matt 2:4–6).
