# Proverbs

## Summary

Proverbs is a book of practical wisdom for living well in God's world. It opens with a father's instructions to his son, pleading with him to choose wisdom over folly. Then come hundreds of short sayings about speech, work, money, friendship, marriage, anger, pride and justice. Its foundation is "the fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge" (1:7): true wisdom starts with reverent trust in God.

## Setting

- **When:** mostly from Solomon's time (10th century BC). Hezekiah's officials gathered a further collection around 700 BC (25:1), and the book was finished later (VanGemeren; Lutheran Study Bible).
- **Where:** the royal court in Jerusalem, and the life of Israelite families: the home, the city gate, the marketplace.
- **Who wrote it:** chiefly Solomon (1:1; 10:1; 25:1), with sayings from "the wise" (22:17; 24:23), Agur (30) and King Lemuel's mother (31). VanGemeren notes that the other named contributors show Solomon didn't write it all.
- **Where it sits:** the central wisdom book of the Old Testament, alongside Job and Ecclesiastes, which wrestle with the hard cases Proverbs doesn't cover.

## Structure

- 1:1–7: Title and purpose: "the fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge"
- 1:8–9:18: A father's speeches to his son, and the poems of Lady Wisdom
- 10:1–22:16: The proverbs of Solomon: hundreds of two-line sayings
- 22:17–24:34: The words of the wise, including "thirty sayings" (22:20)
- 25–29: More proverbs of Solomon, collected by Hezekiah's men
- 30: The words of Agur
- 31: The words of King Lemuel, and the poem of the excellent wife (31:10–31)

## Chapter by chapter

- **Proverbs 1:** The book's purpose, and its motto: "the fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge" (1:7). A father warns his son against violent companions, and Wisdom cries out in the streets against those who refuse her.
- **Proverbs 2:** Search for wisdom like hidden treasure. The Lord gives it (2:6), and it protects from evil men and the adulterous woman.
- **Proverbs 3:** "Trust in the Lord with all your heart" (3:5–6). Honour God with your wealth, and don't despise his discipline (3:11–12). Wisdom is worth more than gold, and God founded the earth by wisdom.
- **Proverbs 4:** A father passes on his father's teaching. The path of the righteous and the way of the wicked. "Keep your heart with all vigilance" (4:23).
- **Proverbs 5:** A warning against the adulteress. Rejoice in the wife of your youth.
- **Proverbs 6:** Don't guarantee another's debts. "Go to the ant, O sluggard". Six things the Lord hates, seven that are an abomination (6:16–19). Adultery's cost.
- **Proverbs 7:** A young man without sense is led astray by the adulteress at twilight, like an ox to the slaughter.
- **Proverbs 8:** Wisdom calls to all. She was with the Lord before creation, rejoicing in his work (8:22–31).
- **Proverbs 9:** Two invitations: Lady Wisdom's feast and Folly's. "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom" (9:10).
- **Proverbs 10:** Solomon's sayings begin: the wise son and the foolish one, the righteous and the wicked, words that give life.
- **Proverbs 11:** Honest scales, the danger of pride, and generosity that enriches the giver (11:24–25).
- **Proverbs 12:** Diligence and laziness, truthful and reckless speech; whoever loves discipline loves knowledge.
- **Proverbs 13:** "Hope deferred makes the heart sick" (13:12). Walk with the wise. Discipline a child out of love (13:24).
- **Proverbs 14:** A way that seems right to a man ends in death (14:12). The fear of the Lord is a fountain of life.
- **Proverbs 15:** "A soft answer turns away wrath" (15:1). Better a little with the fear of the Lord than great treasure with trouble.
- **Proverbs 16:** People plan their way, but the Lord establishes their steps (16:9). Pride goes before destruction (16:18). Even the lot is decided by the Lord (16:33).
- **Proverbs 17:** Better a dry crust in peace than a house full of strife. "A friend loves at all times" (17:17). A joyful heart is good medicine.
- **Proverbs 18:** The Lord's name is a strong tower (18:10). Death and life are in the power of the tongue (18:21). A friend can stick closer than a brother.
- **Proverbs 19:** Kindness to the poor is a loan to the Lord (19:17). Many plans are in the heart, but the Lord's purpose stands (19:21).
- **Proverbs 20:** Wine is a mocker (20:1). The Lord hates false weights. A person's steps are from the Lord.
- **Proverbs 21:** The king's heart is in the Lord's hand (21:1). Doing justice pleases God more than sacrifice (21:3).
- **Proverbs 22:** A good name is better than riches (22:1). "Train up a child in the way he should go" (22:6). The words of the wise begin (22:17).
- **Proverbs 23:** Don't wear yourself out getting rich. Discipline your children. A vivid warning about drunkenness (23:29–35).
- **Proverbs 24:** Rescue those being led to death. Don't gloat when your enemy falls. The sluggard's overgrown field (24:30–34).
- **Proverbs 25:** Hezekiah's collection begins. It is God's glory to conceal things (25:2). A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold (25:11). Feed your hungry enemy (25:21–22).
- **Proverbs 26:** Answering a fool, both ways (26:4–5). The sluggard, and the fool who repeats his folly like a dog returning to its vomit (26:11).
- **Proverbs 27:** Don't boast about tomorrow (27:1). Faithful are the wounds of a friend (27:6). Iron sharpens iron (27:17).
- **Proverbs 28:** The wicked flee when no one pursues, but the righteous are bold (28:1). Whoever confesses and forsakes his sins finds mercy (28:13).
- **Proverbs 29:** Without revelation people cast off restraint (29:18). The fear of man is a snare, but trust in the Lord is safe (29:25).
- **Proverbs 30:** Agur's humble words. "Give me neither poverty nor riches" (30:8–9). Numerical sayings about wonders of nature and society.
- **Proverbs 31:** King Lemuel's mother warns him against women and wine and urges justice for the poor. The book ends with the poem of the excellent wife, who fears the Lord (31:10–31).

## Key people

- **Solomon:** son of David, Israel's wisest king, and the main source of the proverbs.
- **The father (and mother):** the teachers of chapters 1–9, passing wisdom to the next generation.
- **The son:** the young learner addressed throughout, facing the choice between two ways.
- **Lady Wisdom:** wisdom pictured as a woman calling people to life (1; 8–9).
- **Folly / the adulteress:** the rival voice luring the simple to death (5; 7; 9).
- **Hezekiah's men:** officials who copied out Solomon's proverbs (25:1).
- **Agur, son of Jakeh:** author of chapter 30.
- **King Lemuel:** a king taught by his mother (31).
- **The excellent wife:** the woman of strength and fear of the Lord in 31:10–31.

## Key events

Proverbs isn't a narrative. Its few "scenes":

- Wisdom crying out in the street (1:20–33)
- The young man and the adulteress (7)
- Wisdom present at creation (8:22–31)
- The two banquets of Wisdom and Folly (9)
- The sluggard's field (24:30–34)

## Themes

- **The fear of the Lord:** reverent trust in God is the foundation of wisdom (1:7; 9:10; 15:33; 31:30).
- **Two ways:** wisdom and righteousness lead to life; folly and wickedness lead to death (4:18–19; 14:12).
- **Speech:** the tongue can heal or wound, give life or bring ruin (10:19; 12:18; 15:1; 18:21).
- **Work and money:** diligence, honesty, generosity and contentment against laziness, greed and debt (6:6–11; 11:24–25; 30:8–9).
- **Family and friendship:** faithful marriage, training children, loyal friends (5; 17:17; 22:6; 27:6).
- **God's sovereignty and human plans:** we plan, but the Lord directs (16:1, 9, 33; 19:21; 21:1).
- **Three dimensions of wisdom:** VanGemeren describes wisdom as illumination from God (the fear of the Lord), discernment in applying it, and transformation of the self and its relationships.

## How it points to Christ

Proverbs personifies wisdom, present with God at creation and calling people to life (8:22–31). The New Testament reveals Christ as "the wisdom of God" (1 Cor 1:24, 30), the one through whom all things were made (John 1:1–3; Col 1:15–17), in whom "are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3). Jesus is "something greater than Solomon" (Matt 12:42), and he grew in wisdom himself (Luke 2:52).

Proverbs describes the perfectly wise, righteous person who fears the Lord. Only Jesus fully lived that life, and through faith he becomes our wisdom and righteousness (1 Cor 1:30), as the Lutheran Study Bible also notes.

## Common mix-ups

- Proverbs are general truths about how life usually works, not guaranteed promises. VanGemeren calls them "situational". "Train up a child …" (22:6) describes the normal pattern, not an iron law.
- Two proverbs can seem to contradict each other on purpose: "answer not a fool … answer a fool" (26:4–5). Wisdom means knowing which one fits.
- Solomon didn't write the whole book: Agur, Lemuel's mother and other wise men contributed.
- "Spare the rod, spoil the child" isn't a verse. The idea is in 13:24.
- The Bible says pride goes before *destruction*, and a haughty spirit before a fall (16:18).
- Proverbs 31:10–31 is a poem celebrating godly wisdom in a woman's life, not a checklist every wife must meet.
- Lady Wisdom in Proverbs 8 is a poetic personification, not a created being. Christians see her fulfilled in Christ, who is uncreated and eternal.

## Going deeper

- **Proverbs aren't promises.** Read VanGemeren on the situational nature of the sayings. How should we read 3:9–10, 10:4 and 22:6 alongside Job and Ecclesiastes?
- **Proverbs and Egyptian wisdom.** Part of 22:17–24:22 closely resembles the Egyptian "Instruction of Amenemope". VanGemeren discusses Israel's wisdom alongside its neighbours'. What makes Israel's wisdom different? Its root in the fear of the Lord (1:7).
- **Wisdom in Proverbs 8 and Christ.** Compare 8:22–31 with John 1:1–3, Colossians 1:15–17 and Hebrews 1:2–3. What are the parallels, and why doesn't the church read Proverbs 8 as saying the Son was created?
- **Is Proverbs moralistic?** The Lutheran Study Bible addresses the charge that Proverbs is self-help without grace. Note how often success is credited to the Lord (16:1–9; 21:30–31).
- **Speech in Proverbs.** Collect every proverb about the tongue in chapters 10–18. What patterns appear? Compare James 3.
- **The two women.** Lady Wisdom and Lady Folly both invite the simple to dinner (9). What does each offer, and where does each lead?

## Q&A

1. What is the motto of Proverbs? "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge" (1:7).
2. Who wrote most of Proverbs? Solomon (1:1; 10:1; 25:1).
3. Who else contributed? The wise men, Agur, and King Lemuel's mother (22:17; 30:1; 31:1).
4. What does Proverbs 3:5–6 say? "Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding …"
5. Which insect does Proverbs hold up to the sluggard? The ant (6:6).
6. What turns away wrath? A soft answer (15:1).
7. What goes before destruction? Pride (16:18).
8. Who copied out more of Solomon's proverbs? The men of Hezekiah, king of Judah (25:1).
9. What sharpens a person, according to 27:17? Another person, as iron sharpens iron.
10. How does Proverbs end? With the poem of the excellent wife who fears the Lord (31:10–31).
