# Acts

## Summary

Acts continues the story Luke began: what the risen Jesus kept doing through his apostles by the Holy Spirit. After Jesus ascends, the Spirit comes at Pentecost and the church is born in Jerusalem. Through persecution and mission the good news spreads to Judea and Samaria, then to the Gentiles: from Peter's preaching to Cornelius, to Paul's missionary journeys across the Roman world. The book ends with Paul in Rome under house arrest, proclaiming the kingdom of God "with all boldness and without hindrance" (28:31).

## Setting

- **When:** from Jesus' ascension (about AD 30–33) to Paul's two years under house arrest in Rome (about AD 60–62). Cara describes it as a theological interpretation of the church's first three decades.
- **Where:** Jerusalem, Judea and Samaria, Syria (Antioch), Asia Minor, Greece and Macedonia, the islands of Cyprus, Crete and Malta, and finally Rome.
- **Who wrote it:** Luke, Paul's Gentile companion, author of the Gospel of Luke. The "we" passages (16:10–17; 20:5–21:18; 27:1–28:16) show him travelling with Paul. Written to Theophilus (1:1). Cara holds this traditional view.
- **Where it sits:** the only history book of the New Testament, the bridge between the Gospels and the letters. Cara notes a longer "Western" version of the Greek text (about 8.5% longer) that modern translations don't follow.

## Structure

Cara offers two complementary outlines:

- **By its two main figures:** Peter (1–12) and Paul (13–28), with striking parallels. Both heal a lame man, both preach, and both are freed from prison miraculously.
- **By the Spirit's outpouring:** following Jesus' program in 1:8, "in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth":
    - 1–7: Jerusalem: the Spirit comes at Pentecost, the church grows, and Stephen is martyred
    - 8–12: Judea and Samaria: Samaritans believe, Saul is converted, and Gentiles receive the Spirit (Cornelius)
    - 13–28: To the ends of the earth: Paul's three missionary journeys, his arrest, trials and voyage to Rome

## Chapter by chapter

- **Acts 1:** For forty days the risen Jesus teaches about the kingdom. "You will receive power … and you will be my witnesses … to the end of the earth" (1:8). He ascends, and Matthias replaces Judas.
- **Acts 2:** At Pentecost the Spirit comes with wind and fire, and the apostles speak in other languages. Peter preaches Jesus crucified, risen and exalted. About three thousand are baptised, and the believers share life together.
- **Acts 3:** Peter and John heal a man lame from birth at the temple gate, and Peter calls the crowd to repent.
- **Acts 4:** Peter and John are arrested and declare there is salvation in no other name (4:12). Released, the church prays boldly and shares its possessions.
- **Acts 5:** Ananias and Sapphira lie to the Spirit and die. The apostles are jailed and freed by an angel: "We must obey God rather than men" (5:29). Gamaliel counsels caution.
- **Acts 6:** Seven men, including Stephen and Philip, are chosen to serve tables. Stephen is falsely accused.
- **Acts 7:** Stephen reviews Israel's history of resisting God's messengers, sees Jesus standing at God's right hand, and is stoned. Saul approves.
- **Acts 8:** Persecution scatters the church. Philip preaches in Samaria, Simon the magician tries to buy the Spirit, and Philip baptises an Ethiopian official.
- **Acts 9:** Saul meets the risen Jesus on the road to Damascus, is blinded, baptised by Ananias, and begins preaching Christ. Peter heals Aeneas and raises Tabitha.
- **Acts 10:** An angel tells the Roman centurion Cornelius to send for Peter. Peter's vision of the sheet teaches him not to call any person unclean. As he preaches, the Spirit falls on the Gentiles.
- **Acts 11:** Peter explains to Jerusalem why Gentiles were baptised. The church in Antioch grows, and the disciples are first called "Christians" there. Famine relief is sent.
- **Acts 12:** Herod Agrippa I kills James and imprisons Peter, whom an angel frees. Herod is struck down.
- **Acts 13:** The Antioch church sends Barnabas and Saul (Paul) on the first journey: Cyprus, then Pisidian Antioch, where Paul preaches and turns to the Gentiles.
- **Acts 14:** In Iconium, Lystra (where the crowds call them gods and Paul is stoned) and Derbe. They return, strengthening the churches and appointing elders.
- **Acts 15:** The Jerusalem council decides Gentile believers need not be circumcised. Paul and Barnabas part ways over John Mark.
- **Acts 16:** The second journey. Timothy joins, and a vision calls Paul to Macedonia. In Philippi, Lydia believes, a slave girl is freed, and a jailer and his household are saved.
- **Acts 17:** Thessalonica, then Berea, whose people examine the Scriptures daily. In Athens, Paul speaks at the Areopagus about the "unknown god".
- **Acts 18:** Eighteen months in Corinth with Aquila and Priscilla; Gallio refuses to judge. Apollos is taught more accurately in Ephesus.
- **Acts 19:** In Ephesus, disciples of John receive the Spirit. Paul teaches two years, magic books are burned, and the silversmiths of Artemis riot.
- **Acts 20:** Through Macedonia and Greece. Eutychus falls from a window and is raised. Paul's farewell to the Ephesian elders at Miletus.
- **Acts 21:** Paul goes to Jerusalem despite warnings (Agabus) and is seized in the temple.
- **Acts 22:** Paul tells his conversion story to the crowd and claims his Roman citizenship.
- **Acts 23:** Before the council, Paul divides Pharisees and Sadducees over the resurrection. A plot is uncovered, and he is moved to Caesarea.
- **Acts 24:** Paul's defence before Governor Felix, who keeps him in custody for two years.
- **Acts 25:** Before Festus, Paul appeals to Caesar. King Agrippa II arrives.
- **Acts 26:** Paul's defence before Agrippa, telling his conversion again. Agrippa says Paul almost persuades him.
- **Acts 27:** The voyage to Rome, a violent storm, and shipwreck. Everyone reaches land safely.
- **Acts 28:** On Malta, Paul survives a snakebite and heals the sick. In Rome he lives under house arrest for two years, preaching the kingdom of God freely (28:30–31).

## Key people

- **Peter:** leader of the apostles, preacher at Pentecost, who opens the door to the Gentiles.
- **John:** Peter's companion in the early chapters.
- **Stephen:** one of the seven, the first Christian martyr.
- **Philip:** one of the seven, an evangelist to Samaria and the Ethiopian.
- **Saul / Paul:** a persecutor turned apostle to the Gentiles.
- **Barnabas:** an encourager, Paul's first missionary partner.
- **James:** the Lord's brother, leader of the Jerusalem church (15).
- **Cornelius:** a Roman centurion, the first notable Gentile convert.
- **Silas, Timothy and Luke:** Paul's companions.
- **Lydia:** the first convert in Europe.
- **Aquila, Priscilla and Apollos:** teachers and co-workers.
- **Herod Agrippa I and II, Felix, and Festus:** rulers Paul and the apostles face.

## Themes

- **The Holy Spirit:** the Spirit empowers the witness, from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth (1:8). Cara traces the Spirit's coming to Jews, Samaritans and Gentiles.
- **Witness to the risen Christ:** the apostles' central message is that God raised Jesus (2:32; 3:15; 13:30).
- **The spread of the word:** "the word of God continued to increase and multiply" (12:24), despite opposition.
- **Jews and Gentiles in one church:** God shows no partiality, and Gentiles are saved by grace through faith without becoming Jews (10:34–35; 15:7–11).
- **God's plan fulfilled:** events happen "according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God" (2:23) and in fulfilment of Scripture. Cara notes how Luke connects events with Old Testament fulfilment.
- **Suffering and boldness:** persecution, imprisonment and martyrdom don't stop the gospel (4:29–31; 5:41; 14:22).
- **The life of the church:** the apostles' teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread, prayer, generosity, and elders (2:42–47; 14:23; 20:28).

## How it points to Christ

Acts begins by calling the Gospel of Luke an account of "all that Jesus began to do and teach" (1:1). Acts is what the risen, ascended Jesus continues to do by his Spirit through his church. He pours out the Spirit (2:33). He is the one name by which people are saved (4:12). Stephen sees him standing at God's right hand (7:56). He confronts Saul ("I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting", 9:5). He is preached to Jews and Gentiles as Lord and Christ (2:36; 10:36). The book ends with Paul "teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ" (28:31).

## Common mix-ups

- Acts is written by Luke, not by Paul or Peter.
- Saul and Paul are the same man. Paul is his Roman name, used from 13:9 on.
- The James killed by Herod (12:2) is the son of Zebedee. The James who leads the Jerusalem council (15) is the Lord's brother.
- Philip the evangelist (one of the seven) is a different person from Philip the apostle.
- Herod Agrippa I (12) and Agrippa II (25–26) are father and son.
- Pentecost came fifty days after Passover. It is when the Spirit was poured out, not the day of the resurrection.
- Acts doesn't record Paul's death; it ends with him alive in Rome.
- Believers were first called "Christians" in Antioch (11:26), not Jerusalem.

## Q&A

1. Who wrote Acts, and to whom? Luke, to Theophilus (1:1).
2. Where did Jesus say the apostles would be his witnesses? In Jerusalem, all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth (1:8).
3. What happened at Pentecost? The Holy Spirit came on the believers, they spoke in other languages, and about three thousand were saved (2).
4. Who was the first Christian martyr? Stephen (7).
5. Where was Saul when Jesus appeared to him? On the road to Damascus (9).
6. Which Gentile's household received the Spirit while Peter preached? Cornelius' (10).
7. Where were believers first called Christians? Antioch (11:26).
8. What did the Jerusalem council decide? Gentile believers did not need to be circumcised to be saved (15).
9. Who was converted in Philippi besides Lydia? The jailer and his household (16:25–34).
10. How does Acts end? With Paul in Rome under house arrest, preaching the kingdom of God without hindrance (28:30–31).
