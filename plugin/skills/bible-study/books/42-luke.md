# Luke

## Summary

Luke writes an "orderly account" of Jesus' life so that Theophilus may be certain of what he has been taught (1:3–4). He tells the fullest story of Jesus' birth, follows his ministry in Galilee, and devotes a long section to his journey to Jerusalem. That section is full of parables found only in Luke: the good Samaritan, the prodigal son, and the Pharisee and the tax collector. Jesus is the Saviour who came "to seek and to save the lost" (19:10): the rejected Prophet and Messiah who dies, rises, explains the Scriptures, and ascends.

## Setting

- **When:** from the announcement of John the Baptist's birth (about 7–6 BC) to Jesus' ascension (about AD 30–33). Written before Acts, which ends around AD 62; many date Luke to the early 60s. The Reformation Study Bible agrees: Acts ends with Paul still under house arrest, says nothing of Jerusalem's fall in AD 70 or of Paul's letters, and the later dates (AD 75–85) mostly assume Jesus couldn't have predicted Jerusalem's destruction.
- **Where:** the temple in Jerusalem (where the book begins and ends), Nazareth, Bethlehem, Galilee, a long journey toward Jerusalem, and Jerusalem itself.
- **Who wrote it:** Luke, "the beloved physician" (Col 4:14), Paul's travelling companion, implied in the "we" passages of Acts, and probably a Gentile (Paul lists him after his Jewish co-workers, Col 4:10–14). Tradition says he came from Syrian Antioch. He wasn't an eyewitness, but researched carefully and consulted those who were (1:1–4). Cara holds this traditional view, as did the church from the 2nd century; even the heretic Marcion chose Luke's Gospel to edit.
- **Where it sits:** the third Gospel and the first volume of a two-part work with Acts. Both are addressed to Theophilus, "most excellent", a title for Roman officials of high rank (Cara; compare Acts 23:26), who stands for instructed Gentile believers. Only Luke carries the story on past the resurrection, into the church's growth. His polished opening gives way to the style of the Greek Old Testament, presenting Jesus as the climax of Israel's history (RSB).

## Structure

After the prologue the outline is mainly geographical (Cara; Reformation Study Bible):

- 1:1–4: Prologue: a researched, orderly account, based on eyewitnesses, so that readers may be certain
- 1:5–2:52: The births of John and Jesus in parallel, and Jesus' childhood
- 3:1–9:50: The Lord's ministry in Galilee: John's preaching, Jesus' baptism and temptation, the Nazareth sermon, teaching and miracles
- 9:51–19:27: The journey to Jerusalem: Jesus "set his face" to go there (9:51), and Luke keeps reminding readers where he is heading. Much teaching, and many parables found only in Luke
- 19:28–21:38: The Lord's ministry in Jerusalem: the entry, the temple, debates, and the end-times discourse
- 22–23: The Lord's suffering in Jerusalem
- 24: The resurrection, the road to Emmaus, and the ascension

## Chapter by chapter

- **Luke 1:** Luke's preface to Theophilus. Gabriel announces John's birth to Zechariah, who is struck mute, then Jesus' birth to Mary. Mary visits Elizabeth and sings the Magnificat. John is born, and Zechariah prophesies.
- **Luke 2:** A census brings Joseph and Mary to Bethlehem, where Jesus is born and laid in a manger. Angels announce him to shepherds. In the temple Simeon and Anna recognise him. At twelve, Jesus stays behind in his Father's house.
- **Luke 3:** John preaches repentance. Jesus is baptised and the Spirit descends. Jesus' genealogy is traced back to Adam, "the son of God".
- **Luke 4:** Jesus is tempted in the wilderness. In Nazareth's synagogue he reads Isaiah 61, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled", and is driven out. He heals and drives out demons in Capernaum.
- **Luke 5:** A miraculous catch of fish, and Simon is called. A leper and a paralysed man are healed. Levi is called, and Jesus speaks of new wine in new wineskins.
- **Luke 6:** Sabbath disputes, and the Twelve chosen. The Sermon on the Plain: blessings and woes, love your enemies, and the house on the rock.
- **Luke 7:** A centurion's servant is healed and a widow's son raised at Nain. John the Baptist asks his question. A sinful woman anoints Jesus' feet: "her sins … are forgiven, for she loved much".
- **Luke 8:** Women support Jesus' ministry. The parable of the sower. Jesus calms a storm, frees a demon-possessed man, heals a bleeding woman, and raises Jairus' daughter.
- **Luke 9:** The Twelve are sent out, five thousand are fed, and Peter confesses "the Christ of God". The transfiguration. Jesus "set his face to go to Jerusalem" (9:51).
- **Luke 10:** Seventy-two are sent out. The parable of the good Samaritan. Martha is busy while Mary sits at Jesus' feet.
- **Luke 11:** The Lord's Prayer and the persistent friend. The Beelzebul charge, the sign of Jonah, and woes on Pharisees and lawyers.
- **Luke 12:** Fear God, not people. The rich fool. Don't be anxious, and be ready for the master's return.
- **Luke 13:** Unless you repent you will perish. The barren fig tree. A bent woman healed on the Sabbath. The narrow door, and Jesus' lament over Jerusalem.
- **Luke 14:** Healing at a Pharisee's dinner, and lessons in humility. The great banquet, and the cost of discipleship.
- **Luke 15:** The lost sheep, the lost coin, and the prodigal son, with joy in heaven over one sinner who repents.
- **Luke 16:** The shrewd manager, and the rich man and Lazarus.
- **Luke 17:** Forgiveness and faith. Ten lepers are healed, and only a Samaritan returns to give thanks. The coming of the kingdom.
- **Luke 18:** The persistent widow. The Pharisee and the tax collector. Jesus blesses children. The rich ruler, and a blind beggar near Jericho.
- **Luke 19:** Zacchaeus: "the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost" (19:10). The parable of the minas. The triumphal entry, Jesus weeping over Jerusalem, and the temple cleansed.
- **Luke 20:** Questions about authority, the wicked tenants, taxes to Caesar, the resurrection, and David's son.
- **Luke 21:** The widow's two coins. Jesus foretells the temple's destruction and his coming.
- **Luke 22:** Judas agrees to betray Jesus. The Last Supper: "this cup … is the new covenant in my blood". The disciples argue over greatness. Jesus prays on the Mount of Olives, is arrested, and Peter denies him.
- **Luke 23:** Trials before Pilate and Herod, and Barabbas released. On the cross: "Father, forgive them", "today you will be with me in paradise", and "into your hands I commit my spirit". Joseph of Arimathea buries him.
- **Luke 24:** The women find the tomb empty. The risen Jesus walks with two disciples to Emmaus, appears to the apostles, opens the Scriptures about himself (24:44–47), and ascends near Bethany.

## Key people

- **Jesus:** Saviour, Christ the Lord, the Prophet and Son of God.
- **Theophilus:** the "most excellent" recipient of Luke and Acts.
- **Zechariah and Elizabeth:** John the Baptist's elderly parents.
- **Mary:** Jesus' mother, who treasures these things in her heart.
- **Joseph:** Mary's husband.
- **John the Baptist:** Jesus' relative and forerunner.
- **Simeon and Anna:** who recognise the infant Christ in the temple.
- **Peter and the Twelve:** Jesus' apostles.
- **Martha and Mary:** sisters who host Jesus.
- **Zacchaeus:** the chief tax collector who repents.
- **Herod Antipas and Pilate:** rulers at Jesus' trial.
- **The criminal on the cross:** promised paradise.
- **Cleopas:** one of the two on the road to Emmaus.

## Themes

- **Salvation:** Cara highlights "salvation" as a key Lukan theme. Jesus is "a Saviour, who is Christ the Lord" (2:11), and "today salvation has come to this house" (19:9).
- **The rejected, suffering Prophet and Messiah:** Jesus is rejected like the prophets before him, and must suffer, die and rise (4:24; 13:33; 24:26) (Cara).
- **God's plan in continuity with the Old Testament:** everything happens according to Scripture, and the plan includes the Gentiles (2:32; 24:44–47) (Cara). Words like "it is necessary" mark God's sovereign plan, worked out even through sinful acts (9:22; 24:26; Acts 2:23).
- **Christ the Lord:** Jesus is David's heir and God's Son, conceived by the Spirit (1:32–35), so Luke often simply calls him "the Lord". He is also the suffering Servant of Isaiah (22:37) and the prophet like Moses (9:35).
- **Good news for outsiders:** the poor, the sick, women, tax collectors, Samaritans and sinners are welcomed (4:18; 7:36–50; 15; 19:1–10).
- **The Holy Spirit and prayer:** the Spirit is active from Jesus' conception onward and is the Father's good gift (11:13), preparing for Pentecost (24:49). Jesus prays at key moments, and Luke records nine of his prayers, seven found only here (3:21; 6:12; 9:28; 22:41).
- **The coming kingdom:** Luke stresses salvation now, but also the coming judgment and Christ's return (12:35–48; 21:25–36).
- **Joy:** angels, shepherds, the lost found, and disciples returning to Jerusalem "with great joy" (2:10; 15:7; 24:52). Only Luke records the songs around Jesus' birth: Mary's, Zechariah's and Simeon's (1:46–55, 68–79; 2:29–32).
- **Certainty:** Luke writes so that readers may know the truth of what they've been taught (1:4).

## How it points to Christ

The whole Gospel is "the things that have been accomplished among us" in Christ (1:1). Jesus himself, after his resurrection, explained "in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself", showing that the Christ had to suffer and rise, and that repentance and forgiveness would be preached in his name to all nations (24:27, 44–47). He is the Spirit-anointed preacher of Isaiah 61 (4:18–21), the Son of Man who seeks and saves the lost (19:10), and the one whose blood makes the new covenant (22:20).

Luke's genealogy traces him back to Adam (3:38), presenting him as the Saviour for all humanity.

## Common mix-ups

- Luke and Acts were written by the same author to the same person, Theophilus.
- Luke's genealogy goes from Jesus back to Adam; Matthew's goes from Abraham forward to Jesus.
- Only Luke records the announcement to Zechariah, the shepherds, Simeon and Anna, Jesus at twelve, the good Samaritan, the prodigal son, Zacchaeus, and the Emmaus road.
- The Sermon on the Plain (Luke 6) parallels much of the Sermon on the Mount (Matt 5–7) but is shorter, with blessings and woes.
- Luke was probably a Gentile, the only non-Jewish author in the New Testament, by most accounts.
- The criminal who asked to be remembered is only in Luke (23:39–43).

## Going deeper

- **Luke and Acts together.** Cara discusses whether to read them as two books or one work, "Luke-Acts". What changes if you read Luke 24 and Acts 1 back to back?
- **The parallels of John and Jesus (1–2).** Cara shows Luke pairing their announcements, births and responses. What does Luke want readers to see in the comparisons?
- **The travel narrative (9:51–19:27).** Why does Luke stretch Jesus' journey to Jerusalem over ten chapters, and what teaching fills it?
- **The lost things (15).** Who are the three parables really addressed to (15:1–2)? What is the point of the older brother?
- **Jesus and the Scriptures (24).** How does the Emmaus road show Christians how to read the Old Testament?
- **Salvation for outsiders.** Trace women, Samaritans, tax collectors and Gentiles through Luke. How does that prepare for Acts?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** To whom did Luke write his Gospel?

   **A:** Theophilus (1:3).

2. **Q:** Why did Luke write?

   **A:** So that Theophilus might have certainty about what he had been taught (1:4).

3. **Q:** Who announced John's birth to Zechariah?

   **A:** The angel Gabriel (1:19).

4. **Q:** To whom did the angels announce Jesus' birth?

   **A:** Shepherds near Bethlehem (2:8–14).

5. **Q:** Who recognised Jesus as the Christ when he was presented in the temple?

   **A:** Simeon and Anna (2:25–38).

6. **Q:** Which passage did Jesus read in the Nazareth synagogue?

   **A:** Isaiah 61:1–2 (4:16–21).

7. **Q:** Which parable answers the question "Who is my neighbour?"

   **A:** The good Samaritan (10:25–37).

8. **Q:** Which three parables of lost things are in Luke 15?

   **A:** The lost sheep, the lost coin and the lost (prodigal) son (15:3–32).

9. **Q:** Who climbed a tree to see Jesus?

   **A:** Zacchaeus, the chief tax collector (19:1–10).

10. **Q:** Where did the risen Jesus walk with two disciples?

    **A:** On the road to Emmaus (24:13–35).
