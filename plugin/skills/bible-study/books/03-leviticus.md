# Leviticus

## Summary

Leviticus ("about the Levites"; in Hebrew *vayikra*, "and he called") answers the question left at the end of Exodus: how can a holy God live among sinful people? It is a manual for worship at the tabernacle, addressed to all Israel, not just the priests. God provides sacrifices to atone for sin, priests to serve, laws to separate clean from unclean, and a calendar of holy days. All of it calls Israel to "be holy, for I am holy" (11:45; 19:2).

## Setting

- **When:** about a year after the exodus, during Israel's stay at Mount Sinai (about 1445 BC in the Lutheran Study Bible's dating). It was probably written down during the wilderness years, before Moses died (Reformation Study Bible).
- **Where:** the tabernacle in Israel's camp at the foot of Sinai.
- **Who wrote it:** Moses. McKelvey points out that no book claims more often to be God's own words: "The Lord spoke to Moses" opens chapter after chapter. Many critical scholars date it after the exile, but the RSB answers that its worship doesn't match the second temple's, and earlier books (Deuteronomy, Amos, Ezekiel) already assume or quote it.
- **Where it sits:** the third book of Moses, the middle of the five. It is almost all speeches, with only two short stories: the priests' ordination with the deaths of Nadab and Abihu (8–10), and the blasphemer (24). It picks up straight from the end of Exodus, when God's glory fills the tabernacle (Ex 40:34; Lev 1:1), and Numbers carries on from it.

## Structure

McKelvey's outline has two parts: how to worship the holy God, and how to live with him. The Reformation Study Bible divides the same material into four: sacrifice (1–7), priesthood (8–10), uncleanness and its cleansing (11–16), and holiness in practice (17–27).

- 1–10: Worshipping the holy God
    - 1–7: laws on sacrifice: the five offerings, and instructions for the priests
    - 8–10: the priesthood begins: Aaron and his sons ordained; Nadab and Abihu die
- 11–27: Living with the holy God
    - 11–15: clean and unclean: food, childbirth, skin disease, discharges
    - 16: the Day of Atonement
    - 17–25: laws of holiness, holy times, the Sabbath year and the Jubilee
    - 26: blessings and curses
    - 27: vows and gifts to the Lord

## Chapter by chapter

- **Leviticus 1:** The burnt offering: a whole animal given to God, with the worshipper's hand laid on its head. It is "a pleasing aroma to the Lord".
- **Leviticus 2:** The grain offering: flour, oil and incense, with no yeast or honey, seasoned with "the salt of the covenant".
- **Leviticus 3:** The peace offering, a fellowship meal. The fat and the blood belong to the Lord.
- **Leviticus 4:** The sin offering for unintentional sins, scaled to the person: the priest, the whole community, a leader, or an ordinary Israelite.
- **Leviticus 5:** Particular sins needing a sin offering, with cheaper options (birds or flour) for the poor. The guilt offering begins.
- **Leviticus 6:** The guilt offering requires repayment plus a fifth. Instructions for priests: the altar fire must never go out.
- **Leviticus 7:** More on the guilt and peace offerings and the priests' portions. Israel must never eat fat or blood.
- **Leviticus 8:** Moses ordains Aaron and his sons over seven days.
- **Leviticus 9:** Aaron offers his first sacrifices. Fire from the Lord consumes them, and the people shout and fall on their faces.
- **Leviticus 10:** Nadab and Abihu offer "unauthorized fire" and are consumed. Priests must distinguish holy from common and clean from unclean (10:10).
- **Leviticus 11:** Clean and unclean animals, ending with "be holy, for I am holy" (11:44–45).
- **Leviticus 12:** Purification after childbirth (the offering Mary brought in Luke 2:22–24).
- **Leviticus 13:** The priests diagnose skin diseases, often translated "leprosy", and mildew in clothing.
- **Leviticus 14:** The cleansing ritual for someone healed of skin disease, with two birds, and for houses with mould.
- **Leviticus 15:** Uncleanness from bodily discharges, and how it is cleansed.
- **Leviticus 16:** The Day of Atonement. The high priest enters the Most Holy Place with blood. One goat is sacrificed, and the other, the scapegoat, carries the people's sins into the wilderness.
- **Leviticus 17:** Sacrifices only at the tabernacle. No eating blood, because "the life of the flesh is in the blood" and blood makes atonement (17:11).
- **Leviticus 18:** Sexual holiness. Israel must not live like Egypt or Canaan.
- **Leviticus 19:** Holiness in daily life: honest dealing, gleanings for the poor, care for the foreigner, and "love your neighbour as yourself" (19:18).
- **Leviticus 20:** Penalties for child sacrifice to Molech, for occult practices and for sexual sins. Israel is set apart to God.
- **Leviticus 21:** Holiness required of the priests and the high priest.
- **Leviticus 22:** Handling holy food, and offerings that must be without blemish.
- **Leviticus 23:** The holy calendar: Sabbath, Passover and Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, Weeks, Trumpets, the Day of Atonement, and Booths.
- **Leviticus 24:** Oil for the lampstand and the bread of the Presence. A man who blasphemes God's name is stoned.
- **Leviticus 25:** The Sabbath year and the Year of Jubilee: land returns to its families and slaves go free, because "the land is mine" (25:23).
- **Leviticus 26:** Blessings for obedience, curses and exile for disobedience, and God's promise to remember his covenant when Israel repents.
- **Leviticus 27:** Vows, dedicated people and property and how to redeem them, and tithes.

## Key people

- **Moses:** receives the laws from God at the tent of meeting and passes them on.
- **Aaron:** the first high priest, ordained in chapter 8.
- **Nadab and Abihu:** Aaron's oldest sons, struck down for offering unauthorized fire (10:1–2).
- **Eleazar and Ithamar:** Aaron's surviving sons, who continue the priesthood.
- **The blasphemer:** the son of an Israelite mother and an Egyptian father, punished for cursing God's name (24:10–23).

## Key events

- The five offerings instituted (1–7)
- Aaron and his sons ordained (8)
- Fire from the Lord consumes Aaron's first offering (9:24)
- Nadab and Abihu die for offering unauthorized fire (10)
- The laws of clean and unclean (11–15)
- The Day of Atonement and the scapegoat (16)
- "Love your neighbour as yourself" (19:18)
- The holy calendar of feasts (23)
- The Year of Jubilee (25)
- Blessings and curses of the covenant (26)

## Themes

- **God's presence:** God now lives among his people in the tabernacle (26:11–12), but sin restricts access: only the high priest enters the Most Holy Place, once a year (16).
- **God's holiness:** the book's thesis is 11:45 and 19:2, "You shall be holy, for I the Lord your God am holy". McKelvey notes the Hebrew root for "holy" appears about 143 times, more than in any other Old Testament book.
- **Atonement by blood:** sin defiles, and blood shed on the altar cleanses and reconciles (17:11; Heb 9:22).
- **Clean and unclean:** daily life is marked off into holy and common, clean and unclean (10:10). God is the source of whole, perfect life, so blemishes, disease and anything linked with death picture what can't come near him (RSB).
- **Mediation:** priests stand between a holy God and sinful people. Luther observed that the priesthood exists only because of sin (Lutheran Study Bible).
- **Holy time:** even Israel's week and year belong to the Lord: Sabbaths, feasts, the Day of Atonement, and the Jubilee (23; 25).
- **Holiness reaches everyday life:** the same law that regulates sacrifice also commands honesty, justice, care for the poor and the foreigner, and love for neighbours (19:18).
- **What still applies:** Christians don't keep the sacrifices, food laws or purity rites, which were tied to the tabernacle and fulfilled in Christ (Mark 7:19; Heb 10). But God's moral standard is unchanged: his people must still be holy as he is holy (1 Pet 1:15–16).

## How it points to Christ

Every offering foreshadows Christ's one sacrifice. He is the Lamb of God (John 1:29), offered once for all (Heb 10:10–14). On the Day of Atonement the high priest entered an earthly holy place with animal blood each year. Christ, the greater High Priest, entered heaven itself with his own blood, once (Heb 9:11–14, 24–28). The scapegoat carrying sins away pictures the one on whom the Lord laid "the iniquity of us all" (Isa 53:6), and Jesus suffered "outside the gate" like the sin offering burned outside the camp (Heb 13:11–12).

Jesus, untouched by uncleanness, touched lepers and made them clean (Mark 1:40–42), and declared all foods clean (Mark 7:19). He began his ministry by announcing "the year of the Lord's favour", Jubilee language (Luke 4:18–19). And he named "love your neighbour as yourself" (19:18) as the second great commandment (Matt 22:39).

## Common mix-ups

- Leviticus is named for the Levites, but it is mostly about the priests (Aaron's family). All priests were Levites; not all Levites were priests.
- The Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur, Lev 16) is not Passover (Ex 12).
- The scapegoat isn't killed: it is sent into the wilderness. The other goat is sacrificed.
- "Love your neighbour as yourself" comes from Leviticus 19:18, not first from the Gospels.
- Nadab and Abihu (Lev 10) are different from Korah's rebellion, which is in Numbers 16.
- "Be holy, for I am holy" (1 Pet 1:16) is Peter quoting Leviticus (11:44–45; 19:2).

## Going deeper

- **Do the laws of Leviticus still bind Christians?** The Westminster Confession distinguishes ceremonial laws, fulfilled and ended in Christ; civil laws, expired with Israel's state except for their "general equity"; and the moral law, which still binds (19.3–5). Work through chapters 11, 19 and 25 with that lens.
- **What were the five offerings for?** Burnt, grain, peace, sin and guilt offerings each address a different side of approaching God: consecration, thanksgiving, fellowship, cleansing and restitution. Read: McKelvey, Sacrifice; Hebrews 9–10.
- **Why did Nadab and Abihu die?** The text doesn't say exactly what "unauthorized fire" was. The Lutheran Study Bible lists possible explanations. What does 10:3 teach about approaching God on his terms?
- **What is "Azazel"?** Some modern scholars claim the scapegoat was originally an offering to goat-demons. The Lutheran Study Bible sees no basis for that. Discuss what the scapegoat ritual pictures.
- **Clean and unclean.** Why would ordinary things like skin disease, childbirth or certain foods make someone unclean? McKelvey connects uncleanness with death and disorder, which can't come near the God of life. How does Jesus' touch reverse the flow?
- **Jubilee and the gospel.** Debts cancelled, land restored, slaves freed (25). How does this frame Jesus' sermon in Luke 4?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** What is the key verse of Leviticus?

   **A:** "You shall be holy, for I the Lord your God am holy" (19:2).

2. **Q:** What are the five main offerings?

   **A:** Burnt, grain, peace, sin and guilt offerings (1–5).

3. **Q:** Who were struck down for offering unauthorized fire?

   **A:** Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's sons (10:1–2).

4. **Q:** What happened to the two goats on the Day of Atonement?

   **A:** One was sacrificed; the other, the scapegoat, was sent into the wilderness carrying the people's sins (16:7–10, 20–22).

5. **Q:** Why were Israelites forbidden to eat blood?

   **A:** Because the life is in the blood, and God gave it for atonement on the altar (17:11).

6. **Q:** Which famous command to love others comes from Leviticus?

   **A:** "Love your neighbour as yourself" (19:18).

7. **Q:** What happened in the Year of Jubilee?

   **A:** Land returned to its original families and Israelite slaves were set free (25).

8. **Q:** Why is the book called Leviticus?

   **A:** The name means "about the Levites": it is a manual for worship led by the priests from Levi's tribe, though addressed to all Israel (1:2).

9. **Q:** How does Hebrews describe Christ's work in Leviticus' terms?

   **A:** As the High Priest who entered the true holy place once for all with his own blood (Heb 9:11–14).
