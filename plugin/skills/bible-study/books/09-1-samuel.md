# 1 Samuel

## Summary

1 Samuel tells how Israel moved from judges to kings. God answers barren Hannah's prayer with Samuel, the last judge and a great prophet. When Israel demands a king "like all the nations", God gives them Saul, who looks the part but disobeys and is rejected. God then chooses David, a shepherd boy after his own heart, and the book ends with Saul's death on Mount Gilboa while David waits to take the throne.

## Setting

- **When:** about 1100 BC (roughly Samuel's birth) to about 1010 BC, when David began to reign at Hebron. The length of Saul's reign is uncertain, because the Hebrew of 13:1 is damaged (Reformation Study Bible); the Lutheran Study Bible gives about 1048–1009 BC.
- **Where:** Shiloh, Ramah, Mizpah, Gibeah (Saul's home), Bethlehem (David's), the wilderness of Judah, and the borderlands with the Philistines.
- **Who wrote it:** unknown. It is named for Samuel, who anointed both Saul and David, but his death is recorded in 25:1. Samuel may have written some early material, with others continuing it (compare 1 Chr 29:29). Phrases like "to this day", and the note that Ziklag belongs "to the kings of Judah" (27:6), show the final form came after the kingdom divided; the RSB suggests a court historian in the ninth or eighth century BC.
- **Where it sits:** 1 and 2 Samuel were one book in Hebrew. The Greek translation split them and treated Samuel and Kings together as "the Kingdoms". They carry on from Judges and Ruth, which ends with David's family line.

## Structure

The Reformation Study Bible's outline follows the move from judges to kings:

- 1–7: Before the monarchy: God rules and rescues his people (Samuel's birth, Eli's fall, the ark captured and returned, Samuel as judge)
- 8–12: The people demand a king; Saul is chosen and confirmed
- 13–15: Israel's first king: Saul's disobedience and rejection
- 16–31: God's chosen king: David's rise and Saul's fall (David anointed, Goliath, Saul's pursuit, Saul's death at Gilboa)

Across 1 and 2 Samuel, Saul and David run in parallel: both begin with victories and both sin gravely. The difference is how each responds to his sin (RSB). McKelvey divides the book by its three figures: Samuel, Saul and David.

## Chapter by chapter

- **1 Samuel 1:** Barren Hannah pours out her heart at Shiloh and vows her son to the Lord. Eli thinks she is drunk. Samuel is born and given to the Lord's service.
- **1 Samuel 2:** Hannah's song: the Lord lifts the lowly and will exalt his anointed (2:10). Eli's sons Hophni and Phinehas abuse the offerings, and a man of God foretells the fall of Eli's house.
- **1 Samuel 3:** The Lord calls the boy Samuel in the night: "Speak, for your servant hears" (3:10). Samuel becomes known as a prophet.
- **1 Samuel 4:** Israel takes the ark into battle as a lucky charm and is defeated. The ark is captured, Hophni and Phinehas die, and Eli falls dead. His grandson is named Ichabod, "the glory has departed".
- **1 Samuel 5:** In Dagon's temple, the idol falls before the ark, and the Philistine cities are struck with tumours.
- **1 Samuel 6:** The Philistines send the ark back on a cart with a guilt offering. Men of Beth-shemesh are struck for looking into it.
- **1 Samuel 7:** Samuel calls Israel to put away its idols. God thunders against the Philistines at Mizpah, and Samuel sets up the stone Ebenezer: "Till now the Lord has helped us".
- **1 Samuel 8:** Samuel's sons are corrupt, and the people demand a king like the nations. God tells Samuel, "they have rejected me", and warns what kings will take.
- **1 Samuel 9:** Saul, tall and handsome, goes looking for lost donkeys and meets Samuel, who has been told to anoint him.
- **1 Samuel 10:** Samuel anoints Saul privately, the Spirit comes on him, and he is chosen by lot at Mizpah, hiding among the baggage.
- **1 Samuel 11:** Saul rescues Jabesh-gilead from the Ammonites, and his kingship is confirmed at Gilgal.
- **1 Samuel 12:** Samuel's farewell: he is innocent, the people sinned in asking for a king, but if king and people fear the Lord, all will be well.
- **1 Samuel 13:** Saul offers sacrifice instead of waiting for Samuel. His dynasty won't last; the Lord has sought "a man after his own heart" (13:14).
- **1 Samuel 14:** Jonathan's daring raid at Michmash brings victory. Saul's rash oath nearly costs Jonathan his life.
- **1 Samuel 15:** Saul spares King Agag and the best of the Amalekite livestock. "To obey is better than sacrifice" (15:22). The Lord rejects Saul as king.
- **1 Samuel 16:** Samuel anoints David, Jesse's youngest son: "the Lord looks on the heart" (16:7). The Spirit leaves Saul, and David plays the lyre to soothe him.
- **1 Samuel 17:** David kills Goliath with a sling and a stone, trusting the Lord who saves "not with sword and spear" (17:47).
- **1 Samuel 18:** Jonathan makes a covenant with David. The women's song makes Saul jealous, and David marries Saul's daughter Michal.
- **1 Samuel 19:** Saul tries to kill David. Michal helps him escape, and Saul is overcome by the Spirit at Ramah.
- **1 Samuel 20:** Jonathan warns David with the signal of the arrows, and they part in tears and loyalty.
- **1 Samuel 21:** At Nob, the priest gives David holy bread and Goliath's sword. At Gath David pretends to be mad.
- **1 Samuel 22:** David gathers men at the cave of Adullam. Saul massacres the priests of Nob; only Abiathar escapes.
- **1 Samuel 23:** David saves Keilah from the Philistines. Saul hunts him in the wilderness, and Jonathan strengthens David in God.
- **1 Samuel 24:** In a cave at En-gedi, David cuts Saul's robe but refuses to harm the Lord's anointed.
- **1 Samuel 25:** Samuel dies. Foolish Nabal insults David, wise Abigail stops David's revenge, and after Nabal dies she marries David.
- **1 Samuel 26:** David spares Saul a second time, taking his spear and water jug.
- **1 Samuel 27:** David takes refuge among the Philistines at Ziklag, under King Achish of Gath.
- **1 Samuel 28:** Saul, desperate and unanswered by God, consults a medium at En-dor. Samuel appears and foretells his death.
- **1 Samuel 29:** The Philistine commanders refuse to let David fight with them against Israel.
- **1 Samuel 30:** Amalekites raid Ziklag. David "strengthened himself in the Lord", pursues them, and recovers everything.
- **1 Samuel 31:** Saul and Jonathan die in battle on Mount Gilboa. The men of Jabesh-gilead retrieve their bodies.

## Key people

- **Hannah:** Samuel's mother, a woman of prayer whose song praises God for raising up the humble.
- **Eli:** priest at Shiloh, who fails to restrain his wicked sons.
- **Hophni and Phinehas:** Eli's corrupt sons, who die when the ark is captured.
- **Samuel:** the last judge, a prophet and priestly figure, who anoints both Saul and David.
- **Saul:** Israel's first king, from Benjamin. Impressive to look at, but impatient and disobedient; rejected by God.
- **Jonathan:** Saul's son, a brave warrior and David's loyal friend.
- **David:** Jesse's youngest son, a shepherd anointed as king, who kills Goliath and patiently refuses to seize the throne.
- **Goliath:** the Philistine giant from Gath.
- **Michal:** Saul's daughter and David's wife.
- **Abigail:** Nabal's wise wife, who keeps David from bloodguilt and later marries him.
- **Abner:** commander of Saul's army.
- **The medium of En-dor:** the woman Saul consults to call up Samuel.

## Themes

- **God's kingship and human kings:** Israel's demand for a king rejects God as their King (8:7). Yet God means to give a king who rules under him (Deut 17:14–20). McKelvey makes kingship, God's and man's, the book's major theme.
- **The Lord looks on the heart:** Saul has the appearance of a king, David a heart for God (16:7). McKelvey traces "heart" through Hannah, Eli, Saul and David.
- **Obedience over ritual:** "To obey is better than sacrifice" (15:22). Saul's religion is outward and his obedience partial.
- **God raises the lowly and humbles the proud:** Hannah's song (2:1–10) previews the whole book: barren Hannah, the boy Samuel, the shepherd David are raised; Eli's house, the Philistines' Dagon and proud Saul are brought low.
- **God rules behind the scenes:** he steers even Saul's search for lost donkeys to bring him to Samuel (9:15–16), and chooses David before anyone else sees it (16:1–13).
- **God's presence cannot be manipulated:** treating the ark as a charm brings defeat; the ark humiliates Dagon on its own.
- **Waiting on God's timing:** David refuses twice to kill "the Lord's anointed" (24:6; 26:9), trusting God to give the throne.
- **Vows and oaths:** the Lutheran Study Bible traces vows through the book: Hannah's, Saul's rash oath, and Jonathan and David's covenant.

## How it points to Christ

Hannah's song is the first place Scripture calls the coming king the Lord's "anointed" (2:10), in Hebrew *mashiach*, Messiah. Mary's song in Luke 1:46–55 echoes it closely. David, born in Bethlehem, a shepherd chosen by God though overlooked by men, anointed with the Spirit, and fighting as Israel's champion against Goliath, foreshadows the Son of David. Jesus, born in Bethlehem, is the Good Shepherd (John 10:11), anointed with the Spirit at his baptism, who defeats our great enemy on our behalf.

Jesus himself cites David eating the holy bread at Nob (21; Mark 2:25–26). Like David, Jesus was hunted and rejected before he took his throne.

## Common mix-ups

- 1 and 2 Samuel were originally one book. Samuel dies in 1 Samuel 25, long before the "book of Samuel" ends.
- Saul is from Benjamin, and his home is Gibeah, the town disgraced at the end of Judges. David is from Judah, from Bethlehem.
- Eli's sons Hophni and Phinehas are different from Aaron's grandson Phinehas in Numbers.
- David is anointed three times: privately by Samuel (1 Sam 16), as king over Judah (2 Sam 2), and over all Israel (2 Sam 5).
- Saul is rejected twice: his dynasty in 13, and Saul himself in 15.
- Jonathan is Saul's son, not his brother.
- David spares Saul twice: in a cave at En-gedi (24) and in Saul's camp (26).

## Going deeper

- **Was asking for a king a sin?** Deuteronomy 17 expects a king, yet God calls Israel's request a rejection of him (8:7). The problem is wanting a king "like all the nations" instead of one under God's law. Read: McKelvey, Kingship.
- **Why was Saul rejected for what seem small failures?** Compare 13:8–14 and 15:1–23. What does partial obedience reveal about the heart?
- **Did Samuel really appear at En-dor?** The text says Samuel spoke (28:12–19). Interpreters debate whether God permitted it or it was a deception. The point stands: Saul turned to what God forbade (Deut 18:10–12).
- **David's patience.** David had Saul at his mercy twice. Why wouldn't he strike "the Lord's anointed"? What does this teach about waiting for God's promises?
- **The Lord looks on the heart.** Trace the word "heart" through Hannah, Eli, Saul and David, as McKelvey does. Where is your own trust: in appearance or in God?

## Q&A

1. **Q:** Who was Samuel's mother, and what did she vow?

   **A:** Hannah; to give her son to the Lord all his life (1:11).

2. **Q:** What did Samuel say when God called him?

   **A:** "Speak, for your servant hears" (3:10).

3. **Q:** What does Ichabod mean?

   **A:** "The glory has departed", because the ark was captured (4:21).

4. **Q:** Why did Israel ask for a king?

   **A:** To be like all the nations and have someone to fight their battles (8:5, 20).

5. **Q:** Who was Israel's first king?

   **A:** Saul, from the tribe of Benjamin (9:1–2; 10:1).

6. **Q:** What did Samuel tell Saul after he spared Agag?

   **A:** "To obey is better than sacrifice" (15:22).

7. **Q:** Why was David chosen over his older brothers?

   **A:** "The Lord looks on the heart" (16:7).

8. **Q:** How did David defeat Goliath?

   **A:** With a sling and a stone, in the name of the Lord (17:45–50).

9. **Q:** How did Jonathan warn David?

   **A:** With a signal of arrows shot in the field (20:35–42).

10. **Q:** How did Saul die?

    **A:** Wounded in battle against the Philistines on Mount Gilboa, he fell on his own sword (31:4).
