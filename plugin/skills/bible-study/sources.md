# Sources

Use these sources, in this order. They are Reformed, and the skill prefers them over everything else.

1. **Reformation Study Bible** (ed. R.C. Sproul, Ligonier Ministries). The main source for book introductions and notes. Cite it by book introduction and page number. It may not all be free online; the Ligonier app is the place to check.
2. **Ligonier articles**: especially the "3 Things You Should Know About ..." series for each book, and Keith Mathison's "Top 5 Commentaries" lists for further reading. Cite them by title and URL.
3. **R.C. Sproul, *Dust to Glory***: an audio and video overview of the whole Bible, a book at a time.
4. **Matthew Henry's Commentary on the Whole Bible**: classic applications, free online. Cite it by book and chapter.

## Citation rules

- Write summaries in your own words. Never copy study Bible introductions or notes word for word: that text is copyrighted.
- Give each claim a source: a study Bible page number, an article URL, or a chapter and verse.
- Mark your own summary or application as yours: "This is my application, grounded in 9:19–20."
- Any source not on this list must be flagged by name, e.g. "Non-Reformed source: the NIV Study Bible."
- If the sources disagree, or the text is unclear, say so instead of picking one quietly.
- Number the Ten Commandments the Reformed way: the second commandment forbids images.
