# Sources

Teach from these sources, in this order. They come from the Protestant Reformation and the Reformed tradition that grew out of it, and the skill prefers them over everything else. Many of the older works are free online, for example at the [Christian Classics Ethereal Library](https://www.ccel.org/).

## 1. Scripture

The text of the Bible comes first and has the final word. Quote or point to chapter and verse for every key event and theme, and let Scripture interpret Scripture: explain unclear passages by clearer ones.

## 2. Confessions and catechisms

These are summaries of what the Bible teaches, written and adopted by Reformed churches. Use them to check doctrine, not as substitutes for the text.

- **Westminster Standards**: the Westminster Confession of Faith (1646) and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms (1647).
- **Three Forms of Unity**: the Belgic Confession (1561), the Heidelberg Catechism (1563) and the Canons of Dort (1618–19).

## 3. Study Bibles

The main sources for book introductions, outlines and notes. Cite them by book introduction and section heading. Each book note gives the sections it drew on in its footnotes.

- **The Reformation Study Bible** (*RSB*; Reformation Trust, 2015). General editor R.C. Sproul, of Ligonier Ministries. Its editors and contributors are mainly Reformed and Presbyterian scholars, among them J.I. Packer, Bruce K. Waltke, T. Desmond Alexander and Gregory K. Beale. It uses the English Standard Version (ESV). Each book's introduction covers the title, author, date and occasion, genre, literary features, main themes and theology, the book's place in the larger story of the Bible, Christ in the book, the history of interpretation, and an outline. Alongside the verse notes it has short theological articles.
- **The Reformation Heritage KJV Study Bible** (*RH*; Reformation Heritage Books, 2014). General editor Joel R. Beeke, with Michael P. V. Barrett (Old Testament), Gerald M. Bilkes (New Testament) and Paul M. Smalley. It uses the King James Version (KJV) and writes in the Reformed and Puritan tradition. Each book's introduction covers authorship, date, theme, purpose, a synopsis of the book's place in the history of redemption, and usually an outline. Each chapter's notes open with a one-line summary and end with "Thoughts for Personal/Family Worship". Its articles cover Christian living, church history, and the historic creeds and confessions.
- **The Lutheran Study Bible** (*LSB*; Concordia Publishing House, 2009). General editor Edward A. Engelbrecht, with contributors from many countries. Concordia is the publishing house of the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod. It uses the ESV. It has book introductions and outlines, verse notes, and a "Luther on …" feature for each book drawn from Luther's own writings. Its teaching follows Lutheran theology, especially on the sacraments and on law and gospel.

## Biblical-theological introductions

Book-by-book introductions by Reformed scholars, most of them on the faculty of Reformed Theological Seminary. Each chapter has its own author and covers a book's background (authorship, date and setting), its structure and outline, its message and theology, and how it leads to Christ and the New Testament. Cite the chapter author, e.g. *John J. Yeo, "Ruth," in Van Pelt, ed.*

- **Miles V. Van Pelt, ed., *A Biblical-Theological Introduction to the Old Testament: The Gospel Promised*** (*BTO*; Crossway, 2016). Chapters by John D. Currid, Richard P. Belcher Jr., Mark D. Futato, Peter Y. Lee, Daniel C. Timmer, Willem A. VanGemeren and others.
- **Michael J. Kruger, ed., *A Biblical-Theological Introduction to the New Testament: The Gospel Realized*** (*BTN*; Crossway, 2016). Chapters by Kruger, Reggie M. Kidd, Benjamin Gladd, Robert J. Cara, Guy Prentiss Waters, Simon J. Kistemaker, Charles E. Hill and others.

## 4. Commentaries and teachers

Cite each by work, book and chapter, or by title and URL for articles.

- **John Calvin**: his *Commentaries* cover most books of the Bible (not Revelation), and the *Institutes of the Christian Religion* set out the doctrine behind them.
- **Martin Luther**: the Reformer who started it all, and a trusted guide on justification by faith, grace and Scripture's authority. Useful works include his *Lectures on Genesis*, *Commentary on Galatians* (1535), *Preface to Romans* (1522) and the *Small* and *Large Catechisms*. The commentary on Galatians, the preface to Romans and both catechisms are free at [Project Wittenberg](https://www.projectwittenberg.org/pub/resources/text/wittenberg/wittenberg-luther.html). On the Lord's Supper, baptism and some points of law and gospel he holds Lutheran views.
- **Matthew Henry**: *Commentary on the Whole Bible*, rich in practical application. Henry finished it through Acts; other ministers completed Romans to Revelation from his notes.
- **Joel R. Beeke**: Reformed and Puritan scholar at Puritan Reformed Theological Seminary. See *Reformed Systematic Theology* (with Paul M. Smalley), *A Puritan Theology* (with Mark Jones) and *Meet the Puritans*, as well as the study Bible above.
- **The Puritans**: for example Thomas Watson's *A Body of Divinity* and John Owen's commentary on Hebrews.
- **Charles Spurgeon**: sermons, and *The Treasury of David* on the Psalms. A Reformed Baptist, he held to believers' baptism.
- **R.C. Sproul and Ligonier Ministries**: Sproul's *Dust to Glory* overview of the whole Bible, the Ligonier "3 Things You Should Know About ..." articles for each book, and Keith Mathison's "Top 5 Commentaries" lists for further reading.

## Abbreviations in the book notes

The book notes cite their five main works by abbreviation, each linked to a markdown footnote that gives the full reference and the sections used:

- **BTO**: Miles V. Van Pelt, ed., *A Biblical-Theological Introduction to the Old Testament: The Gospel Promised* (Crossway, 2016). The notes usually name the chapter's author, e.g. `Timmer[^bto]`.
- **BTN**: Michael J. Kruger, ed., *A Biblical-Theological Introduction to the New Testament: The Gospel Realized* (Crossway, 2016), cited the same way.
- **LSB**: *The Lutheran Study Bible* (Concordia, 2009).
- **RSB**: *The Reformation Study Bible* (Reformation Trust, 2015).
- **RH**: *The Reformation Heritage KJV Study Bible* (Reformation Heritage Books, 2014).

## Citation rules

- Write summaries in your own words. Never copy study Bible introductions or notes word for word: that text is copyrighted.
- Give each claim a source: chapter and verse, a confession or catechism article, a study Bible introduction and section, a commentary reference, or an article URL.
- Mark your own summary or application as yours: "This is my application, grounded in 9:19–20."
- Any source not on this list must be flagged by name, e.g. "Non-Reformed source: the NIV Study Bible."
- If the sources disagree, or the text is unclear, say so instead of picking one quietly.
- Number the Ten Commandments the Reformed way: the second commandment forbids images. (Luther's catechisms number them differently.)
