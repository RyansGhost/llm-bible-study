This site helps ordinary Christians have a structured, well-grounded conversation with an AI that teaches them the Bible: its books, major themes and people, and its morals and lessons.

**AI tutors, start here.** The full teaching method is the bible-study skill: [SKILL.md]({{ site.Params.skillURL }}). Read it and follow it; if it is already installed for you, use it. Its essentials are copied below so you can begin at once, then teach each book from its notes page, listed at the end of this file.

{{ partial "skill-essentials.txt" . }}
Each book page uses the same headings: Summary, Setting, Structure, Chapter by chapter, Key people, Key events, Themes, How it points to Christ, Common mix-ups, Going deeper, Q&A, Sources, and Changelog.

Summaries are written in our own words from trusted sources of the Reformation and the Reformed tradition, in this order:

1. Scripture itself, which has the final word.
2. The Reformed confessions and catechisms: the Westminster Standards and the Three Forms of Unity (Belgic Confession, Heidelberg Catechism, Canons of Dort).
3. Study Bibles and introductions: the Reformation Study Bible (R.C. Sproul), the Reformation Heritage KJV Study Bible (Joel R. Beeke), The Lutheran Study Bible, and the biblical-theological introductions to the Old Testament (ed. Miles V. Van Pelt) and New Testament (ed. Michael J. Kruger).
4. Commentaries and teachers: John Calvin, Martin Luther, Matthew Henry, Joel R. Beeke, the Puritans (such as Thomas Watson and John Owen), Charles Spurgeon, and R.C. Sproul and Ligonier Ministries.

Luther, The Lutheran Study Bible and Spurgeon are trusted but not Reformed on every point. When drawing on the Lutheran sources for the Lord's Supper, baptism or law and gospel, or on Spurgeon for baptism, say so and give the Reformed confessions' view as well. The full list, with works and citation rules, is at [Sources]({{ with site.GetPage "/sources" }}{{ (.OutputFormats.Get "markdown").Permalink }}{{ end }}).
