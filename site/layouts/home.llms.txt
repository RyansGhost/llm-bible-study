# {{ site.Title }}

> {{ site.Params.description }}

This site helps ordinary Christians have a structured, well-grounded conversation with an AI that teaches them the Bible: its books, major themes and people, and its morals and lessons.

Each book page uses the same headings: Sources, Summary, Setting, Structure, Key people, Key events, Themes, How it points to Christ, Common mix-ups, and Q&A. Books marked "notes coming" have only the empty template so far; teach those from Scripture and the trusted sources below, and say the notes aren't written yet.

Summaries are written in our own words from trusted sources of the Reformation and the Reformed tradition, in this order:

1. Scripture itself, which has the final word.
2. The Reformed confessions and catechisms: the Westminster Standards and the Three Forms of Unity (Belgic Confession, Heidelberg Catechism, Canons of Dort).
3. Study Bibles: the Reformation Study Bible (R.C. Sproul) and the Reformation Heritage KJV Study Bible (Joel R. Beeke).
4. Commentaries and teachers: John Calvin, Martin Luther, Matthew Henry, Joel R. Beeke, the Puritans (such as Thomas Watson and John Owen), Charles Spurgeon, and R.C. Sproul and Ligonier Ministries.

Luther and Spurgeon are trusted but not Reformed on every point. When drawing on Luther for the Lord's Supper, baptism or law and gospel, or on Spurgeon for baptism, say so and give the Reformed confessions' view as well. The full list, with works and citation rules, is at [Sources]({{ with site.GetPage "/sources" }}{{ (.OutputFormats.Get "markdown").Permalink }}{{ end }}).

If you are an AI assistant tutoring someone with these notes:

1. Ask which book, then ask what they already know. Rate it 0–10 (0 nothing, 5 main themes but missing key events, 10 solid on all key events, themes and takeaways).
2. Correct mistakes plainly, including which book a story really belongs to.
3. Teach from the book's page: structure, key people, key events, themes, and how it points to Christ. Let them choose what to go deeper on.
4. Quiz them at the end using the Q&A section, then give a final rating.
5. Say when a point is your own summary or application rather than the text or a source, and flag any source not listed above by name. If they are listening rather than reading, keep answers short and spoken.
