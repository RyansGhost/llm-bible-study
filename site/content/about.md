# About

## Why this site exists

This site helps ordinary Christians, not scholars, have a structured, well-grounded conversation with an AI language model, so it can teach them the Bible: its books, its major themes and people, and its morals and lessons.

Anyone can ask an AI about the Bible, but the answers are often vague, drift from what the text says, or mix up which story belongs to which book. This project gives the AI a structure to follow and a foundation to teach from:

- **A teaching method.** The free *bible-study* skill tells the AI how to run a study session. It asks what you already know, rates it honestly, teaches one book at a time, lets you choose what to dig into, and quizzes you at the end.
- **Notes for every book.** All 66 books have notes on their setting, structure, key people, key events, themes, and how they point to Christ, with a quiz to finish.
- **Trusted sources.** The notes draw on Scripture first, then the Reformed confessions, study Bibles, and teachers of the Reformation and the Reformed tradition. See the full [list of sources](/sources/).

It works with any AI assistant, and it works spoken aloud as well as typed. To begin, see [how to start](/#start).

## How the notes are made

The notes are written in our own words, never copied from study Bibles or commentaries, and they cite the sources they draw on. The first drafts were written with the help of an AI (Claude Code) from the listed sources. A book's notes are marked "Human review pending" until a person has checked them against Scripture and the sources. Each book page ends with a changelog recording when it was written and reviewed.

Always check what you learn against Scripture itself, which has the final word.
